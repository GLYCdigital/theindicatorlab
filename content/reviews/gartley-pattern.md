**Gartley_Pattern Review: Settings, Strategy & How to Use It**

The Gartley is arguably the most recognizable harmonic pattern—but also the easiest to misidentify. This indicator automates the detection, removing the manual Fibonacci measurement work. Here's what it offers and where it falls short.

**What This Indicator Actually Does**

It scans price action for the classic Gartley pattern (XABCD structure) and plots the potential reversal zone (PRZ) with labeled points. The pattern is defined by specific Fibonacci relationships between the legs: an XA retracement, an AB retracement of XA, and a CD extension of AB. The indicator highlights the completion zone and draws the structure directly on your chart.

**Key Features That Set It Apart**

- **Pattern detection** – It marks completed and developing Gartleys on the chart.
- **Customizable Fibonacci thresholds** – You can tighten or loosen the tolerance for ratio hits, which matters for filtering signal quality.
- **Alert system** – Alerts can be set for pattern completion.
- **Multi-timeframe support** – Works across timeframes, though higher timeframes tend to produce cleaner structures.

**Settings and How to Tune Them**

- **Tolerance:** Controls how strictly the Fibonacci ratios must be hit. Loosening it surfaces more patterns; tightening it surfaces fewer, cleaner ones.
- **Minimum swing size:** Filters out small structures. The right value depends on your instrument's typical range.
- **Show PRZ:** Toggles the potential reversal zone box, which marks the entry area.
- **Extend lines:** Toggles line projection forward, which adds visual clutter.

**How to Use It for Entries and Exits**

- **Entry:** Wait for the pattern to complete (point D). Enter at the PRZ with a limit order rather than chasing price past the zone.
- **Stop loss:** Place it beyond point X—below for a bullish Gartley, above for bearish.
- **Take profit:** Scale out at Fibonacci levels of the CD leg or at the prior swing high/low.
- **Confirmation:** Don't trade the pattern alone. Wait for a reversal candlestick (pin bar, engulfing) at D. The indicator shows the zone; price action provides the trigger.

**Honest Pros and Cons**

Pros:
- Saves hours of manual Fibonacci work.
- More reliable on higher timeframes.
- Alerts flag completions so you don't have to watch the chart constantly.

Cons:
- **False signals in ranging markets.** The indicator will draw Gartleys in chop that never materialize. Trend context matters.
- **No volume filter.** A valid pattern without volume confirmation is weaker. Consider pairing it with an oscillator like RSI or MACD.
- **Point labeling can be confusing** when multiple patterns overlap—zoom in.

**Who It's Actually For**

It's for traders who already understand harmonic patterns and want automation. Beginners will be tempted to trade every pattern it draws. Pair it with trend analysis to filter out low-probability setups.

**Better Alternatives**

**FAQ**

*Q: Does this indicator repaint?*
A: Completed patterns stay fixed. Developing patterns may shift until point D is confirmed—standard for harmonic tools.

*Q: Can I use it on 5-minute charts?*
A: You can, but expect more false signals. Higher timeframes tend to be more reliable.

*Q: What's the difference between Gartley and Bat patterns?*
A: The Gartley has a 0.786 retracement at D, while the Bat uses 0.886. The indicator only detects Gartley, not other harmonics.

**Final Verdict**

This is a solid, no-nonsense Gartley detector. It does one thing well—automating pattern recognition—but doesn't hold your hand beyond that. If you're disciplined about trend context and price action confirmation, it's a useful tool. If you're looking for a magic "buy/sell" button, you'll be disappointed.
