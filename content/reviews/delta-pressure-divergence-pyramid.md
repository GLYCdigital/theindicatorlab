## What the name actually signals

Three words carry the whole concept. "Delta pressure" points to buy/sell pressure measured from order flow or volume delta — the imbalance between aggressive buyers and sellers. "Divergence" is the standard technical idea of price and a second series moving apart, often read as momentum fading. "Pyramid" suggests the tool layers these signals, perhaps stacking multiple divergences into a ranked or tiered view rather than flagging a single event.

That's a plausible reading of the name. It is not a confirmed feature list. The description does not document the inputs, the calculation method, or what the "pyramid" structure looks like on the chart. Treat the above as interpretation, not fact.

## What's documented

Almost nothing. The script is a study, it sits in the Trend category, and the description field contains no setup notes, no parameter list, no explanation of how the divergence or pressure is computed. There is no linked documentation in the source material either.

That thinness matters. For a trend tool built around divergence, the details that decide whether it's useful — how pressure is normalised, which price series it compares against, whether it repaints — are exactly the details the source doesn't give. You'll be evaluating those yourself on the chart.

## How you'd approach it

Given the category and concept, the sensible workflow is to add it to a chart, look at how it behaves across a clean trend and a choppy range, and judge whether its signals line up with what you'd already call a pressure shift or a divergence. Because it's a study, it plots on its own rather than overlaying price, so you'll be reading it alongside your normal price structure rather than on top of it.

As shown in the chart above, the layout is analytical rather than decorative — the job is to make pressure and divergence easier to see, not to shout entries at you.
