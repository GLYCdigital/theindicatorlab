Fair value gaps have become one of the most talked-about concepts in price action trading, and "Almazov Trade: FVG Relay" is a TradingView indicator filed under the Trend category that builds its logic around them. The name tells you most of what you need to know: it is an FVG tool, and "Relay" suggests it is designed to pass information along — tracking gaps as price moves rather than just stamping them on the chart once and forgetting them.

That is the honest scope of what I can tell you here. There is no official documentation available for this one, so this review sticks to what the concept itself implies and avoids the usual trap of inventing settings, win rates or "best timeframes" that nobody can verify.

## What it is, conceptually

A fair value gap is a three-candle imbalance — a stretch of price where one side of the market moved so fast that the other side never got filled. Traders who use FVGs treat those zones as areas where price may return to "rebalance" before continuing. An indicator in this space typically does two jobs: it detects those imbalances and it draws them on the chart as zones.

The "Relay" in the title points to the part that matters more than detection. Anyone can spot a gap by eye on a clean chart. The harder problem is what happens after — does the zone hold, get tapped and rejected, get filled and invalidated, or get left behind entirely? If the tool genuinely tracks that lifecycle, it is doing something more useful than a static drawing.

## Where the value sits

The reason FVG tools have a following is that they enforce a bit of discipline. Instead of entering on a candle that "looks strong," you are waiting for price to interact with a defined area. That gives you a reference point for entries, for stop placement on the far side of the zone, and for judging whether a move has momentum behind it or is just drifting.

A relay-style approach fits that workflow. If the indicator is managing zones over time — updating, marking them as touched, or retiring them once price has worked through them — it saves you from babysitting a chart full of stale boxes. That is the practical difference between a tool you glance at and a tool you actually trade from.

As shown in the chart above, the visual layer is doing the heavy lifting: zones marked clearly enough that you can read structure at a glance without a legend.

## How you would actually use it

The sensible workflow for any FVG indicator is the same: identify the imbalance, wait for price to return, and let the reaction at the zone decide whether you engage. The indicator's job is to make that first step fast and the second step unambiguous.

Where a trend label comes in is context. Gaps that form with the prevailing direction tend to be treated differently from gaps that form against it — one is a continuation setup, the other is a counter-trend gamble. A tool categorised as Trend is presumably nudging you toward that distinction, though without documentation I cannot tell you exactly how it handles it.

## Who it suits

Discretionary price action traders who already think in terms of imbalances and want the zones handled for them. It is a poor fit for anyone wanting a mechanical signal with defined rules and a documented track record — this is a structure tool, not a strategy engine. Scalpers and intraday traders will likely get more from it than swing traders, simply because FVGs matter most when price moves fast.
