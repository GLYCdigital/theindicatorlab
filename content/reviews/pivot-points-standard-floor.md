I’ve tested dozens of pivot point indicators on TradingView, and the **Pivot_Points_Standard_Floor** is the closest thing to the raw, institutional calculation you’ll find on most broker terminals. No fluff, no predictive nonsense—just the math.

If you’ve ever traded Forex or futures, you already know the Floor method: it’s the standard used by banks and professional traders. This indicator simply plots those levels on your chart. But as the chart above shows, it does more than just draw lines—it creates a framework for your entire trading day.

## What This Indicator Actually Does

It calculates and displays seven horizontal levels based on the previous period’s high, low, and close:  
- **Pivot Point (PP)** – the mathematical center  
- **Three Resistance levels (R1, R2, R3)**  
- **Three Support levels (S1, S2, S3)**  

It updates automatically at the start of each new period (daily, weekly, monthly). That’s it. No repainting, no lagging averages, no curve-fitting. Just clean, actionable levels.

## Key Features That Stand Out

- **No repaint.** This is critical. Many pivot indicators recalculate mid-period, which destroys reliability. This one doesn’t.  
- **Multi-timeframe support.** You can set it to daily, weekly, or monthly pivots independently of your chart timeframe. I often use monthly pivots on a 1-hour chart for swing trading.  
- **Customizable line styles and colors.** You can make R3 bright red and S1 dotted green—whatever helps you read the chart faster.  
- **Lightweight.** No performance hit, even on 50+ charts.  

## Best Settings I’ve Tested

After about 200 trades using this indicator, here’s what works:

- **Default timeframe: Daily.** For intraday, daily pivots are the sweet spot. Weekly gives you wider ranges for position trading.  
- **Line thickness: 2.** Anything thinner gets lost on busy charts.  
- **Resistance colors: Red (R1–R3)** . Support colors: Green (S1–S3). The pivot line itself: Yellow or white.  
- **Extend lines to the right.** This keeps levels visible as price moves forward.  

Pro tip: Turn off R3 and S3 unless you’re trading high-volatility news events. They’re rarely touched and clutter the chart.

## How to Use It: Entry Logic That Works

This isn’t a magic signal generator. It’s a framework. Here’s the strategy I use most:

**The “Bounce or Break” method:**

1. **Bounce trade:** If price touches S1 or S2 with a bullish candlestick pattern (hammer, engulfing), I go long with a stop loss 5-10 pips below that level. Target is the pivot point or R1.  
2. **Break trade:** If price closes decisively above R1 (especially with volume), I enter long with a target of R2. Stop loss below R1.  
3. **Reversal at extremes:** When price reaches R3 or S3, I look for exhaustion patterns. These levels are often the day’s boundary.

Notice in the screenshot how price reacted exactly at S1 twice in one session. That’s not luck—it’s self-fulfilling prophecy because thousands of traders watch these levels.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**  
- Dead simple. No learning curve.  
- Works on any market (Forex, crypto, stocks, futures).  
- Reliable for intraday and swing trading.  
- Free and built into TradingView.

**Cons:**  
- Not predictive. It only tells you where price *might* react, not *if* it will.  
- Can get noisy on volatile days when price slices through multiple levels.  
- No volume or momentum confirmation built in—you need to combine it with something else.

## Who It’s For

- **Intraday scalpers** who need quick reference levels for entries and exits.  
- **Swing traders** using weekly pivots on daily charts.  
- **Beginners** who want a clean, no-nonsense support/resistance tool.  
- **Not for you** if you want a “buy/sell” signal. This is a tool, not a robot.

## Alternatives Worth Considering

- **Pivot Points Classic** – Very similar, but some versions include the Camarilla calculation.  
- **Fibonacci Pivots** – Same concept but with Fibonacci ratios. Better for trending markets.  
- **TV Pivot Points** – Includes multiple calculation methods (Woodie, Camarilla, etc.) in one indicator.  

If you need momentum confirmation, pair this with **RSI** or **MACD**. I personally use the **MACD** as shown in the screenshot—when price bounces off S1 and MACD crosses above zero, that’s a high-probability long.

## FAQ

**Does this indicator repaint?**  
No. The levels are fixed for the entire period. Once a daily pivot is set, it stays until the next day.

**Can I use it on crypto?**  
Absolutely. Works on any market with high, low, and close data. I’ve tested it on Bitcoin and Ethereum—same reliability.

**What timeframe should I set it to?**  
Daily for intraday, weekly for swing trading. Monthly only if you’re position trading.

## Final Verdict

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)**

The Pivot_Points_Standard_Floor is a solid, no-BS tool that every trader should have on their chart. It won’t make you money by itself, but it gives you a reliable framework to plan trades. I dock one star because it lacks any confirmation logic—you have to bring your own strategy. That said, for a free, institutional-grade pivot indicator, this is as good as it gets.

**Should you install it?**  
Yes, especially if you trade intraday or swing. Set it, learn the levels, and watch how price respects them. It’s not magic—it’s math. And in trading, math beats hope every time.
