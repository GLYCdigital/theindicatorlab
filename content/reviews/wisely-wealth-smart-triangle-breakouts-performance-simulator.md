Some indicators try to do one thing well. This one tries to do two: flag triangle breakouts as trend signals, then let you eyeball how those signals would have played out via a built-in performance simulator. That combination is the whole pitch, and it's worth being clear about what that does and doesn't mean before you install it.

## What it actually is

At its core, this is a **trend indicator built around triangle consolidations**. A triangle pattern is a compression of price between converging boundaries — a setup where a market is coiling and eventually has to resolve one way or the other. The "breakout" is the moment price leaves that structure, and in trend-following logic that exit is treated as the signal: momentum picking a direction.

The second half of the name is the differentiator: a **performance simulator**. Rather than just painting arrows on the chart, the tool is designed to show how its own signals would have performed historically, so you can gauge the tool against the chart instead of taking its word for it. The chart above illustrates the concept — signals plotted against a MACD panel, giving a momentum context alongside the breakout markers.

## Key features

- **Triangle breakout detection.** The indicator looks for the converging-boundary structure and marks the resolution — the point where price leaves the pattern.
- **Trend framing.** Signals are presented as directional trend cues rather than standalone reversal calls. That's a meaningful distinction: breakouts are continuation-flavored, not "catch the bottom" trades.
- **Built-in simulator.** The performance simulator is the feature that separates this from a hundred other pattern scripts. It's designed to let you review signal outcomes on the chart itself.
- **Single-tool workflow.** Detection and evaluation live in one place, so you're not exporting signals to a spreadsheet to judge them.

## How to use it

The sensible workflow is to treat it as a **context filter, not an entry button**. Read the triangle on the chart, note the breakout marker, then check whether the broader trend agrees — the MACD panel is there for exactly that reason. A breakout that fires in the direction of momentum is a different beast from one that fights it.

The simulator is where you should spend your time first. Before trusting a single live signal, use it to see how the tool behaves on the instrument and timeframe you actually trade. Patterns are notoriously instrument-dependent — a triangle that resolves cleanly on one market can chop endlessly on another. The simulator lets you make that judgment yourself rather than accepting a marketing claim.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- The simulator is a genuinely useful idea. Most pattern indicators ask for blind trust; this one hands you the evidence and lets you decide.
- Triangle breakouts are a well-understood, widely traded structure, so the signal logic is grounded in something real rather than an opaque black box.
- Bundling detection and evaluation means faster iteration — you can tune your read of the tool without leaving the chart.

**Cons**
- Triangles are subjective. Where the boundaries sit depends on how the tool draws them, and breakout signals near the apex of a tightening pattern are famously prone to false starts. The indicator won't fix that; it just marks them.
- A performance simulator shows you the past. It is not a forecast, and it is not a promise. If you read it as a backtest guarantee, you'll be disappointed — that's on you, not the tool.
- Because I have no official documentation to reference here, I can't speak to its specific inputs, defaults, or how it defines a valid triangle. That's a gap you should close yourself before relying on it.

## Who it's for

Discretionary trend traders who already read chart patterns and want a second set of eyes on triangle resolutions. It suits people who like to **validate a tool before trusting it** — the simulator rewards that instinct. It's less suited to anyone wanting a fully mechanical system with fixed rules; the pattern-reading component keeps a human in the loop.

## FAQ

**Does the simulator guarantee future results?**
No. It shows historical signal behavior. Past performance of any indicator is not predictive, and you should treat it as a research aid, not a promise.

**Is this a standalone trading system?**
Treat it as a signal and evaluation tool. The breakout markers still need context — trend, momentum, market conditions — before they mean anything actionable.

**Can I use it on any market?**
Triangle patterns appear across markets, but how cleanly they resolve varies. Use the simulator on your own instrument before committing.

## Verdict

The pairing of breakout detection with an on-chart performance simulator is the right instinct — it puts the evidence in front of you instead of asking for faith. The triangle logic is familiar and sound, and the tool is honestly more useful for *evaluating* signals than for generating them. The subjectivity of pattern drawing and the absence of clear documentation keep it short of top marks. If you trade breakouts and want a tool that lets you check its work, this earns a look.
