The RSI is one of those tools almost everyone loads and almost nobody agrees on. Overbought, oversold, divergence — the classics. "Funk RSI: Break the Rules" signals its intent in the name: it belongs to the school of thought that the RSI is more useful as a momentum and trend gauge than as a simple mean-reversion trigger. That's the concept, and it's worth understanding before you add it to a chart.

**Heads up on what this review can and can't tell you.** The publisher's documentation wasn't available to me, so I'm working from the indicator's stated concept rather than a settings sheet. I haven't run it, backtested it, or measured it. Where a review normally quotes defaults and thresholds, this one deliberately doesn't — because making those up would be worse than leaving them out. Treat the specifics as "go read the inputs panel," and the rest as a fair assessment of the idea.

## What it actually is

Strip away the branding and you have an RSI-derived momentum tool built for trend context. The "break the rules" framing is the interesting part. Conventional RSI teaching treats extreme readings as reversal warnings. This family of indicators flips that: strong readings are read as momentum confirmation, and the trend is presumed to persist until the momentum itself rolls over. On a chart it's presented as a trend companion — the kind of thing you'd layer alongside your existing structure rather than use as a standalone signal generator.

It's filed under Trend for a reason. This isn't a scalping trigger or an entry-timing gadget; it's meant to help you answer "which way is momentum leaning, and is that lean holding?"

## How you'd use it

Without the manual, the honest workflow is the obvious one: load it beside your price action, get a feel for how it behaves on the instruments and timeframes you actually trade, and let it serve as a momentum filter rather than a command. If your approach is trend-following or pullback-buying, a momentum read out of the RSI family can help you decide whether a dip is a pause or a reversal. If you're a countertrend trader who lives on overbought/oversold signals, understand that this tool is philosophically pointed the other way — you'll likely find it fights your instincts.

As with any RSI-based method, the classic failure mode is chop: momentum reads get noisy in ranges and produce whipsaws. That's a property of the underlying math, not a knock on this particular build, but it's the thing to watch for.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Reframes the RSI around momentum and trend, which suits trend-following styles better than the textbook reversal reading.
- Sits comfortably on a chart as a context layer — you're not building your whole system around it.
- The concept is coherent and easy to grasp; no PhD required to understand what it's trying to tell you.

**Cons**
- The documentation gap is real. Without a clear published explanation of the inputs and logic, you're partly reverse-engineering the tool by observation — fine for experienced traders, less so for beginners who want "set and forget."
- RSI-derived momentum will always be vulnerable to range conditions; don't expect magic in sideways markets.
- "Break the rules" is a marketing hook more than a methodology; don't let the name convince you it's a new category of indicator.

## Who it's for

Trend traders and momentum-leaning swing traders who already understand the RSI and want a version tuned toward continuation rather than reversal. If you're disciplined about using it as a filter and comfortable reading an indicator's behaviour without a manual, it fits. If you need documented defaults and a step-by-step guide before you'll trust a tool, wait for clearer documentation or look elsewhere.

## FAQ

**Is this just a relabelled RSI?**
It's RSI-based, but the framing is momentum/trend-oriented rather than the standard overbought/oversold interpretation. The distinction is in how readings are meant to be read, not in the raw math.

**Can it be used as a standalone entry signal?**
Conceptually it's a context tool. Treating any momentum read as a standalone trigger is where traders usually get hurt — pair it with price structure.

**Which timeframes work best?**
I can't say, and anyone who tells you without qualifying it is guessing. Test it on what you trade.

## Verdict

The concept is sound and the positioning is honest about what it is: an RSI-derived momentum lens for trend traders. What holds it back from a higher score is the lack of accessible documentation — a tool that asks you to "break the rules" should explain its own rules clearly. For traders who know the RSI well and want a continuation-flavoured read, it's a reasonable addition to the toolkit.
