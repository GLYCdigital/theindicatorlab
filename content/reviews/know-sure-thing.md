## Know Sure Thing Review: Does This Momentum Indicator Actually Work?

The **Know Sure Thing (KST)** is one of those momentum oscillators that looks like a gimmick until you dig into the math. Developed by Martin Pring, it's a smoothed, rate-of-change composite that tries to give you the "big picture" momentum without the whipsaw noise of RSI or Stochastics. Here's an honest breakdown of what it does and where it fits.

### What This Indicator Actually Does

The KST is not your average oscillator. It's a **weighted moving average of four different rate-of-change (ROC) periods**—typically 10, 15, 20, and 30—each smoothed with a simple moving average. The result is a single line that oscillates around zero, designed to capture medium-term momentum shifts. The default settings (10, 15, 20, 30 with respective SMAs of 10, 10, 10, 15) are fine for daily charts, but they're not optimized for every market.

**Key difference from MACD:** MACD uses exponential moving averages of price, while KST uses ROC. This makes KST more sensitive to *percentage changes* in price, which helps in volatile assets like crypto but can overreact in slow-moving stocks.

### Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Four timeframes baked in:** Unlike a single ROC line, KST smooths multiple lookbacks. This reduces false signals compared to a basic momentum indicator.
- **Zero-line crossovers are the core signal:** When KST crosses above zero, it suggests accelerating bullish momentum. Below zero? Bearish pressure building.
- **Divergence is where it shines:** Price making higher highs while KST makes lower highs = bearish divergence. This is the signal worth watching most.
- **Signal line optional:** You can add a 9-period EMA of KST as a signal line (like MACD). It adds lag, though some traders prefer crossovers.

### Settings and How to Tune Them

The KST is defined by four ROC periods and their respective smoothing lengths. The defaults use ROC periods of 10, 15, 20, and 30, with SMAs of 10, 10, 10, and 15. The general principle: shorter ROC periods make the oscillator more responsive to recent price action, while longer ones emphasize broader momentum. Tighter smoothing shortens the lag but increases sensitivity to noise.

The same logic applies across markets. On faster timeframes or more volatile instruments, shorter ROC periods can make the oscillator keep pace with the swings; on higher timeframes, the defaults tend to hold up. An optional 9-period EMA signal line can be layered on for crossover-based reading, though it introduces additional lag.

### How to Use It for Entries and Exits

**Entry Strategy (Bullish):**
1. Wait for KST to cross **above zero** from below—this confirms a momentum shift.
2. Check for **price pullback to a key moving average** (e.g., 20 EMA) or support level.
3. Enter long on the next green candle. Stop loss below the recent swing low.

**Exit Strategy:**
- Trailing exit: When KST crosses **below its signal line** (if using one) or dips below zero.
- Aggressive exit: When price shows a bearish divergence—price higher, KST lower.

The chart pattern to look for is straightforward: a zero-line cross confirms a momentum shift, and a divergence between price highs and KST highs warns that momentum is fading even as price pushes up.

### Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Less whipsaw than RSI or Stochastics on daily+ timeframes.
- Divergence signals tend to be more readable on 4H and above.
- Works well as a complement to trend-following systems (e.g., with 50/200 EMA).
- Free on TradingView—no paid version needed.

**Cons:**
- **Laggy on lower timeframes** (<1H). Not useful for scalping.
- Signal line crossovers are slow—zero-line crossovers are the cleaner read.
- No built-in alerts for divergences (you'll need to spot them manually).
- Can give false signals in ranging markets, like any momentum oscillator.

### Who It's Actually For

- **Swing traders** on crypto, forex, or stocks.
- **Position traders** using daily or weekly charts.
- Traders who already use MACD but want a different lens on momentum confirmation.

**Not for:** Scalpers, day traders under 1H, or anyone who can't spot divergences manually.

### Better Alternatives If They Exist

- **MACD:** More widely used, more resources online. KST is faster but MACD has better community support.
- **Fisher Transform Indicator:** Less lag than KST but more prone to whipsaw. Better for day trading.
- **ROC (Rate of Change):** Simpler, but no smoothing. KST's smoothing is the whole point.

If you're already comfortable with MACD, consider KST as a secondary confirmation—not a replacement.

### FAQ: Real Trader Questions

**Q: Does KST work on crypto?**
Yes, but the ROC periods can be tightened for faster timeframes. Defaults are slow for Bitcoin's volatility.

**Q: What's the best timeframe?**
Daily or 4H. Lower than 1H gives too many whipsaws.

**Q: Can I set alerts for KST crossovers?**
Yes—TradingView allows alerts for "Crosses zero line" or "Crosses signal line." Zero-line alerts give the cleaner signals.

**Q: How is it different from MACD?**
MACD uses EMAs of price; KST uses smoothed ROC. KST measures *percentage change* momentum, making it more sensitive to volatility.

**Q: Does it repaint?**
No. KST is a fixed calculation based on historical data. It does not repaint.

### Final Verdict

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)** – A solid momentum oscillator for swing traders who can tolerate a bit of lag in exchange for cleaner signals. It won't replace MACD for everyone, but the divergence signals alone are worth adding to your toolkit. Pair it with support/resistance on a daily chart and you've got a useful confirmation tool. Just don't expect miracles on 5-minute charts.

**Worth installing?** Yes—for a swing trading watchlist. For day trading, Fisher Transform or Volume Profile are better suited.
