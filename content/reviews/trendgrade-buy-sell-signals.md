TrendGrade Buy Sell Signals is a TradingView trend indicator that does one thing and doesn't pretend otherwise: it marks buy and sell signals on your chart based on trend direction. There's no dashboard, no multi-factor scoring system, no proprietary oscillator buried under layers of branding. It's a signal layer, and the question worth answering is whether a signal layer earns a place on your chart at all.

That framing matters, because the honest answer for most indicators in this category is "only if you know what to do with it." Here's the straight read.

## What it actually is

Strip away the name and you have a trend-following tool that generates directional markers — buy signals when trend conditions favour upside, sell signals when they flip. The "Grade" in the name implies some form of signal quality or strength ranking, and the branding leans hard on that idea. What I can't tell you is exactly how that grading works, because the public documentation is thin. I won't invent a formula to fill the gap.

What I can say with confidence is what any trend-signal indicator is: a structured way of answering "which way is this moving, and is now a reasonable moment to act on that?" It removes some of the discretionary guesswork. It does not remove risk.

## How you'd actually use it

The workflow is straightforward and familiar to anyone who's traded trend systems before:

1. **Read the marker in context.** A buy signal printed into a clean uptrend with structure behind it means something different from the same signal printed into a choppy range. The indicator gives you the prompt; your read of market structure gives it meaning.
2. **Use it as a filter, not an oracle.** The most defensible use of any buy/sell marker is confirmation — you already had a bias, and the signal agrees. Treating the arrow as the entire thesis is how traders get chopped up.
3. **Pair it with your own risk framework.** The indicator has no opinion about position size, stop placement, or invalidation. That's your job, and no indicator does it for you.

As shown in the chart above, the signals layer cleanly over price without visual clutter — which sounds trivial until you've used indicators that bury the candles under three colours of noise.

## Where it earns its keep

**Clean, decisive output.** Buy and sell markers are the entire interface. There's no ambiguity about what the tool is telling you, which makes it easy to build rules around. Ambiguous indicators produce ambiguous decisions.

**Trend-native logic.** It's built for trending conditions, and it behaves like it. Trend tools are honest about their failure mode — they lag at reversals and whipsaw in ranges — and knowing that upfront is worth more than a hundred features.

**Low cognitive overhead.** You can glance at a chart and immediately see the signal history. For traders managing several instruments, that matters.

## Where it falls short

**Thin documentation.** This is the real weakness. A tool that grades signals should explain what the grade means, and the public material doesn't get there. You're partly trusting the black box.

**Lag is inherent.** Any trend-following signal arrives after the move has begun. That's not a flaw unique to TrendGrade — it's the cost of admission for the entire category — but it means late entries and give-back at turns are baked in.

**Ranges are its enemy.** In sideways conditions, buy/sell markers will fire and reverse. Every trend indicator does this. Plan for it or get bled by it.

## Who it's for

Discretionary trend traders who want a second opinion on entries and exits, and systematic traders building a rule set who need a clean directional trigger to test. If you scalp fast mean-reversion, this isn't your tool — trend signals will fight you. If you swing trade or position trade with the prevailing direction, it fits.

## FAQ

**Does it repaint?** No source material confirms or denies this, so I won't guess. Test it yourself on a replay before trusting a signal.

**Can I use it alone?** You can. You probably shouldn't. Signals without a risk plan are just arrows.

**What timeframes?** Undocumented. Trend logic generally scales across timeframes, but verify on yours.

## Verdict

TrendGrade Buy Sell Signals does the core job well: clean directional markers, no clutter, no pretence of being more than a trend tool. It loses a star for documentation that leaves the "grade" concept under-explained — when your name promises signal quality, you owe users a clearer account of it. For trend traders who want a disciplined confirmation layer and are willing to do their own validation, it's a solid addition. Not a system. A tool.
