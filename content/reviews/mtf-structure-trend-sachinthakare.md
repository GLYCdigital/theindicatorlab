Multi-timeframe analysis is one of those things every trader knows they should do and almost nobody does cleanly. Flipping between charts, mentally stacking structure from the higher timeframe onto the lower one, losing track of which swing actually matters — it's tedious and error-prone. "MTF Structure & Trend Pro [SachinThakare]" is a trend-category study that takes a run at that problem: reading market structure and trend across multiple timeframes and presenting it on a single chart. That's the premise, and it's a genuinely useful one to solve.

## What this indicator actually does

Strip away the "Pro" branding and the core idea is straightforward. The tool is built to identify **market structure** — the swing highs and lows that define whether a market is making higher highs and higher lows, or the reverse — and to layer **trend** context on top of it. The "MTF" in the name is the tell: this isn't a single-timeframe tool. It's designed to bring multi-timeframe structure and trend into one view so you don't have to hold it all in your head.

Because the official description is minimal, I'm going to be careful here. I can tell you what the name and category commit it to — multi-timeframe structure mapping and trend identification — and I can tell you who that serves. I can't tell you the exact inputs, the default lookback windows, or the precise rules it uses to label a swing. If you need those specifics, the script's own settings panel is the source of truth, not a review.

## The case for a structure-first, MTF approach

Most trend indicators are lagging by design — a moving average tells you where price has *been*. Structure-based approaches are different in kind. By anchoring to actual swing points rather than a smoothed average, you're reading the market's own skeleton: where buyers and sellers last made their stands. That's the philosophy this indicator is clearly built around, and it's the right one for anyone who trades price action.

The multi-timeframe layer is where the real value sits. A trader working a lower timeframe wants to know one thing above all: *is the higher timeframe trend with me or against me?* An indicator that surfaces that context directly on the chart — rather than requiring a second window and a mental merge — removes a step that costs attention and invites mistakes.

## How you'd actually use it

The workflow this implies is a top-down one. Establish the higher-timeframe structure and trend first, then drop to your execution timeframe and look for entries that align with it. The indicator's job is to keep that hierarchy visible so you're not taking a short into a screaming higher-timeframe uptrend just because your local chart looked heavy.

As shown in the chart above, the useful read is alignment: when lower-timeframe structure agrees with the higher-timeframe trend, you have a cleaner setup; when they conflict, you have a reason to stand aside or size down. That's not a signal to obey blindly — it's context to weigh. Treat the indicator as a filter, not an oracle.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Multi-timeframe structure and trend in one view — a real attention saver.
- Structure-based logic suits price-action traders better than lagging averages.
- Trend category is the right home for it: it's a context tool, not a trigger.
- The top-down workflow it encourages is sound practice regardless of the tool.

**Cons**
- The official description is thin. Documentation on the exact mechanics is light, which means you'll be reverse-engineering behaviour from the chart rather than reading a clear rulebook.
- Structure-based tools are inherently interpretive. Two traders can read the same swings differently, and the indicator's definition of "structure" is its own — you have to learn to trust or distrust it.
- Multi-timeframe overlays add visual weight. On a busy chart, they can clutter more than they clarify until you tune what's shown.

## Who it's for

This lands best with **discretionary price-action and swing traders** who already think in terms of structure and just want the multi-timeframe layer automated. If you're a systematic, rules-based trader who needs every input documented before you trust a signal, the sparse description will frustrate you. If you're brand new and looking for buy/sell arrows, this isn't that — it's a context layer that assumes you can read a chart.
