Fair value gaps are one of those concepts that sound simple until you try to trade them. Price leaves an imbalance behind, you wait for it to get filled, and half the time it never does — or it does, but only after you've already been stopped out. The **Fair Value Gap Fill Probability Pickmytrade** indicator is built around that exact problem. Instead of just drawing every gap on the chart, it frames each one in terms of how likely it is to get filled.

That's the core idea, and it's a useful one. Let me break down what that means in practice.

## What it actually does

A fair value gap (FVG) is a three-candle formation where the middle candle moves so fast that it leaves a price range the market didn't trade through efficiently. Traders treat these zones as magnets — areas price may revisit to "rebalance." The problem is that not every gap behaves the same way. Some fill almost immediately; others sit open for a long time and act as support or resistance instead of a target.

This indicator sits in the Trend category and takes the FVG concept one step further by attaching a probability dimension to each gap. Rather than treating every imbalance as equally tradeable, it gives you a way to sort them — which is the part that matters when you're deciding where to place an entry.

I want to be clear about one thing up front: I have not run this indicator, and I don't have the vendor's documentation in front of me. Everything below describes what the tool is built to do conceptually. I'm not going to invent settings, thresholds, or numbers that I can't verify.

## Why the probability framing matters

Most FVG indicators are drawing tools. They highlight gaps and leave the interpretation entirely to you. That's fine if you already have a framework for judging which gaps to trade, but it's a lot of discretion to hand a newer trader.

Adding a probability layer changes the workflow. Instead of asking "is there a gap here?" you start asking "is this gap worth watching?" That's a more useful question, and it's closer to how experienced discretionary traders actually think about imbalances.

The trade-off is obvious: any probability model is only as good as its assumptions. If you don't understand what the tool is weighing, you're just trusting a number. So treat the probability output as a filter, not a signal.

## How you'd use it

The natural workflow is to let the indicator surface gaps and rank them, then apply your own read on trend and structure before acting.

- **In a trending market**, unfilled gaps in the direction of the trend tend to get more attention than counter-trend gaps.
- **In choppy conditions**, the probability reading becomes more important, because gaps fill and fail unpredictably.
- **As a confluence tool**, it works best alongside your existing entry logic rather than replacing it.

If you're a pure price-action trader who already reads imbalances well, this adds a second opinion. If you're newer to the concept, it gives you structure.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Turns a discretionary concept into something more structured and sortable.
- Saves screen time by helping you prioritise which gaps to watch.
- Fits naturally into an existing FVG or smart-money workflow.
- Useful as a confluence layer rather than a standalone system.

**Cons**
- The probability output is a model, not a guarantee — gaps still fail.
- Without clear documentation, understanding *why* a gap scores the way it does can be difficult.
- Like most FVG tools, it can clutter a chart if you don't filter aggressively.
- It won't fix bad risk management or a poor read on trend.

## Who it's for

This is aimed at traders who already use fair value gaps or smart-money concepts and want a more systematic way to rank setups. It's also a reasonable learning tool for someone building intuition about which imbalances tend to hold. It is **not** for traders looking for a plug-and-play buy/sell signal — that's not what this is.

## FAQ

**Can I use it on any market?**
Fair value gaps appear across markets, but the behaviour of gaps varies by instrument and timeframe. Test on your own market before relying on it.

**Is it a replacement for a trading plan?**
No. It's a context tool. It informs decisions, it doesn't make them.

The Fair Value Gap Fill Probability Pickmytrade indicator takes a well-known concept and adds a genuinely useful dimension: prioritisation.
