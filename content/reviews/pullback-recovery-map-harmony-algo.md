Most trend indicators tell you a trend exists. Very few try to tell you where a pullback sits within that trend and how the recovery is developing. "Pullback Recovery Map [Harmony Algo]" is one of the second type. It's a study (an overlay-style analysis tool, not a strategy) that focuses on the pullback phase of a move and the recovery that follows it.

Before going further, one honest caveat: the publicly available description for this script is essentially empty. It lists the author's TradingView account tier and nothing else — no parameter documentation, no methodology notes, no default values. So this review is built around what the name and category tell us the tool is designed to do, not around a feature list I can quote. I'm not going to invent settings or numbers to fill the gap. If you want the specifics, the script's own inputs are the place to look.

## What it actually does

The concept is straightforward. A trend doesn't move in a straight line — it advances, retraces, then (ideally) resumes. The interesting part for most discretionary traders is that middle phase: the pullback. Is it a healthy pause or the start of a reversal? And once price starts moving again, how far has the recovery gotten?

The "Pullback Recovery Map" name points to exactly that: a visual map of the pullback and the recovery structure afterward, plotted on your chart. It's filed under the Trend category, which tells you the underlying logic is trend-oriented — it's not a mean-reversion or oscillator tool. It's meant to be read in the context of a directional move.

The "Harmony Algo" suffix is branding. It signals the author's own algorithmic framework rather than a standard built-in formula. Without the source documentation, I can't tell you what that framework computes internally, and I won't pretend otherwise.

## Where it fits in a workflow

Because the tool is about the pullback phase, it's most useful when you already have a directional bias. The natural workflow looks like this:

1. Identify the prevailing trend on a higher timeframe.
2. Drop this study on the chart and watch how pullbacks develop within that trend.
3. Use the recovery mapping to judge whether the retracement is being absorbed or extended.

That makes it a **confirmation and structure tool**, not an entry trigger on its own. If you're the kind of trader who buys pullbacks in an uptrend or sells rallies in a downtrend, the concept aligns with how you already think. If you scalp momentum breakouts, the pullback framing is less relevant to you.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Focused on a genuinely under-served phase. Plenty of indicators flag trend direction; far fewer attempt to map the retracement and recovery structure specifically.
- Trend-category logic keeps it aligned with the way most swing traders actually operate — with the move, not against it.
- As a study, it's non-intrusive: it plots analysis rather than generating signals you're forced to act on.

**Cons**
- The documentation is effectively missing. For a paid-tier-adjacent algorithm tool, that's a real gap — you'll be reverse-engineering the behaviour from the chart.
- Because it's proprietary ("Harmony Algo"), you can't inspect the logic the way you could an open-source script. That's a trust cost.
- Pullback tools are inherently lagging in the sense that they describe what price has already done — they won't tell you the future.

## Who it's for

Swing traders and position traders with a trend-following bias who want a structured read on pullbacks. It suits people who already have a discretionary process for spotting trends and want a second lens on whether a retracement is behaving normally. It's a poor fit for pure scalpers, range traders, and anyone who wants a plug-and-play signal generator with documented rules.

The core idea is sound and genuinely useful: most traders lose money on pullbacks, not on trends, so a tool that maps that phase has real value.
