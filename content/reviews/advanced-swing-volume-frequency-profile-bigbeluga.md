BigBeluga's "Advanced Swing Volume & Frequency Profile" sits in a category that's easy to get wrong: it tries to blend two very different lenses — swing structure and volume distribution — into a single view. The name tells you most of what you need to know. It's a profile-style tool that leans on swing points and volume/frequency data rather than a simple moving-average crossover. Whether that marriage actually pays off depends on how you trade.

Because the official documentation for this indicator is thin (no description was retrievable at the time of writing), this review stays deliberately conceptual. I'm not going to invent default settings, thresholds, or "optimal" timeframes — you won't find any of that here. What I can do is explain the idea, the trade-offs, and who should care.

## What it actually is

The core premise is a familiar one dressed in new clothes. Swing-based tools mark the highs and lows that define market structure. Volume profile tools show where trading activity clusters by price. This indicator attempts to combine the two: instead of treating volume as a single histogram at the bottom of the chart, it frames volume around swing behaviour and frequency, so you can see where price has been "busy" relative to the swings that matter.

In practice, that means you're looking at a chart overlay that highlights swing points and, alongside them, some representation of how often price visits certain levels or how volume concentrates around those swings. The "frequency" part is the interesting wrinkle — it implies a count or density view rather than a raw volume bar.

As shown in the chart above, the output reads as a structural map rather than a signal generator. That's the right way to think about it.

## Key features (conceptually)

- **Swing detection** as the backbone. Everything hangs off identified pivots, so the indicator's usefulness is tied to how cleanly swings are defined.
- **Volume context** layered onto those swings, rather than shown in isolation.
- **Frequency profiling** — a density-style view that suggests where price lingers, which is often more informative than where it moved fast.
- **A trend-oriented lens.** It's filed under Trend, so the intent is to help you read directional bias and structural health, not to fire buy/sell arrows.

## How to use it

Treat it as context, not a trigger. The workflow I'd expect a sensible trader to follow:

1. Read the prevailing swing structure first — are you making higher highs and higher lows, or the reverse?
2. Check where volume and frequency cluster relative to current price. Price sitting above a heavy cluster is a different situation from price trapped inside one.
3. Use those clusters as reference levels for risk placement and target selection, not as automatic entries.

If you're the type who wants a green arrow and a beep, this isn't built for you. If you want to understand *why* a level matters, the profile framing earns its keep.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Combines two genuinely complementary ideas — structure and volume — instead of rehashing a single concept.
- The frequency angle offers something most volume indicators skip: persistence, not just magnitude.
- Fits a discretionary, context-first workflow well.
- Sits comfortably in the trend-reading category without pretending to be predictive.

**Cons**
- Swing detection is inherently subjective; different swing definitions will shift what you see.
- Profile-style overlays add visual density, which some traders find cluttered.
- With limited official documentation, new users have to reverse-engineer the visual language.
- It won't tell you when to click buy — that burden stays with you.

## Who it's for

Discretionary swing and position traders who already think in terms of market structure and want volume context attached to it. If you trade breakouts and retests off swing levels, or you're building a thesis around where price has historically spent time, this is a reasonable addition. Scalpers on lower timeframes will likely find it noisy and slow-moving relative to their decision speed.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
No — based on its design intent, it's a context tool. It maps structure and volume, and you draw the conclusions.

**What timeframe is best?**
The source material doesn't specify one, so I won't pretend to. Swing-based tools generally scale with the structure you're reading, but you'll need to judge fit on your own charts.

**Can I use it alone?**
You can, but it's more useful paired with a trigger mechanism — price action at a level, a momentum confirmation, or your existing entry logic.

**Is it a repainting indicator?**
Not documented, and I won't speculate. Check the behavior on a live chart before relying on it.

## Final verdict

This is a thoughtful, well-intentioned blend of structure and volume that earns points for originality and for not pretending to be a signal machine. The lack of clear public documentation is a real friction point, and the visual complexity won't suit everyone. But as a context layer for discretionary traders who already think in swings, it does a job most indicators ignore.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ — solid, useful, and worth a look if structural context is your thing. Not a holy grail, and it doesn't claim to be.
