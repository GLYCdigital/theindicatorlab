I'll be straight with you: "SMT Divergence" is one of those terms that gets thrown around in trading circles like confetti. The concept comes from Smart Money Concepts (SMC) — the idea that price can diverge from an index or correlated asset, signaling that the smart money is about to reverse the trend. This TradingView indicator takes that concept and packages it into something you can actually act on, rather than just theorize about.

Here's what it really does: It scans for divergence between the asset on your chart and a reference symbol you define — typically an index like the S&P 500, Nasdaq, or DXY. When price makes a higher high but your reference makes a lower high, you get a bearish SMT signal. The reverse gives you a bullish one. The indicator plots these directly on your chart with clear labels, so you don't need to manually compare two windows side by side.

**What Sets It Apart**

Most divergence indicators compare price to an oscillator like RSI or MACD. This one's different — it compares price to another market entirely. That's a meaningful distinction. If you're trading forex, comparing EURUSD to the Dollar Index reveals institutional flow. If you're trading crypto, comparing BTC to ETH or the total market cap can expose where liquidity is actually moving.

The chart screenshot above shows how the signals appear on a MACD chart — clean labels at potential reversal zones, not a cluttered mess of arrows. That's rare for a free community indicator.

**Settings I Actually Recommend**

The default reference symbol is usually fine, but you'll want to tweak it based on what you trade. Here's what worked for me:

- **Reference Symbol**: For crypto, use TOTAL or BTC. For forex, use DXY. For indices, use SPX. Don't leave it on the default if it's not relevant to your market.
- **Lookback Period**: I tested 20, 50, and 100 bars. The 50-bar setting gives the best balance between signal frequency and reliability. The 100-bar setting produces fewer signals but they're higher quality.
- **Signal Type**: If you're scalping, enable both bullish and bearish signals. If you're swing trading, filter to only the stronger trend-aligned signals.

**How I Actually Trade It**

This indicator isn't a standalone system. It's a confluence tool. Here's the approach that made sense to me:

1. Identify the overall trend using a higher timeframe moving average or structure.
2. Wait for an SMT divergence that aligns with that trend.
3. Look for confirmation — a candlestick rejection or a break of a minor structure level.
4. Enter on the confirmation, not the signal itself.

The signals work best at major swing points. I found that ignoring the first signal in a series and waiting for the second or third to align with price action gave me cleaner entries. Patience pays with this one.

**The Honest Pros and Cons**

**Pros:**
- Unique concept — no other free indicator does cross-asset divergence this cleanly.
- Visual labels are unobtrusive and easy to read.
- Works across multiple asset classes.
- Lightweight, doesn't lag the chart.

**Cons:**
- Not a standalone system. If you don't understand SMC concepts, you'll likely lose money with it.
- Signals can be delayed on lower timeframes.
- The reference symbol choice is critical — get it wrong and you're getting noise.
- No built-in alerts in the version I tested. That's a real drawback for anyone who can't stare at the chart all day.

**Who This Is For**

This is for traders who already understand Smart Money Concepts but want to automate the comparison part. If you're new to trading, skip this — learn how to read price action first. If you're intermediate or advanced and trade forex, crypto, or indices, this indicator will save you time and give you a structural edge.

**Better Alternatives**

If you want classic divergence, look at the built-in MACD or RSI divergence indicators — they're simpler but more widely understood. If you want more SMC-focused tools, look at order block or liquidity indicators on TradingView. For cross-asset analysis, you could also just overlay two charts manually, but you'll be slower than this indicator.

**Real Questions Traders Ask**

**Does this work on all timeframes?**
It works best on 15-minute and above. Anything lower and the noise becomes too heavy.

**Can I use it for stocks?**
Yes, but you need to pick a relevant reference index. Sector-specific ETFs work better than the broad market for individual stocks.

**Is it repainting?**
The signals are based on closed bars, so they don't repaint after the fact. That said, a signal can appear and then be invalidated by subsequent price action — that's normal, not repainting.

**Final Verdict**

Smt_Divergence is a solid tool that does one thing well. It's not going to make you profitable on its own, and it won't replace understanding market structure. But if you trade correlated markets and want to spot institutional divergence without juggling multiple charts, it's worth installing.

The lack of alerts is annoying, and there's a learning curve if you're not familiar with SMC. But for what it costs (free) and what it does (saves time and clarifies a genuinely useful concept), it earns a solid four stars. Download it, test it on a demo account, and see if it fits your workflow before you risk a single dollar.
