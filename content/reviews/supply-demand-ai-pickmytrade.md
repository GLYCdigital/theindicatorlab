Supply and demand zones are one of those concepts that every trader claims to trade and almost nobody draws consistently. Supply Demand AI [PickMyTrade] is a TradingView indicator that tries to take that drawing work off your hands — it plots supply and demand areas on the chart so you can judge price reaction against them rather than squinting at candles and guessing where a zone begins and ends.

I want to be upfront about one thing before going further: I don't have the author's full documentation in front of me, so this review sticks to what the tool is and how it fits into a workflow. I'm not going to hand you a list of settings or a performance table I can't verify. What follows is an honest read on the concept and the category.

## What it actually does

The premise is straightforward. Supply zones are areas where selling pressure previously overwhelmed buyers; demand zones are where buying absorbed the selling. Price tends to react when it returns to these levels — sometimes reversing, sometimes pausing, sometimes slicing straight through. The indicator's job is to identify and draw those areas on your chart automatically, so the zones are objective and repeatable instead of redrawn after the fact.

The "AI" in the name signals that zone detection isn't a fixed rule — it's presumably based on pattern recognition rather than a single mechanical condition. I can't tell you what that logic is, and I won't pretend to. What I can say is that the value of any zone tool lives or dies on two questions: are the zones drawn where you'd draw them yourself, and do they stay put once price moves? That's the standard you should hold this indicator to.

## Where it earns a place on the chart

Most traders I know who trade zones do it badly for one reason — they redraw levels to fit the price action after the fact. An automated tool removes that temptation. Once the zone is on the chart, it's there, and you have to deal with price respecting it or ignoring it.

That objectivity is the real selling point. Used well, a supply/demand overlay gives you a visual map: you know where the interesting levels are before the session starts, and you can plan entries around them rather than reacting candle by candle. For a trend-following approach, it's a useful companion — zones help you decide where to add to a position or where a pullback might stall.

As shown in the chart above, the overlay reads cleanly against price. That's the experience you want: zones as context, not as a signal generator screaming at you.

## How I'd actually use it

The honest workflow is this. Put it on a chart, look at the zones it produces, and ask whether they match the levels you'd have marked. If they mostly do, it's saving you time. If they don't, no amount of "AI" branding fixes that.

From there, treat the zones as areas of interest, not triggers. A trend trader might watch for price returning to a demand zone within an established uptrend and look for confirmation before acting. A range trader might use the zones as edges. The indicator draws the map; you still drive.

One caveat worth stating plainly: zone tools are inherently subjective in their output. Two traders can look at the same chart and disagree on where a zone should start. Don't expect this — or any indicator — to settle that argument for you.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Removes the manual drawing and the temptation to redraw after the fact.
- Gives you a repeatable, objective view of supply and demand areas.
- Fits naturally into a trend or pullback workflow as context rather than a signal.

**Cons**
- The "AI" label is doing marketing work; you can't verify the logic from the chart alone.
- Zone quality is the whole game, and that's exactly what you need to test yourself before trusting it.
- Without the author's documentation, understanding precisely how zones are defined takes trial and error.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already think in terms of support, resistance and reaction levels. If you trade pullbacks in a trend, or you want a cleaner visual map of where price has previously turned, this is aimed at you. It's less useful for pure mechanical systems that need a binary signal — zones are context, and context doesn't fire arrows.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
Based on the concept, it draws supply and demand areas. Treat those as levels to watch, not entry triggers.

**Can I use it on any timeframe?**
Zone logic generally works across timeframes, but the zones you see will differ by timeframe. Check it on the one you trade.

**Is the AI part something I can inspect?**
Not from the chart. You'd need the author's documentation to understand the detection logic.

## Verdict

Supply Demand AI [PickMyTrade] does the unglamorous but genuinely useful work of mapping supply and demand zones for you. It won't trade for you, and the "AI" framing oversells what's happening under the hood — but as a context tool for discretionary traders, it earns its place. Four stars: solid, useful, and honest about what it is once you strip the branding.
