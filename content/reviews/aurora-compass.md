## Aurora_Compass Review: Settings, Strategy & How to Use It

Aurora_Compass is not a moving average cross or an RSI clone. It uses a polar coordinate system to project price direction and momentum across multiple timeframes at once. That's an unusual approach, and whether it earns a place in a workflow depends on how much you value multi-timeframe context in a single pane.

### What This Indicator Actually Does

Aurora_Compass plots a compass-like display on the chart—typically a circular gauge or directional arrows—showing dominant trend and momentum vectors across three timeframes. The premise: rather than flipping between charts, you get one visual telling you whether the short-term aligns with the intermediate and long-term trend.

The indicator builds a composite score from price action, volume, and a smoothed momentum oscillator, then rotates a needle or colored arc toward the current trend direction. When all three timeframes point the same way, the compass "locks" and presents a stronger signal.

### Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Multi-timeframe alignment without clutter.** One compass replaces three separate windows.
- **Polar projection visualization.** Unusual but intuitive once you adjust. The needle angle corresponds to trend strength, not just direction.
- **Customizable timeframe inputs.** The three timeframes can be set to match a trading style.
- **Alert logic.** Can trigger when all three timeframes align (bullish/bearish lock) or when they diverge significantly.
- **Low lag.** Smoothing is adjustable, and default settings are intended to stay responsive without whipsawing.

### Settings and How to Tune Them

Timeframes are set in three slots—fast, medium, and slow—and should be chosen to match the horizon being traded. Smoothing is adjustable: lower values make the compass quicker but noisier, higher values make it steadier but slower. The lock threshold governs how much alignment is required before the compass signals a lock; a stricter threshold produces fewer, cleaner lock events, a looser one produces more but with more marginal setups. Color mode can be set to show strength rather than direction alone.

There is no single "best" configuration here. The right combination depends on the instrument, the timeframe being traded, and how much noise the trader is willing to tolerate.

### How to Use It for Entries and Exits

**Entries:**
Wait for the compass to show a lock—all three arrows pointing the same direction. Then look for a pullback toward a moving average or a key support/resistance level on the fast timeframe. Enter when price resumes the lock direction.

**Exits:**
When the compass shows divergence between timeframes—fast pointing up, medium sideways, slow down—that's a warning. A common approach is to exit half the position at that point, and exit the rest if two timeframes flip.

**Stop loss:**
Place below the most recent swing low (for longs) or above the most recent swing high (for shorts) on the fast timeframe. The compass does not provide a stop; that remains a manual decision.

### Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Reduces chart clutter—one indicator replaces three.
- Good at catching multi-timeframe alignment for trend trades.
- Customizable across different time horizons.
- Alerts can be configured for lock events.

**Cons:**
- Learning curve. The polar display is not intuitive at first.
- Not a standalone system. Price action and support/resistance are still required for entries.
- Whipsaws in choppy markets. When all three timeframes are sideways, the compass can flicker.
- No built-in stop loss or take profit logic.

### Who It's Actually For

- **Trend traders** who want a quick read on alignment without stacking multiple indicators.
- **Day traders** who need higher-timeframe context alongside their execution timeframe.
- **Swing traders** who want to avoid taking trades against the higher-timeframe trend.

It is **not** aimed at traders needing sub-minute precision, nor at range-bound traders who thrive in chop.

### Better Alternatives If They Exist

- **Supertrend + EMA combo** – Simpler, but no multi-timeframe overlay.
- **MACD on three timeframes** – More setup work, same underlying concept.
- **Market Cipher B** – A fuller suite (volume, momentum, cycles), but more bloated.

Aurora_Compass is distinctive in its visual approach. No direct replacement does the same thing.

### FAQ

**Q: Does it repaint?**
A: No. The compass updates in real time based on current data. Past signals do not change.

**Q: Can I use it for crypto?**
A: Yes. Works on BTC, ETH, and altcoins. Lower smoothing is recommended due to volatility.

**Q: Does it work on lower timeframes like 1-minute?**
A: Not really. Lag becomes an issue below 5m. Stick to 5m and above.

**Q: Is it free?**

### Final Verdict

Aurora_Compass does one thing well: showing multi-timeframe alignment in a single glance. It's not a holy grail, but it's a solid tool for trend traders who value efficiency. The polar display takes getting used to, and it won't save you from bad entries or choppy markets. Paired with price action and proper risk management, it can sharpen timing.
