Market structure tools are everywhere now, and most of them are the same idea wearing a different coat: mark the swing highs and lows, connect them, and call it "smart money." This one sits in TradingView's trend category under the name Mbedaiwi — Market Structure and Price Action, and the title tells you exactly where it's aiming. It's a chart-reading aid, not a signal machine. That distinction matters more than the feature list.

Worth stating up front: there's no official documentation floating around for this one, so I can't quote you a default lookback, a pivot sensitivity, or a list of toggle names. What follows is about what the tool is for and how you'd actually work with it — not a spec sheet I don't have.

## What it's actually doing

The premise is straightforward. Price action traders read markets by tracking structure: where the last meaningful high printed, whether the low that followed held, and whether the sequence is still making higher highs and higher lows or has started to break down. Doing that by eye is easy on a clean chart and miserable on a noisy one.

This indicator's job is to take that visual labour off your plate. It plots the structural reference points on the chart so you're not squinting at every candle, and it frames price action in the context of trend. In the trend category, that's the sensible place for it — structure is the skeleton that trend direction hangs on.

I'm not going to tell you how many bars it looks back, because I don't know, and guessing would be worse than saying nothing. What I can say is that any structure tool is only as good as its pivot logic, and that's the thing to scrutinise when you load it.

## Where it earns its place

The real value of a tool like this isn't the lines themselves — it's the discipline they impose. Once structure is marked on your chart, "is this a pullback or a reversal?" stops being a feeling and becomes a question you can answer by looking at whether a relevant level held.

That's the workflow it supports:

- Load it on a timeframe you actually trade, not the one that makes the chart look prettiest.
- Use the marked structure to orient yourself — is the sequence intact, or has it broken?
- Then drop to your execution timeframe for entries *in the direction* of that structure.

The tool does the mapping. You still do the deciding. Anyone expecting it to hand over entries will be disappointed, and that's true of essentially every structure indicator worth using.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Focused on a genuinely useful concept. Market structure isn't a gimmick; it's the grammar of price action.
- Belongs to a clear category (trend) rather than pretending to be a universal oracle.
- As a visual aid, it saves the constant manual re-drawing that structural analysis demands.
- Pairs naturally with any entry method you already trust — it doesn't demand you adopt a whole new system.

**Cons**

- Without documentation, you're reverse-engineering the settings by feel. That's a real friction cost.
- Structure tools are inherently lagging: pivots confirm after the fact, so the most recent swing is always provisional.
- Crowded category. There are many indicators doing similar work, and nothing here suggests it's a category-defining outlier.
- It won't tell you *why* structure broke. News, liquidity, exhaustion — all invisible to it.

## Who it's for

Discretionary price action traders who already think in terms of swings and breaks, and want that thinking visualised rather than held in their head. Also useful for people learning structure — having the levels drawn for you is a decent training wheel while you build the eye. Less useful for pure systematic traders who want a rules-based signal they can code against, and close to useless for anyone who wants an indicator to think for them.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
No — it's a structural overlay. It shows you where structure sits; interpreting that is on you.

**What timeframe should I use it on?**
Whatever you trade. Structure exists on every timeframe; the meaningful one is the one you act on.

**Can I use it with other indicators?**
Yes. It's a context layer, so it sits comfortably alongside momentum or volume tools without conflict.

**Do I need to understand market structure first?**
It helps a lot. The indicator shows you the levels, but knowing what a break *means* is the part that makes it useful.

## Verdict

A solid, purposeful tool that does one job honestly. It won't revolutionise your trading, and the missing documentation is a genuine annoyance, but if market structure is how you read charts, having it mapped automatically is worth the chart space. Four stars — good at what it claims, not exceptional, and honest about being an aid rather than an answer.
