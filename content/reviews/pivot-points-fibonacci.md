## What This Indicator Actually Does

Pivot Points Fibonacci is a pivot point calculator that overlays Fibonacci extension levels on top of the classic daily, weekly, or monthly pivots. It plots the central pivot (PP), three support levels (S1–S3), and three resistance levels (R1–R3), then adds Fibonacci-based extensions off the pivot range.

If you have seen one pivot indicator, you have seen most of this. The Fibonacci twist is the main differentiator—it gives you extra reference lines beyond the standard arithmetic levels.

## Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Fibonacci overlay**: Most pivot indicators only give you R1–R3 and S1–S3. This one adds Fib extensions, which can mark areas where price might stall or accelerate.
- **Timeframe flexibility**: Works on intraday and daily/weekly charts.
- **Clean visuals**: The lines are color-coded and can be kept from cluttering the chart depending on the opacity you choose.

Beyond that, there is not much else. There is no multi-timeframe aggregation, no dynamic pivots, and no auto-detection of key levels. It is a static calculator.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

- **Timeframe**: The indicator supports daily, weekly, and monthly pivot periods, plus intraday use. Higher timeframes produce fewer recalculations; lower timeframes recalculate more often.
- **Fib levels**: The extension levels can be toggled on and off individually, so you can display only the ones you find relevant to your approach.
- **Line style**: Pivot and support/resistance lines can be styled independently, so you can visually distinguish the central pivot from the outer levels.
- **Color**: Line colors are configurable, which matters for keeping the chart readable when several extensions are displayed at once.
- **Line width**: Each level can be given its own width, so the central pivot can be made to stand out from the secondary lines.

## How to Use It for Entries and Exits

The indicator itself only plots levels; it does not generate signals. Any strategy built on it is a discretionary overlay on top of those levels.

**Entry ideas**:
- Price touching R1 or S1 with a rejection candle, as a mean-reversion play.
- Price breaking R1 and retesting it as support, targeting R2 or a Fib extension above.
- Price slicing through PP and then retesting it, entering in the direction of the break.

**Exit ideas**:
- Partial profits at the Fib extension levels.
- Stops placed beyond the pivot level being traded.

Note that Fib extensions tend to act as areas where price stalls or hesitates rather than as hard reversal points, so they are more naturally used for partial exits than for full reversal calls.

## Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros**:
- Simple to install and configure. No learning curve.
- Fib extensions add context that standard pivot levels alone do not provide.
- Works across markets—forex, crypto, and stocks.

**Cons**:
- **Static lines**. Pivots recalculate only once per period, so on fast intraday charts they can become stale quickly.
- **No dynamic support/resistance**. A moving average or VWAP will often be more responsive on trending days.
- **Fib levels are arbitrary**. The common retracement ratios are conventions, not predictive guarantees, and false signals are common.
- **No alerts**. There is no notification when price reaches a level, which is a notable omission for a modern indicator.

## Who It's Actually For

- **Beginner traders** who want to learn pivot points without paying for a premium indicator.
- **Swing traders** on daily or weekly charts, where static levels hold up over multiple sessions.
- **Not for scalpers** or day traders on very low timeframes, where the levels decay too fast to be useful.

## Better Alternatives

- **Auto Pivot Levels** (free): Adjusts dynamically to volatility, which can be more useful in trending markets.
- **Market Cipher B** (paid): Includes pivot zones, volume profile, and momentum. Overkill for most, but more feature-rich.
- **Standard TradingView Pivot** (built-in): Similar core functionality with fewer lines. If you do not need the Fib extensions, the native tool covers the same ground.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this repaint?**
A: No. It is based on closed candles.

**Q: Can I use it on crypto?**
A: Yes, though crypto's 24/7 nature means the daily pivot is calculated at a fixed clock time rather than a true session boundary. Adjust accordingly.

**Q: Best timeframe?**
A: Daily for swing trading, 4-hour for intraday swing. Lower timeframes produce more noise.

## Final Verdict

Pivot Points Fibonacci is a *fine* indicator. It does exactly what it says—plots pivot levels with Fibonacci extensions—without bugs or bloat. But "fine" does not stand out in a crowded market. The static nature, lack of alerts, and arbitrary Fib levels hold it back from being great.

If you are new to pivots and want to learn, it is a reasonable starting point. If you are experienced, you will likely outgrow it quickly. Three stars is honest: functional, but unremarkable.
