Some indicators try to do one thing. This one bundles two ideas that are usually sold separately: session awareness and the smart-money vocabulary of liquidity and inducement. Whether that combination is useful depends entirely on how you already trade.

## What it actually does

The name tells you most of what you need to know. **Liquidity & Inducement + Sessions [TradeIQ]** is a TradingView overlay that maps two things onto your chart: session context (the time windows that define when different market participants are active) and the liquidity/inducement framework that comes out of the inner-circle or smart-money style of price action reading.

Liquidity, in this vocabulary, refers to clusters of resting orders — typically around prior highs and lows — that price tends to gravitate toward. Inducement is the related idea that price often makes a small move to trap early entries before reversing toward the real target. Sessions are the time-based filters: Asia, London, New York, and the overlaps between them.

The indicator sits in the Trend category on TradingView, which is a slightly loose fit — this is really a structural and timing tool rather than a trend-following signal generator. Treat the category label as a filing decision, not a description.

## Where it earns its keep

The strongest argument for a combined tool is that it stops you from reading liquidity setups at the wrong time of day. A sweep of an Asian range low means something different at the London open than it does in the dead of the New York afternoon. By putting session shading and liquidity context on the same chart, the indicator forces that question.

That's the whole value proposition, and it's a legitimate one. Most traders who struggle with smart-money concepts don't fail because they can't spot a liquidity pool — they fail because they take the same setup at 3am that they'd only take during a session overlap. Bundling the two ideas into one overlay makes the timing filter hard to ignore.

The "+ Sessions" half is also the part most likely to be genuinely useful regardless of your style, even if you never touch the liquidity side of it.

## How you'd use it

The workflow is straightforward on paper: identify the liquidity levels the indicator marks, note which session you're in, and only act when the two line up. In practice, the discipline is in the second step. If you're going to ignore session context, you're paying for half an indicator.

As shown in the chart above, the visual layering of session blocks against price structure is the core of the tool. Whether the liquidity markings are clear enough to trade off depends on how much other clutter is on your chart — this is not a minimalistic overlay, and stacking it with several other indicators will get busy fast.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Combines two concepts that are genuinely related but usually split across separate scripts
- Session context is a real edge for anyone trading intraday
- Fits naturally into a smart-money or price-action workflow without forcing a signal

**Cons**
- The liquidity/inducement vocabulary is interpretive — two traders can disagree on what counts
- Categorized under Trend, which may mislead anyone expecting an entry signal
- Visual density is a real concern on lower timeframes with other tools loaded

## Who should install it

If you already trade session-based intraday setups and want liquidity levels visible without a second script, this is a reasonable fit. It suits discretionary traders who read structure themselves and want context, not instructions.

It's a poor fit for anyone who wants a mechanical buy/sell trigger, and probably wasted on swing traders holding for weeks where session timing is noise.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
No — based on what the tool does, it's a context and structure overlay, not a signal generator. You still make the call.

**Can I use it on any timeframe?**
Session logic is inherently intraday, so it's most meaningful on lower timeframes. On higher timeframes the session shading becomes less relevant.

**Is it a repaint-free indicator?**
I can't confirm that from available information. Check the author's page on TradingView before relying on historical markings.

## Verdict

A sensible pairing of two ideas that belong together, executed as a contextual overlay rather than a signal machine. The session filter is the durable half; the liquidity markings are useful but interpretive. If that matches how you already trade, it's worth a look — just don't expect it to make the decision for you.
