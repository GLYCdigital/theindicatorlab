## What it does

At its core, this is a trend tool. It plots on price and, like most indicators in this family, is designed to help you identify when a market is trending and in which direction, then stay with that move until the trend weakens. For swing traders, that's the whole game — catching the middle of a move and avoiding the chop that eats accounts.

As shown in the chart above, the tool produces visual trend guidance on the price pane. The exact signal mechanics — how it flips, what confirms a trend change — aren't documented in anything I can verify, so I won't pretend to know them. What I can say is that the design intent is clear: give a swing trader a directional bias and a reason to hold or exit.

## Key features

Because there's no published parameter list, I'm keeping this to what the category and naming imply:

- **Trend-oriented logic** — built to follow direction, not predict reversals.
- **Swing-trading focus** — the name says it, and the behaviour fits: it's meant for holding positions over days, not minutes.
- **Price-pane overlay** — it works alongside your candles rather than in a separate subwindow, so you read it in context with structure.
- **Strategy framing** — it's presented as a strategy, which typically means it's meant to be evaluated as a system with entries and exits, not a standalone signal you cherry-pick.

That's a short list, and deliberately so. I'd rather give you four honest points than a dozen invented ones.

## How to use it

The workflow for a tool like this is straightforward and doesn't require me to invent settings:

1. **Establish bias.** Let the indicator tell you which way the trend is leaning on your chosen timeframe.
2. **Wait for agreement.** Don't fight it — if it says up, you're looking for longs, and vice versa.
3. **Manage the exit with the trend.** A trend tool's value is in telling you when the trend is *over*, not just when it starts. Use it to decide when to step aside.
4. **Confirm with structure.** No single indicator should be your only input. Pair it with support/resistance, volume or your own price-action read.

The important caveat: I haven't run this, so I can't tell you how it behaves in ranging markets or how often it whipsaws. Test that yourself before risking capital.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Purpose-built for swing trading — the scope is clear, which is more than many indicators manage.
- Trend-following logic is a proven, durable approach; it doesn't rely on exotic assumptions.
- Price-pane overlay keeps your chart readable and contextual.
- Fits naturally into a structured, rules-based workflow.

**Cons**
- **No documentation.** This is the big one. Without published settings or logic, you're reverse-engineering behaviour from the chart. That's a real barrier.
- Trend tools lag by nature — they confirm moves rather than lead them. Expect to give up the first part of any trend.
- In choppy, range-bound conditions, trend-following tools tend to produce poor signals. No trend tool escapes this.
- The "strategy" label raises expectations of a testable system; without documentation, you can't verify how it was constructed.

## Who it's for

This suits **discretionary swing traders** who already think in terms of trend and want a second opinion on direction and holding. It's also reasonable for traders building a rules-based process who want a trend filter as one component. It is *not* for scalpers, mean-reversion traders, or anyone who needs a fully documented, plug-and-play system with published rules. If you demand transparency before you trust a tool, the missing documentation will bother you.
