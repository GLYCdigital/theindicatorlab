Market structure is one of those concepts every trader talks about and almost nobody agrees on. Higher highs, lower lows, breaks of structure, changes of character — the vocabulary is shared, the implementation rarely is. The Sight of Market Structure/Setup [JPT] sits squarely in that territory. It is a trend-category tool built around the idea of reading market structure and highlighting setups that flow from it, rather than dumping another moving-average crossover on your chart.

That framing alone tells you what kind of indicator this is: a discretionary aid, not a signal machine. And that distinction matters for whether it belongs in your toolkit.

## What it actually does

Based on how it is catalogued and named, the indicator is designed to help you see the structural context of a chart — the swing behaviour that defines whether a market is trending, consolidating, or transitioning. The "Setup" half of the name suggests it also surfaces moments where that structure presents something actionable, rather than leaving you to interpret raw swing points alone.

As shown in the chart above, the intent is to overlay a structural read on price so you are not mentally tracking every pivot yourself. That is the honest scope of what I can say here: it is a structure-and-setup lens on price, and it lives in the trend category for a reason.

## Where it fits in a workflow

The most sensible way to use a tool like this is as a filter and a frame, not a trigger. You establish the structural bias the indicator is painting — is price making progress in one direction, or chopping — and then you bring your own entry method to the table. If you trade pullbacks, the indicator's structure read tells you whether the pullback is happening inside a healthy trend or after a breakdown. If you trade breakouts, it tells you whether the level you are watching is a meaningful structural boundary or noise.

That division of labour is the whole point. Indicators that try to do structure *and* entries *and* exits tend to collapse into clutter. This one appears to stay closer to the structural side, which is the more durable half.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- It targets market structure directly, which is the part of price action that survives across timeframes and instruments. That makes it more adaptable than a tool hard-wired to one style.
- The trend-category placement suggests it is oriented toward directional context rather than counter-trend noise, which keeps the chart's message coherent.
- The "setup" framing implies it is meant to be used, not just admired — it is trying to bridge structure and decision-making rather than stopping at pretty swing labels.

**Cons**
- Market structure is inherently interpretive. Any indicator that claims to read it is making choices about what counts as a swing, what counts as a break, and where structure "resets." Two traders can look at the same chart and disagree. You will need to calibrate your own read against what the indicator shows, and there is no way around that learning curve.
- Because it is a discretionary aid rather than a mechanical signal, it will not hand you a clean list of trades. Traders who want alerts and arrows will find it underwhelming.
- Structure tools can lag at turning points by nature — a break of structure is only confirmed after the fact. Do not expect it to call tops and bottoms in advance.

## Who it is for

Discretionary price-action traders who already think in terms of swings and structure and want a second set of eyes on the chart. Swing traders and intraday traders who build a bias from structure and then execute with their own method will get the most from it. It is less suited to fully systematic traders, and it is probably the wrong tool for anyone hoping an indicator will replace the work of reading a chart.

## FAQ

**Does it generate buy and sell signals?**
It is framed around structure and setups, so treat it as a contextual tool. Whether it flags specific moments is something you should confirm on your own chart before relying on it.

**What timeframes does it work on?**
The source material does not specify. Market structure logic is generally timeframe-agnostic, but verify behaviour on the timeframe you actually trade.

**Can I use it for trend-following and mean-reversion?**
Its trend-category placement points toward directional context. Mean-reversion traders can still use structure to avoid fighting the dominant move, but that is a secondary use.

## Verdict

The Sight of Market Structure/Setup [JPT] earns a solid four stars for doing something more useful than most trend indicators: it addresses structure, the layer beneath the signal. It will not replace your judgement, and it should not be expected to. But as a structural frame that keeps you honest about what the market is actually doing, it is a reasonable addition for discretionary traders — provided you go in understanding it is an aid, not an oracle.
