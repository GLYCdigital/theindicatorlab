Most "Smart Money" indicators on TradingView are mood boards. They scatter labels around the chart, call every wick a liquidity sweep, and leave you to decide which of the twelve lines actually matters. TL Structure Pro takes the opposite approach — it tries to turn market structure into something mechanical. Break of Structure, Change of Character, liquidity sweeps: the standard SMC vocabulary, but presented as a defined engine rather than a pile of discretionary doodles.

Whether it succeeds is the question worth answering. Here's an honest breakdown.

## What this indicator actually does

The name tells you the scope: BOS, CHoCH, and sweeps. Those are the three structural events that SMC traders build their entire framework around. BOS marks a continuation of the prevailing trend when price breaks a prior structural high or low. CHoCH flags the first sign that the trend may be flipping — price failing to make a new extreme and instead breaking the last opposing swing. Sweeps capture the stop-hunt behaviour where price pushes past a level, grabs liquidity, and reverses.

TL Structure Pro's pitch is that it detects these events mechanically and plots them, so you're reading the chart's structure rather than drawing it by hand. That's the whole value proposition, and it's a legitimate one. For discretionary traders, structure is the most subjective thing on a chart — two people can look at the same swings and disagree about which one broke. An indicator that enforces a consistent definition removes that ambiguity.

## Where it earns its keep

The category is "Trend," and that framing is fair. This tool is fundamentally about trend state — is the structure intact, has it shifted, has it been violated by a sweep? If you trade with a structural bias, the plotted BOS and CHoCH events give you a readable map of where the trend confirmed itself and where it first showed weakness.

What separates this from the usual SMC clutter is the "mechanical" framing. It's presented as a rules-based engine rather than a pattern-recognition toy, which matters for anyone who wants to audit their own decisions. Instead of "the indicator looks bullish," you get a specific structural event at a specific location, and you can accept or reject it against your own read.

The sweep detection is the piece that most SMC indicators get wrong. Sweeps are genuinely hard to define — how far past a level counts? How much reversal is required? TL Structure Pro builds that into the engine, which at least gives you a consistent answer rather than eyeballed discretion.

## How you'd actually use it

This isn't a standalone signal generator and shouldn't be treated as one. The realistic workflow is structural context:

- Establish trend bias from the sequence of BOS events. A clean run of bullish BOS means the structure still favours longs.
- Treat CHoCH as a warning, not a trigger. It tells you the prior sequence broke — it doesn't tell you the new trend is confirmed.
- Watch sweeps around structural levels for the classic stop-hunt reversal setup, where a sweep immediately precedes a CHoCH.

Layer it with your existing entry method — order blocks, fair value gaps, support and resistance, whatever you already use. The indicator handles structure; you handle execution. If you're expecting it to hand you entries, you'll be disappointed, and that's a problem with your expectations, not the tool.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Enforces a consistent, mechanical definition of structure — removes the subjectivity that makes SMC trading hard to backtest or journal.
- Covers the three events that actually matter (BOS, CHoCH, sweeps) rather than flooding the chart with noise.
- The sweep logic is a real differentiator in a category full of vague wick-labeling.
- Fits cleanly into an existing SMC workflow as a context layer.

**Cons**
- It's a structure mapper, not a trading system. No entries, no exits, no risk management — you bring all of that.
- Mechanical structure detection is still structure detection: it can lag on fast moves and will pick up swings you might have classified differently by hand.
- If you don't already think in SMC terms, the labels won't teach you the framework — they'll just add clutter.
- The "engine" branding raises expectations of automation that the tool doesn't deliver.

## Who it's for

Traders who already work in the SMC framework and want to remove the guesswork from marking structure. If you've ever reviewed a trade and wondered whether you were honest about which swing actually broke, this is the tool that settles the argument. It's also useful for anyone journaling — a mechanical structure read is reproducible in a way a hand-drawn one isn't.

It's not for beginners looking for a shortcut. The concepts need to be understood before the labels mean anything, and there's no hand-holding here.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
No. It plots structural events — BOS, CHoCH, sweeps — that inform a decision. Execution is on you.

**Can I use it on any market?**
Structural logic applies to any liquid market, but the source material doesn't specify a best-fit environment, so don't assume one. Test it against the markets you actually trade.

**Is it a replacement for drawing structure manually?**
That depends on how closely your manual definitions align with the engine's. Treat it as a second opinion, not an authority.

## Verdict

TL Structure Pro does one job and does it with a clear philosophy: mechanical structure over discretionary squinting. That's worth four stars in a category where most tools are decorative. It loses a point only because "engine" oversells what's ultimately a very good structure mapper — and because its value is entirely dependent on the trader already understanding SMC. Bring the framework, use this for consistency, and it earns its place on the chart.
