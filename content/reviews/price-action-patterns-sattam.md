Most pattern indicators on TradingView fall into one of two camps: the ones that plaster the chart with labels until you can't see price itself, and the ones that quietly highlight structure you'd otherwise have to hunt for. Price Action Patterns Sattam sits in the trend category, and its stated job is the second kind — surfacing price action patterns as they form on your chart.

A caveat up front: there's no official documentation I can point to for this one. No published parameter list, no methodology write-up. So this review stays at the level of what the tool is for and how you'd actually work with it, rather than pretending to know its internals. If you need a settings-by-settings breakdown before installing anything, this isn't that — and honestly, anyone who gives you one without the source is guessing.

## What it does

Price action patterns are the old language of the chart: consolidations, breakouts, continuation and reversal structures that show up repeatedly because they reflect how buyers and sellers behave at certain price levels. Sattam's premise is to identify those structures and mark them for you, so you're not redrawing boxes and trendlines by hand every session.

Because it's filed under trend, the emphasis is presumably on structures that matter for directional bias — the formations you'd use to decide whether to be long, short, or flat. That framing is useful: pattern recognition without a trend context is just decoration.

## How you'd actually use it

The sensible workflow is to treat it as a second pair of eyes, not a signal generator. Pull it onto a chart you already know well, compare what it flags against what you'd have marked yourself, and see where you agree. Where you disagree, you learn something — either about the indicator's sensitivity or about your own bias.

From there, it belongs in a confirmation role. If you're watching a level and the indicator flags a pattern forming around it, that's a prompt to look closer, not a reason to click buy. Patterns fail constantly; the tool can only tell you a shape exists, not that it will resolve the way you hope.

## Pros

- **Concept-first design.** It's built around price action, which means it's grounded in the same structures discretionary traders already use — no black-box math to trust blindly.
- **Fits a discretionary process.** It slots into how a manual trader already works rather than demanding you adopt a new system.
- **Trend-category framing.** Keeping pattern detection tied to trend context filters out a lot of noise that pure "pattern scanner" tools generate.
- **Low commitment.** It's a chart overlay, so you can evaluate it against your own markings without changing anything else in your setup.

## Cons

- **No public documentation.** You're reverse-engineering the logic from what it draws. That's a real limitation for anyone who wants to understand *why* a pattern was flagged.
- **Pattern tools are inherently subjective.** Two traders can disagree on whether a formation qualifies. An indicator has to commit to a definition, and that definition may not match yours.
- **Repainting risk is unknown.** Without documentation, you can't confirm whether patterns appear only after confirmation or shift as price develops. Assume nothing until you've watched it live for a while.
- **No performance claims to lean on.** And that's fine — no pattern indicator should be trusted on the strength of a win-rate screenshot anyway.

## Who it's for

Discretionary price action traders who already read structure and want a systematic cross-check. Swing traders working off chart patterns on higher timeframes are the natural fit. If you're a pure mechanical/systematic trader looking for entry signals with defined rules, this isn't aimed at you — and if you're brand new to price action, learn the patterns by hand first, or you'll have no basis for judging whether the tool is right.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
It identifies patterns. Turning a pattern into a trade — entry, stop, target — is your job.

**Will it work on any market?**
Price action structure exists in any liquid market, so the concept travels. Whether the implementation suits your instrument is something you'd have to judge on your own charts.

**Can I use it with other indicators?**
Yes — it's a chart overlay, so it coexists with oscillators, moving averages, or whatever else you run. Pair it with a momentum tool and you get structure plus confirmation.

**Is it a complete trading system?**
No. It's one input in a process. Treating it as a system is how people lose money with pattern tools.

## Verdict

Price Action Patterns Sattam does the unglamorous work: flagging price action structure so you don't have to redraw it every session. The concept is sound, the category is right, and it respects the way discretionary traders actually operate. The missing documentation is the main thing holding it back — you can't fully audit what you don't understand, and that matters for anything you're going to lean on in live decisions. Four stars: genuinely useful for the right trader, with a transparency gap worth knowing about before you install it.
