Most trend indicators on TradingView answer the same question: which way is price leaning? Almazov Trade: Structure takes a different angle. It belongs to the trend category, but its premise is structural — reading the skeleton of the market (how swings and momentum are arranged) rather than printing a simple directional arrow. If you've ever felt that a moving average crossover told you the trend but not the *context*, this is the gap the indicator is trying to fill.

One important note up front: the available source material for this indicator is thin. There is no published description or documentation to draw from, so this review sticks to what the concept does and what it's suited for. I won't quote default settings, thresholds or performance figures, because I don't have them — and neither should you accept them from anyone who hasn't published them.

## What it actually is

The name is the tell. "Structure" is the operative word. Where a plain trend tool flattens the market into up/down/neutral, a structure-based trend tool tries to show the *shape* of the move — the way higher highs and higher lows (or the bearish inverse) are forming, and how that arrangement holds up or breaks. Placed in the trend category, it's meant to be your directional compass, but one that accounts for how price got to where it is.

As shown in the chart above, the indicator overlays structural information directly on price, which keeps your eyes on the same panel rather than bouncing between a subwindow oscillator and the candles. That layout matters more than it sounds: structure is a *price* concept, so it belongs next to price.

## How you'd actually use it

The honest workflow here is confirmation, not prediction. A few sensible ways to apply a structural trend tool:

- **Trend context first.** Before taking a setup, check whether the structure agrees with your bias. If it doesn't, your "pullback entry" may be a reversal in disguise.
- **Break-of-structure awareness.** Structural tools are often most useful at the moments structure *changes* — that's where trend continuation bets get invalidated and where disciplined traders reduce risk.
- **Combine, don't replace.** Structure tells you the *where*; your entry trigger (volume, momentum, a candlestick pattern) still tells you the *when*.

What I'd avoid is treating it as a standalone signal generator. Structural reads are inherently interpretive, and an indicator that draws structure on your chart is a lens, not an oracle. You still have to make the judgment call.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Fills a real gap. Plenty of indicators tell you *that* there's a trend; far fewer help you read *how* it's built.
- Keeps structure on the price panel, which suits discretionary traders better than a separate oscillator.
- Conceptually compatible with multiple styles — swing, intraday, position — because market structure is timeframe-agnostic in principle.

**Cons**
- Structure is interpretive by nature, so two traders can read the same chart differently. The indicator narrows the debate but doesn't eliminate it.
- Without published documentation, new users will spend time figuring out how it defines a swing or a break. That's a learning-curve tax.
- It's a trend-category tool, so expect it to lag at genuine turning points — that's true of nearly everything in this class, not a knock specific to this one.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who think in terms of market structure — swing traders mapping higher highs and lows, intraday traders who want trend context before executing, and anyone tired of crossover tools that feel disconnected from price action. It's less suited to pure mechanical/system traders who need fully specified, rule-based signals they can code and backtest themselves.

## FAQ

**Does it repaint?** I can't confirm this from the source material. Treat any structural tool as potentially recalculating until you've verified behaviour on a live chart yourself.

**Which timeframes work best?** Not documented, and I won't guess. Structure concepts apply across timeframes, but test on the ones you actually trade.

**Is it a buy/sell signal indicator?** Based on its category and name, it's a trend/structure tool, not a signal generator. Use it for context.

## Verdict

Almazov Trade: Structure does something genuinely useful: it treats trend as a structure to be read rather than a line to be crossed. That's the right instinct, and for discretionary traders who already think this way, it's a natural fit on the chart. The lack of public documentation holds it back from a higher score — you're partly buying a concept you have to reverse-engineer — but the underlying idea is sound and the category placement is honest.

**Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)** — a solid structural trend tool for traders who want context, not hand-holding.
