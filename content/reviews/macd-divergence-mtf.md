# Macd_Divergence_Mtf Review

Macd_Divergence_Mtf is a divergence scanner that checks multiple timeframes simultaneously and plots the results on your current chart. The concept is straightforward; the execution is what determines whether it's useful.

## What This Indicator Actually Does

MACD divergence is a widely used reversal signal. The practical problem is that manually scanning several timeframes is slow and setups get missed. This tool automates that process: it calculates MACD on your chosen higher timeframes while you remain on a lower-timeframe chart, then plots divergence arrows directly on that lower timeframe.

The arrows appear at the price level where the divergence forms, so there's no need to switch charts or monitor multiple tabs.

## Key Features That Matter

**Multi-timeframe flexibility.** The indicator supports several distinct timeframes in addition to your current one. You set which higher timeframes are relevant to your strategy, so day traders and swing traders will configure it differently.

**Clean visual output.** Divergence arrows come in two types — regular and hidden — plotted with different colors. The labels are sized to remain visible without overwhelming the chart, so price action stays readable.

**Alert functionality.** Alerts can be set for new divergence signals, which is where the tool provides most of its practical value, since it lets you monitor higher-timeframe signals while working on a lower timeframe.

**Source customization.** The indicator can be applied to close, open, high, low, or typical price. Most traders leave it on close, but those trading reversals off wicks may prefer high/low to filter noise.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

The default MACD parameters are standard and generally don't need adjustment.

For the MTF configuration, the sensible approach depends on your style:

- **Day trading on a low timeframe:** set higher timeframes to short, medium, and intermediate intervals so you capture short-term momentum shifts alongside the broader picture. Very slow timeframes add little for intraday moves.
- **Swing trading:** use higher intervals that filter out minor pullbacks and surface only meaningful reversals.
- **Pivot strength:** the indicator uses pivot points to confirm divergence. A longer pivot lookback tends to reduce false signals but introduces more lag; a shorter lookback reacts earlier at the cost of more duplicates. The right balance depends on the instrument and timeframe, so it's worth testing on your own pair.

## How to Trade With It

The indicator provides a signal, not a strategy. A workable workflow:

1. **Wait for divergence to form on a higher timeframe.** A bearish regular divergence on a higher timeframe serves as a warning.
2. **Drop to the lower timeframe for confirmation** — a trendline break, a lower high structure, or a bearish candle pattern.
3. **Enter on the lower timeframe with a stop beyond the swing high.** Target the previous support level or a retracement of the recent swing.
4. **Hidden divergence as a trend-continuation play.** Hidden bullish divergence on a higher timeframe during an uptrend can be used to add to existing positions on pullbacks rather than opening fresh ones.

The key point: don't take every arrow. Divergence signals used blindly, without higher-timeframe trend context or confirmation, tend to produce poor results.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**
- Saves time scanning multiple timeframes
- Alerts for new divergence signals
- Customizable enough for different trading styles
- Clean plot that doesn't obscure price action

**Cons:**
- Divergence arrows depend on pivot detection, which can repaint as new bars form. A signal that looks confirmed can disappear later. This is the biggest drawback.
- No built-in trend filter. It needs to be combined with something like a moving average or market structure to avoid fading strong trends.
- Can produce an overwhelming number of signals if too many timeframes are enabled.

## Who This Is For

Day traders and swing traders who already use divergence in their playbook. If you're manually checking higher-timeframe MACD while trading a lower timeframe, this will save time. Scalpers on very fast charts won't find much value — the indicator needs higher-timeframe structure to work.

Newer traders should approach with caution. Divergence without context is a quick way to lose money. The indicator makes finding signals easier, but it doesn't teach you when those signals are valid.

## Alternatives Worth Considering

If you want a more complete package, consider **Rsi Divergence Scanner** or **Awesome Divergence** — they use different momentum oscillators. The MACD version catches slower, more significant shifts, while RSI divergence tends to fire more often. If you want trend context built-in, look for a divergence indicator that includes a moving average filter. For pure simplicity, TradingView's built-in MACD with the divergence feature enabled does the job if you're only trading one timeframe.

## FAQ

**Does Macd_Divergence_Mtf repaint?**
Yes. The arrows are based on pivot confirmations, which means a signal can disappear if the pivot gets invalidated by new price action. Wait for the bar to close before acting.

**Can I use it for crypto?**
Yes. Crypto's volatility produces plenty of divergence signals — sometimes too many. Higher timeframes tend to reduce noise.

**Does it work in real-time or only on closed bars?**
It calculates on closed bars, which means alerts arrive shortly after a timeframe closes. This is fine for swing trading but not for tick-level precision.

**Do I need to buy multiple copies for different timeframes?**
No. That's the point of the MTF feature. One chart, multiple timeframe calculations.

## Final Verdict

Macd_Divergence_Mtf does what it promises: it finds MACD divergence across multiple timeframes without requiring you to switch charts. It isn't a complete trading system — you'll still need your own entry criteria and risk management. But as a scanner for a widely used reversal signal, it's efficient and well-built.

The repainting issue and the lack of a built-in trend filter are the main drawbacks. If those don't bother you, this is a solid addition to a divergence toolkit.
