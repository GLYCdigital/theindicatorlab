Most "ICT" indicators on TradingView are really just a pile of line-drawing shortcuts with a smart-sounding name slapped on top. This one is narrower than that, and that's the point. ICT Setup 03 is built around a single narrative: the New York open produces a false push (the Judas Swing), price then shifts character, and the resulting fair value gap is the trade. The indicator tries to make that sequence readable on the chart instead of leaving you to eyeball it.

The source material for this one is thin — there's no published parameter list I can verify — so I'm going to describe what the tool is *for* and how the concept works, not pretend I know its exact internals. Where I'd normally quote a default, I'll leave it out.

## What it's actually doing

The name is doing a lot of work here, so let's unpack it. Three things are being combined:

- **The Judas Swing** — the idea that the session open can bait traders with a move in the wrong direction before the real move develops. In an ICT framework, that initial push is treated as a liquidity grab, not a signal.
- **CHoCH (change of character)** — a structural shift where the market stops making the kind of swing it was making and starts making the opposite kind. It's the confirmation layer.
- **FVG (fair value gap)** — an imbalance left behind by an aggressive move, where price skipped over a zone. It's the entry layer.

The indicator's job is to tie these together around the New York morning window. Conceptually that's a clean, defensible model: bait, shift, imbalance. Whether the chart does it cleanly is the whole question, and that's what you're judging.

## Where it earns its keep

The real value of a tool like this isn't that it finds setups you couldn't find yourself — it's that it forces a checklist on you. Judas Swing without a CHoCH is just a guess. A CHoCH without an FVG behind it is a weaker entry with worse risk placement. By framing all three together, the indicator discourages the classic discretionary mistake of seeing one ingredient and calling it a trade.

It also keeps you honest about *when*. Anchoring the logic to the New York open window means you're not scanning for ICT patterns at 3am on a Tuesday, finding something that looks vaguely like a gap, and talking yourself into it. That constraint is a feature.

As shown in the chart above, the output is a trend-context tool — it's telling you something about structure and direction, not firing a signal you blindly follow.

## How most people will use it

The sane workflow is top-down: let the indicator flag the session behaviour and the structural shift, then treat the FVG as the *area* to watch rather than a magic entry price. You still decide your stop placement, your target, and whether the higher-timeframe context even supports a trade. The indicator narrows your attention; it doesn't make the decision.

One thing worth being blunt about: this is a discretionary framework with assistance, not a mechanical system. If you're looking for a green arrow that prints a win rate, this isn't it, and nothing in its design suggests it's trying to be.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Combines three genuinely complementary concepts instead of dumping them on the chart separately.
- The session anchor gives it a discipline that most ICT tools lack.
- Narrow scope — it does one thing rather than pretending to be a full trading suite.

**Cons**
- ICT concepts are inherently interpretive. Where exactly the CHoCH "counts" and which gap is the *right* gap will vary by trader, and the indicator can't settle that argument for you.
- Following a session-specific model means long stretches where it's quiet. Traders who want constant activity will get bored and start overriding it.
- Without documented, transparent parameters, you're partly trusting a black box's reading of structure.

## Who it's for

Discretionary intraday traders who already think in ICT terms and want a structured, session-aware layer on top of that. It's also useful for traders *learning* the Judas Swing → CHoCH → FVG sequence, because seeing the three stages mapped together teaches the logic faster than reading about it. It is not for mechanical system traders, swing traders holding for weeks, or anyone who wants a signal service.

## FAQ

**Does it work on any market?**
The concept is built around the New York session open, so it's inherently an intraday, session-driven tool. Applying it to instruments with no meaningful NY participation defeats the premise.

**Can I use it without knowing ICT theory?**
You can read the markings, but you'll misread them. The indicator labels structure; it doesn't explain why the structure matters. The concepts do the heavy lifting.

**Is it a buy/sell signal indicator?**
No. It highlights a setup *context* — the swing, the shift, the gap. Execution is on you.

## Verdict

For what it claims to be — a focused, session-anchored ICT setup mapper — this is a solid piece of work. The concept is coherent, the scope is disciplined, and it avoids the bloat that ruins most indicators in this genre. I'm docking a star because the interpretive grey areas and the lack of transparent, documented parameters mean you're trusting its read of structure more than you might like. If you're an ICT-leaning intraday trader, it's worth a look. If you're not, it won't convert you.
