Two of the most widely used momentum tools in technical analysis, stacked into a single script. That's the concept here: an indicator that brings RSI and MACD together rather than forcing you to load and read them as separate panes. The idea is straightforward — combine a momentum oscillator with a trend-and-momentum crossover tool so you're making one read instead of juggling two.

As shown in the chart above, the result is a momentum-focused pane that leans on both signals at once. If you already know what RSI and MACD do individually, you already understand the raw ingredients. What you're really evaluating is whether combining them helps or clutters your process.

## What it actually does

RSI (Relative Strength Index) measures the speed and magnitude of price changes and is typically used to flag overbought or oversold conditions. MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence) tracks the relationship between two moving averages and is used for trend direction and momentum shifts, usually read through its line, signal line and histogram. This indicator folds both into a single view.

The appeal is confirmation. RSI tells you about momentum strength; MACD tells you about momentum direction and trend. When the two agree, you arguably have a cleaner read than either alone. When they disagree, you get a visible warning that the move may be less reliable than it looks. That's the entire thesis, and it's a reasonable one.

## Key features

The core feature is consolidation — one pane, two oscillators, less chart real estate consumed. That matters if you run a tight layout or trade on smaller screens. Beyond that, the value is in the pairing: momentum extremes (RSI) read alongside trend momentum (MACD) rather than in isolation.

Because I have no documented parameter list for this specific build, I won't quote lengths, periods or thresholds. The standard RSI and MACD inputs are what you'd expect to tune, and how sensitive the combined read is will depend on how you set those up.

## How to use it

Think of it as a filter, not a signal generator. A common workflow:

- Use MACD to establish momentum direction — is the trend pushing up or fading?
- Use RSI to gauge whether that momentum is stretched or has room.
- Look for agreement between the two before acting; treat disagreement as a reason to wait.

It's most useful as a confirmation layer on top of your existing price-action read, not as a standalone entry trigger. No oscillator is a strategy by itself, and combining two doesn't change that.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Fewer panes to manage; both momentum views in one place
- Built-in cross-check — two signals that can confirm or contradict each other
- Familiar building blocks, so there's little learning curve if you know RSI and MACD

**Cons**
- Combining oscillators can create analysis paralysis when they disagree
- It's a mashup, not a new methodology — you're still reading two classic tools
- Overlapping momentum signals can feel redundant rather than additive on trending charts

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already use RSI or MACD and want a tidier single-pane read. It suits swing and intraday traders who value confirmation over speed. Beginners may find two oscillators in one pane more confusing than one — start with a single tool first.

## FAQ

**Is this better than RSI or MACD alone?**
Not inherently. It gives you both signals together; whether that's better depends on how you use confirmation.

**Does it generate buy/sell signals?**
It's a momentum indicator built on RSI and MACD logic. Treat its output as context, not a trading system.

**What timeframes does it suit?**
Undocumented here. Momentum oscillators generally behave differently across timeframes — test it on your own charts.

## Verdict

A sensible, low-friction combination of two proven momentum tools. It doesn't reinvent anything, and that's fine — the value is consolidation and cross-confirmation. If you already trade with RSI or MACD and want them in one pane, it earns its place. If you're hoping a mashup will produce a magic signal, it won't.
