Supply and demand zones are one of those ideas that sound simple and get messy fast. Marking a zone is easy. Keeping it relevant as price moves, trends shift, and old levels get retested is the hard part. "Ghost Zones Gti Adaptive Demand Supply Zones" is a TradingView tool built around that problem: it plots demand and supply zones and adapts them to changing market structure, so the levels on your chart stay tied to current price action instead of piling up into clutter.

As shown in the chart above, the indicator overlays zone regions directly on price, giving you a visual read of where buying and selling pressure has historically stepped in.

## What it actually does

At its core, this is a structural tool. It identifies areas where price has reacted — the classic demand zone (where buyers defended) and supply zone (where sellers capped a move) — and draws them as shaded regions. The "adaptive" part is the differentiator: rather than leaving every zone static forever, the indicator adjusts zones as the trend evolves. That matters because a demand zone that was valid three swings ago is often noise by the time price returns to it.

Because it sits in the Trend category, the intent is to combine zone logic with directional context. You're not just looking at isolated rectangles; you're reading where price is likely to find support or resistance within an ongoing move.

## How to use it

The practical workflow is straightforward:

1. Add it to your chart and let it plot the current zone map.
2. Identify the nearest demand zone below price and supply zone above it — these define your risk/reward framing.
3. In an uptrend, demand zones are candidate entries; in a downtrend, supply zones are. The adaptive behaviour helps you avoid trading zones that no longer align with structure.
4. Use the zones as confluences with your existing entry trigger, not as standalone signals.

The honest caveat: zone indicators tell you *where* something might happen, not *when*. Pair it with a trigger — a candlestick reaction, a momentum shift, a break-and-retest — or you'll be entering on hope.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Adaptive zones reduce the clutter problem that plagues static supply/demand tools.
- Visual and immediate — no reading required, the chart tells you where structure sits.
- Works naturally alongside trend-following or pullback strategies.
- Fits a discretionary workflow rather than forcing a rigid signal.

**Cons**
- Zones are inherently subjective; even adaptive ones won't match how every trader would draw them.
- Without a defined entry trigger, it can encourage premature positioning.
- Zone-based tools tend to perform differently across timeframes, and there's no universal "right" setting.
- If you already trade pure price action, the added overlay may be redundant.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who think in terms of structure and want a cleaner way to track supply and demand as a trend develops. Pullback and continuation traders will get the most out of it. If you're a purely mechanical, signal-driven trader, this won't replace your system — it's a context tool.

## FAQ

**Is it a buy/sell signal indicator?**
No. It maps zones. You supply the decision logic.

**Does it repaint?**
Adaptive zone tools adjust as structure changes, so expect zones to update as new price action forms. Treat zones as dynamic, not fixed.

**Which timeframes work best?**
That depends on your strategy and the instrument. Test it against your own approach rather than assuming a universal answer.

**Can I use it with other indicators?**
Yes — it's designed to complement, not replace, your existing toolkit.

## Final verdict

Ghost Zones Gti Adaptive Demand Supply Zones does one job and does it with a sensible twist: it keeps supply and demand levels relevant instead of letting them rot on the chart. It won't hand you entries, and it demands that you bring your own trigger and risk plan. But as a structural overlay for trend traders, it's a genuinely useful addition — clean, adaptive, and honest about what it is.

**Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)** — a solid structural tool for discretionary traders; docked a star only because it's context, not a complete system.
