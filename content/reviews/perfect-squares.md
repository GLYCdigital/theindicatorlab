**Perfect_Squares** is not another moving average or RSI clone. It's a geometric tool that projects potential reversal zones based on the square-of-9 theory—think Gann meets modern charting.

---

### What This Indicator Actually Does

Perfect_Squares draws horizontal lines (or shaded zones) at price levels derived from squaring key highs, lows, or current price. The input lets you choose the "root" (starting value) and step increments. The idea: markets often react at levels that are perfect squares of previous price action—like 100, 121, 144, etc.

It's **not** a predictive oracle. It's a **reference grid**—like having Fibonacci levels but built on a different math.

---

### Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Custom root selection**: You can pin it to a swing high, low, or current price.
- **Step size control**: Adjustable step increments, with a default of 1.
- **Zone vs. line display**: Zones suit stop placement; lines are cleaner for entry signals.
- **Alert capability**: It can trigger alerts when price touches a square level. Handy for pending orders.

---

### Settings and How to Tune Them

- **Root**: Set to a swing low for support zones, or a swing high for resistance. An "Auto" mode also exists that lets the root change dynamically.
- **Step**: Default is 1. Lower steps add more levels and clutter the chart.
- **Display**: Lines or zones, depending on whether you want active-trading clarity or a band for holding through swings.
- **Timeframe**: Higher timeframes are generally preferred; lower timeframes tend to produce more false touches and noise.

---

### How to Use It for Entries and Exits

**Entry**: Wait for price to touch a square level with a candlestick confirmation (e.g., bullish engulfing at a square support). Do **not** buy the line blindly—use it as a trigger, not a reason.

**Exit**: Take partial profits at the next square level above. Trail stops to the previous square.

**Stop-loss**: Place stops below the square level. The zones are not exact—price can wick through.

---

### Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros**:
- Unique—not another lagging indicator. It gives you levels before price reaches them.
- Works well alongside trendlines and Fibonacci. Overlay it and look for confluence.
- Clean, non-intrusive visual.

**Cons**:
- **Not for beginners**. Without an understanding of square-of-9 theory, this will seem like random lines.
- **False breaks** are common on lower timeframes. It needs a filter (e.g., RSI divergence or volume spike).
- No built-in multi-timeframe analysis. You have to add it manually.

---

### Who Is This Actually For?

- **Swing traders** who want geometric support/resistance.
- **Gann enthusiasts** who already respect square theory.
- **Any trader** who uses Fibonacci but wants a second opinion tool.

It's **not** for scalpers or news traders. Those need raw price action, not math.

---

### Better Alternatives

If you want the same concept but more developed: **Gann Box** or **Square of 9 by LonesomeTheBlue**. They're fancier but also more complex. Perfect_Squares is the stripped-down, no-BS version.

---

### FAQ

**Q: Does it repaint?**  
A: The levels are static once set. Only the root can change if you use "Auto" mode.

**Q: Can I use it on crypto?**  
A: Yes, but it works better on higher timeframes. Crypto is too volatile for lower-timeframe squares.

**Q: What's the best pair?**  
A: EUR/USD and S&P 500 futures are commonly cited as respecting geometric levels more than erratic assets.

### Final Verdict

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)** — Perfect_Squares is a solid, free tool for swing traders who want a different perspective on support and resistance. It's not magic, but it adds a mathematical layer that most indicators lack. It loses a star because it requires manual interpretation and doesn't filter false moves. If you're willing to pair it with price action, it's a keeper.
