Trendlines are one of those things every trader draws and almost nobody draws the same way twice. Auto Trendlines [ITA] takes that repetitive, subjective job and hands it to an algorithm. The pitch is simple: instead of manually connecting swing highs and lows, the indicator identifies them for you and plots the lines on the chart. This review covers what that actually means in practice — and where the tool stops short.

A quick note on sourcing: the public documentation for this indicator is thin, so I'm keeping specifics to what the concept itself implies rather than inventing settings or numbers. That keeps this honest, if a little shorter than a typical deep-dive.

## What it actually does

At its core, Auto Trendlines [ITA] automates a task that most discretionary traders already do by hand. It scans price action, picks out the pivots that matter, and connects them into trendlines. The lines update as new data comes in, so you're not redrawing support and resistance every time the market prints a new high or low.

That's the whole idea. It's not a signal generator in the buy/sell arrow sense — it's a chart annotation engine. The value is in consistency and time saved, not in telling you what to do next. If you've ever stared at a chart and wondered whether a trendline should connect to the wick or the body, you already understand the appeal of letting a rule-based process decide.

## The honest case for it

The strongest argument for an automated trendline tool is objectivity. Manual trendlines are notoriously prone to hindsight bias — you see what you want to see, and you adjust the line until it "works." An algorithm doesn't care about your position. It applies the same logic to every chart, every time. That consistency is worth something, especially if you're trying to build a repeatable process or review your own past decisions.

The second argument is speed. On a fast timeframe with a lot of price action, redrawing lines manually is tedious. Having them appear automatically frees your attention for the actual decision-making.

As shown in the chart above, the effect is a cleaner, less cluttered view of structure than most hand-drawn setups — assuming you trust the pivots the tool selects.

## Where it gets complicated

The trade-off is control. When you draw a trendline yourself, you're encoding judgment — this wick matters, that one doesn't, this touch is valid, that one's noise. An automated tool has to make those calls with a fixed rule. Sometimes that rule will disagree with your read of the chart, and you'll be left either overriding the line or trusting it blindly. Neither is ideal.

There's also the question of what a trendline *means* once it's automated. A line that's been touched five times carries psychological weight for traders who drew it; a line an indicator generated carries less. You're borrowing structure, not creating it. That's fine as a starting point, but it's not the same as the shared reference points that manual trendlines often become.

## How I'd approach using it

The sensible workflow is to treat the indicator as a first pass, not a final answer. Let it draw the lines, then apply your own judgment about which ones are worth respecting. Use it to spot structure you might have missed on a crowded chart, or to sanity-check your own manual drawings. Don't outsource the decision entirely.

It also pairs naturally with other trend tools — moving averages, momentum readings, or price action patterns — because trendlines on their own never tell you *when* to act, only *where* structure sits.

## Who it's for

- **Discretionary traders** who draw trendlines regularly and want a faster starting point.
- **Beginners** still learning to identify swing structure, for whom a visual guide is genuinely useful.
- **Systematic traders** looking for a consistent structural overlay to combine with other signals.

It's less useful for anyone expecting standalone trade signals, and probably redundant for traders who already have a rigid, rule-based structure method they trust.

## FAQ

**Does it generate buy and sell signals?**
No. It's a chart annotation tool that plots trendlines. Any trade decision is still yours.

**Can I use it on any market or timeframe?**
The concept applies to any price chart, but how well the auto-detected pivots match your own read will vary. There's no documented "best" setting, so expect some adjustment.

**Will it replace manual trendline drawing?**
It can supplement it. Whether it replaces it depends on how much you trust the algorithm's pivot selection over your own judgment.

## Verdict

Auto Trendlines [ITA] does one job and does it cleanly: it takes the tedium and inconsistency out of drawing trendlines. It won't tell you what to trade, and it won't always agree with your eye — but as a structural overlay and a time-saver, it earns its place. The lack of clear public documentation is a real weakness, and the tool's value depends heavily on how much you trust automated pivot detection. For traders who lean on trendlines, it's a sensible addition. For everyone else, it's a nice-to-have rather than a must.
