Some indicators try to be everything — momentum, volume, volatility, sentiment — and end up as a wall of lines nobody can read. The Supreme BUY/SELL Signal Indicator by Chaitu50c takes a narrower path. It's a trend tool that exists to do one thing: mark potential long and short entries on your chart when the prevailing direction shifts.

That focus is the whole pitch. There's no hidden second product here, no kitchen-sink dashboard. It flags directional changes and lets you decide what to do with them. Whether that's enough to earn a slot on your chart is the question worth answering.

## What it actually is

Strip away the "Supreme" branding and you have a standard trend-following signal indicator. It generates buy and sell markers that appear when its internal trend logic flips from one side to the other. The signals show up on the chart as visual cues, not as a separate sub-panel readout you have to interpret line-by-line.

If you've used any trend-flip indicator on TradingView, the experience will feel familiar. The value of a tool like this is rarely in the underlying concept — most trend followers are variations on the same idea — it's in how cleanly the signals are presented and how much noise they filter out. On that front, the Supreme indicator keeps things visually simple.

## How you'd actually use it

The workflow is straightforward. You load it onto a chart, it plots buy and sell markers as trend direction changes, and you treat those markers as a prompt to look closer rather than an instruction to click.

That distinction matters. No signal indicator — this one included — tells you where to place a stop, how to size a position, or whether the broader market backdrop supports the trade. The marker is the start of your analysis, not the end of it. A sensible process looks like this: signal fires, you check the higher timeframe trend, you confirm price isn't sitting directly into obvious resistance or support, and only then do you consider an entry.

Because the tool follows trend, it will by design lag the exact turn. That's the trade-off every trend follower makes — you accept entering a little late in exchange for not fighting the direction.

## Where it earns its keep

The clearest strength is simplicity. A trend indicator that keeps its signals readable is doing its job. You're not decoding color-coded histograms or reconciling three sub-panels that disagree with each other. Buy is buy, sell is sell.

That makes it approachable for traders who want an objective directional cue without a steep learning curve. It also pairs naturally with manual price-action work — you can use the markers as a timing aid on top of your own structure reading, rather than replacing it.

## Where it falls short

The honest limitation is the same one that applies to every trend-flip signal: choppy, range-bound conditions will produce whipsaws. When price oscillates without a clear direction, a trend indicator flips back and forth, and each flip looks like a signal. That's not a flaw unique to this tool, but it's a real cost you need to plan around.

There's also the matter of documentation. The concept is presented simply, but that means you're leaning on the signals themselves rather than a detailed explanation of the internal logic. Traders who want to understand exactly what drives each flip may find it opaque.

And like all signal tools, it carries no edge on its own. It's a lens, not a strategy.

## Who it's for

This suits discretionary traders who already read price structure and want a clean directional prompt to confirm their bias. It's also reasonable for swing traders working on higher timeframes, where trend signals tend to be less noisy than on fast intraday charts.

It's less suited to scalpers chasing every tick, or to anyone expecting the indicator to function as a complete trading system. If you want a tool that tells you exactly when to enter and exit with no judgment on your part, this isn't it — and neither is anything else.

## FAQ

**Does it repaint?**
No source documentation confirms repainting behavior, so treat any signal as provisional until the bar closes. That's sound practice for any trend indicator regardless.

**Can I use it on any market?**
It's a general trend tool, so it applies to any instrument with directional price movement. Whether a given market suits it depends on how much it trends versus ranges.

**Does it give stop-loss or target levels?**
No. It marks directional signals only. Risk management is on you.

## Verdict

The Supreme BUY/SELL Signal Indicator by Chaitu50c does a focused job. It plots clear trend-flip signals without clutter, and for traders who want an uncomplicated directional cue to layer onto their own analysis, that's genuinely useful. It won't hand you a strategy, and it will whipsaw in chop like every trend follower. But judged as what it is — a clean, simple signal tool — it holds up.
