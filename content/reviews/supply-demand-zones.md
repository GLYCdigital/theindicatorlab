## What This Indicator Actually Does

Supply Demand Zones automatically identifies areas where price previously reversed sharply—what most traders call "support and resistance on steroids." It draws rectangular boxes around these zones based on aggressive price moves away from a range. The idea is simple: price left these zones fast, so it may react there again.

The zones it draws are clean—no overlapping clutter like some other scripts. The indicator highlights zones with a slight transparency, making them easy to spot without hiding price action.

---

## Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Auto-zone detection** – No manual drawing. The indicator finds both supply (sell) and demand (buy) zones based on swing highs/lows and momentum.
- **Zone freshness labels** – It marks zones as "Fresh" (untested) or "Tested." Fresh zones are more reliable; tested ones get weaker.
- **Customizable lookback** – You can adjust the sensitivity to control how many zones are drawn.
- **Drop-down zone filtering** – You can disable old zones or limit how many are shown. Keeps the chart readable.

---

## Settings and How to Tune Them

The indicator exposes a handful of inputs worth understanding before you rely on it:

- **Zone detection sensitivity:** Controls how aggressive the zone detection is. Lower values catch more minor moves; higher values filter down to stronger impulses.
- **Max zones shown:** Caps how many zones stay on the chart. Too many and the chart becomes unreadable.
- **Zone extension:** Controls how far zones are projected to the right, giving price a buffer to wick into the zone before reversing.
- **Color scheme:** Typically green for demand, red for supply, with transparency so candlestick patterns remain visible inside the zone.

Because crypto and equity instruments behave differently, expect to adjust sensitivity per market rather than using one fixed value everywhere.

## How to Use It for Entries and Exits

Don't just buy at the zone line—wait for confirmation.

**Entry setup (long example):**
1. Price enters a fresh demand zone.
2. Wait for a bullish rejection candle (long wick, close near high).
3. Enter on the next candle's break above that rejection candle's high.
4. Stop loss below the zone's lowest point.

**Exit strategy:**
- Take partial profits at the nearest supply zone (or a fixed risk-to-reward target).
- Move stop to breakeven after price moves one zone width away.

**The trap:** Don't fade every zone. Fresh zones with a strong prior impulse move work best. Old, tested zones get chopped up—skip those.

## Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Saves hours of manual drawing. Zones are consistent, not subjective.
- Fresh zone labels are genuinely useful—untested zones have a higher probability of holding.
- Works across timeframes, from intraday up through daily charts.

**Cons:**
- Can't adjust zone strength based on volume or order flow. It's purely price-based.
- Sometimes draws zones on minor swings that aren't significant, so some discretion is required.
- No multi-timeframe alignment built in. You have to add the indicator to each chart manually.

## Who It's Actually For

- **Swing traders** who want a quick, consistent way to map key levels.
- **Beginners** who struggle with drawing zones consistently.
- **Not for** pure price action purists who prefer manual analysis—this tool is a shortcut, not a replacement.

## Better Alternatives If They Exist

If you want more granularity, look at **Order Flow Imbalance** or **Volume Profile** indicators. They show supply/demand based on traded volume, not just price moves. But for a simple, clean zone-drawing tool, this is one of the better options on TradingView.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it repaint?**
Once a zone is drawn, it stays. The "fresh" label updates to "tested" when price revisits, but the zone itself doesn't move.

**Q: Can I use it for crypto?**
Yes—it works on BTC/ETH, though crypto moves are choppier, so expect to raise sensitivity to filter out noise.

**Q: How many zones should I keep visible?**
Keep the count low. Too many zones on the chart and you're just guessing.

**Q: What timeframe is best?**
Higher timeframes for swing trading, lower intraday timeframes for scalping. Very low timeframes tend to produce too many false zones.
