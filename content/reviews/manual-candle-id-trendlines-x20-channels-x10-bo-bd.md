Most trend indicators want to think for you. This one doesn't. The name says it plainly: **Manual Candle ID**, then trendlines, channels, and breakout/breakdown markers. This is a drawing-first workspace, not a signal generator that taps you on the shoulder. The value is in the workflow, not a magic number.

## What it actually is

Strip away the long title and you get a manual annotation tool for trend work. The "Candle ID" part suggests you're identifying candles yourself — reading the structure bar by bar — rather than letting an algorithm tag swings for you. The trendlines and channels are the scaffolding you build on top of that read, and the breakout/breakdown (BO/BD) markers are how you flag the moment price leaves the structure you drew.

The "x20" and "x10" in the name read like capacity hints: room for a set of trendlines and a set of channels on a single chart. Treat that as a description of scope, not a spec sheet — I can't verify the limits without documentation.

## Why a manual tool earns its place

Automated trendline scripts are fast and wrong in predictable ways. They latch onto every minor pivot and clutter the chart with lines that mean nothing. A manual approach flips that: fewer lines, each one deliberate, each one something you can defend. You're not outsourcing the read — you're giving yourself better tools to record it.

That's the real pitch here. If your process already involves drawing structure by hand, this consolidates it into one indicator instead of scattering lines across the chart with the native drawing tools.

## How you'd use it

The workflow is straightforward and entirely on you:

1. **Mark your candles.** Identify the swing highs, swing lows, and impulse legs that define the current structure.
2. **Draw trendlines** to connect the relevant pivots — support, resistance, or the diagonal trend you're tracking.
3. **Build channels** where price respects a parallel range, so you have a defined zone rather than a single line.
4. **Flag breakouts and breakdowns** as price exits the structure, keeping a visual record of where the trend shifted.

The discipline is the point. When a line breaks, it's because *you* decided it broke — not because a smoothing parameter crossed a threshold.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Puts you in control — no black-box signals, no parameter archaeology.
- Consolidates trendlines, channels, and breakout marking in one place.
- Scales to multi-timeframe work because the analysis is yours, not the script's.
- Forces a repeatable, disciplined read of structure.

**Cons**
- It won't tell you what to do. Traders who want alerts and entries handed to them will find it hollow.
- Manual means manual — the quality of every line depends on your judgment.
- The absence of documentation is a real friction point; expect to learn it by clicking around.
- The long name promises a lot; the tool is only as good as the chart you build with it.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already draw structure by hand and want a cleaner way to keep it on one chart. Swing and position traders working off trendlines and channels will get the most from it. If you're a systematic trader hunting for a signal engine, this isn't it — and the name is honest enough to tell you so.

## FAQ

No. It's an annotation and structure tool. Any signal comes from your own read of the lines you draw.

**Do I have to draw everything myself?**
Based on the "Manual" in the name, yes — that's the design intent, not a limitation.

**Can it replace TradingView's native drawing tools?**

**Is it beginner-friendly?**
