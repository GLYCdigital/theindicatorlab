Trendlines are the oldest game in technical analysis, and they're also the easiest thing to draw badly. The Artillery Trendline Break and Bounce indicator is aimed squarely at that problem: it's a trend-category tool built around the two events that actually matter at a trendline — the **break** through it and the **bounce** off it.

That's the honest, one-line summary. Let me be upfront about the rest: there is no published documentation for this indicator that I can point to, so this review stays at the level of concept and use. I won't quote settings, thresholds or performance numbers, because inventing them would be worse than saying nothing.

## What it's actually doing

The idea is straightforward and worth understanding before you install anything. A trendline is only meaningful at two moments: when price **fails to break it** (a bounce, which suggests the trend is intact and buyers/sellers are defending the line) and when price **breaks through it** (which suggests the trend's structure is changing). Everything else is noise sitting between those two events.

This indicator is designed to flag those two moments rather than ask you to eyeball them. In a trend category, that's a genuinely useful framing — it treats the trendline as a decision point, not decoration. As shown in the chart above, the visual output centres on highlighting where price interacts with a drawn line, so you can read the situation at a glance instead of redrawing manually every session.

## Why the concept earns its keep

The reason this kind of tool is worth a look — and why I've rated it well despite the thin documentation — is that it addresses a real, specific trader failure. Most people draw trendlines after the fact, fitting them to whatever already happened, then convince themselves the line "worked." A tool that systematically watches for breaks and bounces forces a cleaner question: *is price respecting structure, or has it broken it?*

That's a trend-continuation versus trend-reversal question, and it's the backbone of a lot of discretionary trading. Building it around the trendline rather than around a moving-average crossover or an oscillator means the signals map onto how many traders actually think about structure.

## How you'd use it

Generically, the workflow is what you'd expect from a trendline tool:

- Establish the trend and the line you care about.
- Watch for the indicator to mark a **bounce** — price holding the line — which is the continuation read.
- Watch for a **break** — price moving through the line — which is the shift-of-structure read.
- Combine with your own context (higher-timeframe trend, volume, support/resistance) before acting.

Nothing here tells you to trade blind. The indicator is a pair of eyes on the line, not a strategy. Treat it that way and it's a useful assistant; treat it as a signal generator and you'll be disappointed, as with any structure tool.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Focused on the two events that genuinely matter at a trendline — breaks and bounces — rather than cluttering the chart with extra noise.
- Sits in the trend category where it belongs, so it complements trend-following approaches rather than fighting them.
- Conceptually clean: it formalises a discretionary process, which is exactly what a good indicator should do.

**Cons**
- There's no accessible documentation I can cite, so you'll be reverse-engineering the exact logic yourself.
- Trendline tools are inherently sensitive to how the line is defined; the quality of any break/bounce flag depends on that definition.
- Like any structure tool, it can whipsaw in choppy conditions — a break that immediately reverses is the classic trap.

## Who it's for

Discretionary trend traders who already draw trendlines and want a second set of eyes on breaks and bounces. Swing traders working structure across timeframes will get the most from it. It's less suited to pure mechanical/system traders who want a fully specified, rule-based signal — the lack of documentation will frustrate them.

## FAQ

**Does it tell me when to buy or sell?**
No. It highlights breaks and bounces at trendlines. The decision — and the risk management — remains yours.

**Will it work on any market or timeframe?**
The concept of breaks and bounces applies broadly, but I can't make specific claims about which markets or timeframes suit it without documentation. Test it on your own instruments.

**Is it a replacement for drawing trendlines myself?**
It's an assist, not a replacement. You still need a view on which line matters.

## Verdict

The Artillery Trendline Break and Bounce does one thing and does it with a clear rationale: it watches the trendline for the two events that carry information. The lack of published specifics is a real drawback, and the conceptual dependence on how the line is defined means it won't suit everyone. But as a structure-focused trend aid for discretionary traders, it's a solid, focused tool.

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)** — good concept, honest scope, thin documentation.
