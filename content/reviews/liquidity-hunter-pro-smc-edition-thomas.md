"Smart Money Concepts" has become one of those labels that gets slapped on everything, so it's fair to be skeptical when you see it in a title. Liquidity Hunter Pro SMC Edition [Thomas] is a TradingView indicator that approaches trend through the lens of liquidity and market structure — the SMC framework — rather than through a conventional moving-average or oscillator stack. That framing matters, because it tells you what kind of tool this is before you ever load it.

A note on scope up front: the publisher's documentation wasn't available to review here, so this isn't a settings-by-settings breakdown. What follows is an honest read of what the indicator is built to do, based on its stated concept and category, without invented numbers or claims about how it performs.

## What it's actually doing

SMC is a discretionary school of price action built around a few core ideas: that price moves between areas of resting liquidity, that "smart money" leaves footprints in the form of order blocks and imbalances, and that trend is defined by breaks in market structure rather than by an averaged price line. An indicator that carries "Liquidity Hunter" in its name is, in plain terms, trying to surface those liquidity zones and structural shifts on the chart so you don't have to hand-mark them every session.

The "Trend" category placement makes sense within that framework: in SMC, trend is read through structure — higher highs and higher lows, or the failure of them — not through momentum. So you should expect this tool to annotate the chart with structure and liquidity concepts, giving you a visual scaffold for a discretionary read.

## Where it fits in a workflow

The realistic use case is as a mark-up layer, not a signal generator you blindly follow. You load it, let it draw the zones and structure it's designed to highlight, and then apply your own judgment about which levels are worth trading. That's how most SMC-style indicators are meant to be used, and it's the honest expectation to set.

Practically, that means the indicator is doing prep work for you: identifying where liquidity is likely pooled, where structure has shifted, where price might react. The decision to enter, size a position, or stand aside stays with you. If you're looking for a tool that tells you when to buy and sell with an arrow, this isn't that — and no SMC indicator genuinely is.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- The SMC framing is coherent and self-consistent. Liquidity and structure are a real, widely-used way to read a chart, not a marketing gimmick.
- It saves manual annotation. Marking order blocks and structure shifts by hand across multiple pairs and timeframes is tedious; automating the scaffolding is genuinely useful.
- It's a concept-first tool. That makes it flexible — you can apply it across markets and timeframes rather than being locked to one setup.

**Cons**
- SMC is discretionary by nature, and any indicator that encodes it will involve the author's interpretation of what counts as a valid zone or structure break. Two traders can look at the same chart and disagree.
- If the documentation is thin, as it is in this case, you're partly reverse-engineering the author's logic from the chart rather than from a clear spec. That's a real friction point.
- It adds visual density. SMC annotations can clutter a chart, and if you're not already fluent in the framework, the extra lines may confuse more than clarify.

## Who this is for

This suits traders who already think in market-structure terms and want to speed up their chart prep — swing and intraday traders working off liquidity levels, particularly those who trade the SMC playbook manually and want the zones drawn for them. It is *not* for someone who wants a hands-off, mechanical system, or for a beginner with no exposure to SMC concepts, who will likely find the output opaque.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
Based on its SMC/liquidity framing, treat it as an annotation and structure tool rather than an entry-signal generator. The trading decision remains yours.

**Which timeframes and markets?**
No specific recommendation can be made without the publisher's documentation. The concepts it's built on are timeframe-agnostic, so test it against how you actually trade.

**Is it a replacement for learning SMC?**
No. It visualizes concepts; it doesn't teach them. If you don't understand liquidity and market structure, the marks won't mean much.

## Verdict

A solid, concept-driven indicator with a clear identity. It doesn't pretend to be a magic signal box — it automates the tedious parts of an SMC read and leaves the judgment to you, which is the right division of labor. The main caveat is documentation: without a clear spec, part of your job is figuring out the author's logic from the chart itself. If you're already fluent in Smart Money Concepts and want faster chart prep, this earns its place. If you're after mechanical signals, look elsewhere.
