Some indicators try to be everything. This one has a narrower brief, and it's better for it: it maps order blocks and supply/demand zones onto your chart and leans on the logic of smart money concepts to do it. If you already think in terms of where price left inefficiency behind rather than where an oscillator crossed a line, this is aimed squarely at you.

I'll be upfront about a limitation before going further: there's no official documentation to lean on here, so everything below stays at the conceptual level. No parameter lists, no default thresholds, no claims about accuracy. Just what the tool is for and how a trader would realistically use it.

## What it actually is

The name tells most of the story. "OB" is order blocks — the candles or clusters where price made a decisive move and left a footprint worth revisiting. "Supply & demand" is the older, simpler framing of the same idea: zones where selling overwhelmed buying, or the reverse. "SMC" is smart money concepts, the school of thought that treats those zones as the trace of larger participants and builds a narrative around market structure shifts, breaks of structure and liquidity grabs.

Put together, this is a chart overlay that identifies and draws those areas so you don't have to manually mark them candle by candle. It's a visual aid for a discretionary method, not a signal generator that tells you when to click buy.

## Where it earns its keep

The main value of any order-block tool is consistency. Hand-marking zones is fine until you're tired at the end of a session and your judgment drifts. A systematic overlay keeps the definition of a zone stable from chart to chart and day to day, which matters if you're comparing setups across instruments.

Two things stand out conceptually:

- **It's a structural overlay, not a trigger.** It's categorised as trend, but the honest read is that it's helping you locate where price is likely to react, which then informs a trend or continuation trade. The indicator frames the map; your entries still come from your own read.
- **The supply/demand framing bridges two camps.** Traders who never bought into SMC jargon can still use the zones, because a demand zone is a demand zone regardless of the label. That makes it more approachable than a lot of pure-SMC tools.

## How you'd actually use it

The realistic workflow is top-down. You mark your zones on a higher timeframe to establish the playing field, then drop to a lower one to watch how price behaves as it approaches. A reaction at a zone — a rejection, a failed push, a structure break — is your context. The zone itself is not a trade.

Where this kind of tool earns its fee is in filtering. Not every zone deserves attention. The skill is in deciding which ones price is actually respecting and ignoring the rest, and an overlay like this makes that decision easier by putting the candidates in front of you consistently.

As shown in the chart above, the zones render as bands across price, which is the right way to think about them — areas, not lines.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Consolidates order blocks and supply/demand into one overlay instead of stacking three separate scripts.
- SMC framing keeps you thinking in terms of structure and liquidity rather than lagging averages.
- The dual vocabulary makes it usable whether or not you subscribe to smart money theory.
- As a visual organiser, it reduces the manual marking that eats into screen time.

**Cons**
- No published documentation means you're learning it by observation, which is slower and less certain.
- Order-block logic is inherently subjective — different traders draw different zones, and no overlay resolves that ambiguity for you.
- It won't tell you when to enter, so it's a poor fit if you want alerts and signals.
- Zone-heavy charts get cluttered fast on lower timeframes if you're not disciplined about which ones you keep.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already work with market structure and want a cleaner way to mark zones — swing and intraday traders on higher timeframes especially. If you're a systematic trader looking for a mechanical edge with backtested rules, this isn't that. If you're brand new and hoping the zones will tell you what to do, you'll be disappointed; the tool assumes you bring the judgment.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
No. It's a structural overlay. Zones are context, and the decision to act on them is yours.

**Which timeframes work best?**
Nothing official specifies this, so treat it as a judgment call. The concept of order blocks and supply/demand is timeframe-agnostic; the practical constraint is chart clutter, which gets worse the lower you go.

**Can I use it without knowing SMC?**
Yes. The supply/demand half of the name is plain price action, so you can use the zones on that basis alone.

## Verdict

A focused, well-conceived overlay that does one job without pretending to do more. The lack of documentation is a real friction point, and the subjective nature of zone-drawing means it will never be a plug-and-play system. But for the trader it's built for — someone who already thinks in structure and wants a consistent visual map — it's a solid addition to the toolkit rather than a novelty.
