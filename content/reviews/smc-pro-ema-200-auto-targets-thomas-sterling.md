Most trend indicators answer one question and stop there: is price above or below a moving average? The **SMC Pro EMA 200 Auto Targets** indicator from Thomas-Sterling tries to answer a longer chain — where's the trend bias, what structure supports it, and where might price go next — and packages it into a single overlay. That's an ambitious scope, and the name tells you exactly what it's reaching for: Smart Money Concepts (SMC) read through the lens of the 200 EMA, with targets plotted for you.

## What it actually does

At its core this is a trend-following tool built on two layers. The first is a 200-period exponential moving average, used as the directional filter — the classic "which side of the 200 are we on" bias test that swing traders and position traders already lean on. The second layer is the SMC component: the indicator reads market structure (the higher-highs/higher-lows versus lower-highs/lower-lows logic that SMC traders use to define trend) and, from that, projects target levels automatically rather than making you draw them by hand.

The "Auto Targets" in the name is the part that separates it from a plain EMA plot. Instead of just showing you where the average sits, it generates objective levels ahead of price — likely derived from recent swing structure — so you're not eyeballing where a move might exhaust.

As shown in the chart above, the overlay sits on price and layers these elements together, so you're reading bias, structure, and targets in one glance rather than across three separate tools.

## Key features

- **200 EMA bias filter.** A single, well-understood directional anchor. If price is respecting the 200, the trend context is clean; if it's chopping through it, that's your signal to stand aside.
- **Market-structure reading.** The SMC layer tracks swing structure, so the trend call isn't just "price is above a line" — it's whether that move is structurally valid.
- **Automatic targets.** The indicator projects target levels for you, which removes a repetitive manual step for anyone who draws structure by hand every session.
- **Single-overlay workflow.** Bias, structure, and targets share one pane, cutting down on chart clutter versus stacking three separate indicators.

## How to use it

The workflow is straightforward and matches how trend traders already operate. Use the 200 EMA to establish directional bias — long setups when price is holding above it, short setups when below. Then let the SMC structure confirm whether that bias is genuinely intact or just a brief poke through the average. The auto targets give you a reference for where a move might reasonably extend, which is useful for setting profit-taking zones or for judging whether a trade still has room.

The practical value is discipline. Because the targets are generated for you, the temptation to "adjust" your exit to fit a narrative is smaller — the levels are on the chart before you enter. That's a behavioural edge as much as a technical one.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Combines two complementary ideas — a slow trend filter and structure-based reading — instead of one, which reduces the false-signal problem pure EMA crossovers suffer from.
- Auto targets save real time for traders who'd otherwise draw levels manually each session.
- Clean single-overlay design keeps the chart readable.
- The 200 EMA is a well-understood anchor, so there's no black-box mystery about the trend layer.

**Cons**
- Combining SMC structure with a fixed 200 EMA can produce conflicting signals — structure may say "uptrend" while price sits below the average, leaving you to adjudicate. That takes judgement, not just following arrows.
- Auto targets are only as good as the structure logic behind them; in choppy, range-bound conditions structure-based targets tend to get run through, so it's not a tool that flatters a ranging market.
- Because the concept is layered, new users need to understand both SMC structure and EMA bias to use it well — it's not plug-and-play for a beginner.

## Who it's for

This suits swing and position traders who already think in terms of trend bias and market structure and want those reads consolidated with objective targets. It's a good fit for SMC-inclined traders who want a trend filter layered on top, and for anyone tired of maintaining a manual level-drawing routine. Discretionary intraday traders will find less value — the 200 EMA is a slow anchor, and the tool's strength is in higher-level trend context rather than fast scalping signals.

## FAQ

**Does it repaint?**
I can't verify repainting behaviour from the available material, so treat any structure-based indicator with the usual caution and confirm signals on closed bars.

**Can I use it on any timeframe?**
The 200 EMA works on any timeframe, but its meaning shifts — on lower timeframes it's a much faster anchor than on daily charts. Match the timeframe to your holding period.

**Do the targets guarantee price will reach them?**
No indicator can promise that. Auto targets are reference levels, not predictions, and should be managed with your own risk rules.

## Verdict

The SMC Pro EMA 200 Auto Targets earns its place as a consolidation tool. It takes two ideas most trend traders already use separately — a slow EMA bias filter and structure-based SMC reading — and merges them with automatic target generation, cutting manual work and chart clutter. It's not magic, and the layering means it demands some understanding to use well. But for traders who already think in these terms, it's a sensible, time-saving overlay.
