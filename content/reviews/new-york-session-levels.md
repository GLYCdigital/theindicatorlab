# New_York_Session_Levels Review

Session-based indicators are a crowded category, and many are cosmetic—lines that look tidy on a chart but carry little information. New_York_Session_Levels takes a narrower approach: it marks the open, high, low, and close of the New York session and projects those levels forward.

## What It Actually Does

The indicator draws four horizontal lines on the chart: the NY session open, high, low, and close. These levels stay static until the next NY session, giving a fixed framework for intraday reference. Price behaviour around these levels—reactions at the open, breaks of the high, holds at the low—is the intended use case.

There is no predictive component here. The levels are plotted from historical session data.

## Key Features

- **Session-specific focus**: These are NY session levels, not generic daily levels.
- **Customizable time zone**: You can set the indicator to your local time so levels appear when relevant to you.
- **Clean visuals**: Lines are thin and color-coded, with adjustable opacity and line style.
- **Alerts on breakouts**: Alert conditions exist for price crossing any of the four levels.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

- **Line style**: Differentiating the high/low from the open/close makes visual scanning faster.
- **Opacity**: Lower opacity for high/low and higher for open/close keeps the chart readable without clutter.
- **Time zone**: Set this manually rather than relying on auto-detect.
- **Extend levels**: Enabling this projects the levels through the next session, which is where much of the practical value sits.

## How to Use It

This is a framework, not a signal generator. Common approaches:

- **Open as pivot**: If price holds above the NY open after the early part of the session, that supports a long bias on pullbacks to the level. Below the open, the bias flips short.
- **High/low breakouts**: A clean break of the NY high invites a long with a stop at the open. A rejection at the high invites a short back toward the open.
- **Close as magnet**: Late in the session, price can drift back toward the NY close, which scalpers may treat as a mean-reversion setup.

**Risk management**: The NY low serves as a stop reference for longs, the NY high for shorts.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**:
- Levels are fixed once the session closes—no repainting.
- No lag; this is raw session data rather than a derived average.
- Works on intraday time frames.
- Free on TradingView.

**Cons**:
- Only useful for US session traders. Asian and European session participants get less from it.
- No multi-session comparison (e.g. NY open versus London open).
- Lines can feel static if you are used to dynamic indicators.

## Who It's For

- Day traders focused on the NY open (stocks, forex, futures).
- Traders who avoid repainting indicators.
- Anyone wanting a clean, no-nonsense level framework.

**Not for**: Swing traders, overnight hold strategies, or anyone trading purely on indicators.

## Alternatives

- **Session Map** – Shows all three major sessions (Asia, London, NY) with full OHLC. Better for global traders.
- **Daily Open/Close Levels** – Simpler; just the daily open and close. Good if you want fewer lines.
- **FTD (First Trading Day) Levels** – More complex, uses first hour data. Suited to breakout traders wanting tighter levels.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this repaint?**
A: No. The levels are fixed once the NY session closes.

**Q: Can I use it on crypto?**
A: Yes, but the NY session doesn't carry the same significance for crypto. It works best on equities and forex pairs involving USD.

**Q: What time frame should I use?**
A: Intraday time frames for session trading; higher time frames if you are trading the session breakouts.

**Q: Does it work with pre-market data?**
A: No. It starts at the NY session open.

## Final Verdict

New_York_Session_Levels does one thing and does it well. It isn't flashy, but it is consistent. If you trade the US session, it's a useful addition to your toolkit. If you don't, skip it.
