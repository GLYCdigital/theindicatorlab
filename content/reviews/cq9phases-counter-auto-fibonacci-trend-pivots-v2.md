Some indicators try to do one thing well. This one tries to do several things at once, and the name is refreshingly honest about it. CQ_(9)_Phases Counter + Auto Fibonacci + Trend Pivots v2 is a trend-category tool that combines three distinct analytical concepts into a single overlay: a phase counter, automatically drawn Fibonacci levels, and trend pivots.

One caveat before we go further: there is no retrievable official documentation or published description for this indicator. That means everything below is based on what the title and category tell us — the three named modules — and I am deliberately not going to invent settings, default values, thresholds or performance claims. This is a conceptual read, not a walkthrough of documented inputs. Keep that in mind when weighing the rating.

## What the name actually tells you

The title is a parts list. "Phases Counter" suggests some form of sequential counting logic — a way of marking where price sits within an ongoing move rather than just firing a single on/off signal. "Auto Fibonacci" points to Fibonacci retracement or extension levels that are drawn for you instead of hand-placed. "Trend Pivots" implies the indicator marks swing highs and lows to help define trend structure.

Bundling three modules is the core design idea. Instead of stacking three separate scripts on your chart and wrestling with their different scaling and visual styles, you get one study that handles the lot. That is the real selling point, and it is a legitimate one — chart clutter is a genuine problem in discretionary trading.

## Where the bundle helps

The strongest argument for a combined tool is workflow. Fibonacci levels are tedious to draw by hand and easy to get inconsistent — anchoring them to the wrong swing changes everything. A module that places them automatically removes a repetitive task and, in principle, keeps your levels tied to the same structural logic each time. That consistency is worth something.

The phase counter and pivot tracking serve a related purpose. Pivots give you the skeleton of trend structure; a counter adds a sense of progression within a move. Together they aim at the same question a trend trader is always asking: where are we in this move, and what structure backs that up? Having both in one place, visually aligned, is more useful than having them scattered across separate indicators.

As the chart above suggests, the output is an overlay meant to sit on price and frame the trend context around it.

## Where to be skeptical

Three modules in one script is also the main risk. When a single indicator tries to cover counting, Fibonacci and pivots, each module gets less room to be deep. A dedicated Fibonacci tool can offer more control over anchoring and level selection; a dedicated pivot script can offer more nuance in how swings are confirmed. The bundled version is a convenience play, not a specialist play.

There is also a cognitive-load question. Three layers of information on one chart can clarify, or it can overwhelm, depending on how the visuals are configured. Without documented guidance on the settings, you will be doing your own discovery on what each module is actually telling you — and that takes screen time.

And the usual rule applies: any indicator that draws levels and marks structure is still interpreting price, not predicting it. Treat the output as context, not as a signal to act on alone.

## How I would approach it

Start by isolating each module. Turn your attention to the pivots first and see whether the marked swings match the structure you would draw yourself. If they do, the Fibonacci levels anchored to those pivots become far more trustworthy. Then watch the phase counter over a stretch of chart and ask whether its progression lines up with how the move actually unfolded.

If any one of the three modules does not fit how you read a chart, the value of the bundle drops quickly — because you are now carrying visual noise for a feature you do not use. That is the honest test.

## Who it suits

Discretionary trend traders who already think in terms of swing structure and Fibonacci retracements, and who want those tools consolidated rather than scattered across the chart, are the natural audience. It is less suited to traders who want a single clean signal or a mechanical system — this is a context tool, not a trigger.

## FAQ

**Is it a buy/sell signal indicator?** No — it is a trend-context overlay built from structural and Fibonacci concepts, not a signal generator.

**Can I use only part of it?** That depends on how the inputs are structured, which is not documented. Assume you may need to work with the full overlay.

**Does it work on any timeframe?** No documented guidance exists, so test it on your own charts rather than assuming.

## Verdict

A sensible, convenience-driven bundle that consolidates three trend tools a discretionary trader would otherwise assemble by hand. The lack of documentation is a real drawback — you are partly buying on faith — but the underlying concept is sound and the workflow benefit is genuine. ⭐⭐⭐⭐
