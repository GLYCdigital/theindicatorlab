Fibonacci and impulse moves are two ideas that traders keep trying to bolt together. Fibonacci Impulse Layers [josseliani] is one attempt to do exactly that — a trend-category indicator that layers Fibonacci structure over an impulse move to help you read where a trending leg might pause or reverse.

## What it's actually doing

The name tells you most of the story. An "impulse" in the Elliott/price-action sense is a directional thrust — the leg that does the work in a trend. Fibonacci "layers" refers to the retracement and extension levels that get projected across that leg. The indicator takes an impulse move and maps Fibonacci structure onto it, so instead of eyeballing where the 0.618 or the 1.272 sits, you get those layers drawn for you as the trend develops.

That's a legitimate workflow. Most traders who use Fibonacci manually spend half their time dragging the retracement tool between swing points and arguing with themselves about which high and low to anchor to. Automating the projection removes a layer of subjectivity from a method that is already pretty subjective to begin with.

Because it sits in the trend category, the intent is clearly directional: you use the layers to judge whether a pullback is a healthy retracement inside the trend or the start of something else. The Fibonacci framework gives you reference zones; the trend context tells you which side to favour.

## How you'd use it

The general workflow for a tool like this is straightforward. You identify a trending move on your chart, let the indicator project its Fibonacci layers across the impulse, and then watch how price behaves at those zones. A shallow retracement that holds above a key level reads differently than a deep one that slices through — and the layers give you a consistent frame for that read across different setups.

As shown in the chart above, the value is in having the structure drawn for you rather than in any single magic level. Fibonacci works because enough participants watch the same ratios; an indicator that keeps those ratios consistently placed just saves you the manual work.

One honest caveat: any tool that requires the impulse to be defined has an anchoring question. Whether the indicator handles that automatically or leaves it to the user is something I can't confirm from the source material, so I won't pretend to know. If you buy in, that's the first thing to check.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Removes the manual tedium of drawing Fibonacci retracements and extensions on every impulse leg.
- Keeps a consistent Fibonacci framework across setups, which is more disciplined than eyeballing levels trade to trade.
- Pairs naturally with trend-following logic — the layers tell you where, the trend tells you which direction.
- Being a TradingView indicator, it lives on your chart alongside your existing tools rather than in a separate window.

**Cons**
- Fibonacci is interpretive. Different anchors produce different levels, and no indicator resolves that debate for you.
- The source documentation is thin, so you're partly reverse-engineering behaviour from the chart rather than reading a clear spec.
- It's a trend tool, so it will be least useful in choppy, range-bound conditions where impulse legs don't hold together.
- No performance data is published, which is normal for a drawing/structure tool but worth stating plainly.

## Who it's for

This is for the discretionary trend trader who already thinks in terms of impulse and correction and wants the Fibonacci structure handled automatically. If you've ever abandoned a Fibonacci retracement mid-draw because you couldn't decide which swing to anchor to, this targets that frustration directly.

It is **not** for someone looking for a mechanical buy/sell signal with a documented win rate. Nothing here suggests it fires entries; it's a structural overlay for your own decision-making. Scalpers on very low timeframes and pure range traders will likely find little use for it.

Fibonacci Impulse Layers [josseliani] is a sensible, focused tool that automates one of the more tedious parts of a Fibonacci-based trend workflow.
