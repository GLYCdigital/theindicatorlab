Some indicators try to predict. This one just draws what price already did. **HH/LL Trendlines [twr]** is a structural trend tool: it takes the higher highs and lower lows that price prints and connects them into trendlines, so you can read the market's structure without manually anchoring every swing point yourself.

That's the entire premise, and it's a good one. Swing structure is the backbone of trend analysis, and most traders waste a lot of screen time drawing lines by hand that become wrong the moment a new leg forms. An indicator that handles that mechanical work has obvious appeal.

## What it actually does

The concept is straightforward: identify higher highs and lower lows, then draw trendlines through them. Higher highs and higher lows define an uptrend; lower highs and lower lows define a downtrend. By drawing lines off those points, the indicator visualizes the structural rhythm of the market — the series of peaks and troughs that tells you whether buyers or sellers are in control.

This is the kind of tool that belongs on the chart as context, not as a signal generator. It doesn't tell you to buy or sell. It tells you where the structure is, and leaves the trading decision to you.

## How you'd use it

The workflow is intuitive for anyone who thinks in terms of market structure:

- **Read the slope and sequence.** If the trendlines are stepping up, structure is bullish. If they're stepping down, bearish. If they're messy or overlapping, the market is likely ranging.
- **Watch for breaks.** A trendline break is a classic early warning that a trend's rhythm is changing — not a guaranteed reversal, but a cue to pay closer attention.
- **Use it as a filter.** If you trade with the trend, structural lines give you a reference for where a pullback might find support or resistance.

The value here is speed and objectivity. The chart above shows how the lines frame the swing sequence — you see the structure at a glance instead of hunting for it.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- **Removes manual drawing.** Hand-drawn trendlines are inconsistent and need constant maintenance. Automating swing-based lines saves time and keeps your analysis objective.
- **Structure-first approach.** It focuses on the concept that actually matters — higher highs and lower lows — rather than piling on lagging oscillators.
- **Works as context.** It slots into almost any trend-following or pullback strategy as a structural filter.

**Cons**
- **It's a line-drawing tool, not an edge.** Trendlines are subjective even when automated; where a swing is "confirmed" is a judgment call, and different logic produces different lines.
- **No signal layer.** If you want alerts, entries, or confluence scoring, you'll need to build that yourself around it.
- **Thin documentation.** The concept is simple enough to grasp quickly, but you'll be working out the exact behavior by observation rather than from a detailed manual.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already think in terms of market structure — swing traders, price-action traders, and anyone who keeps a trendline on the chart as a matter of habit. It's less useful for pure system traders looking for a black-box signal, because this indicator deliberately doesn't give one.

## FAQ

**Does it repaint?** I can't confirm that from the available material. Swing-based tools depend on how a swing is confirmed; watch how the lines behave in real time before relying on them.

**Can I trade its signals?** It doesn't produce signals — it draws structure. Treat the lines as context and combine them with your own entry logic.

**Does it work on any timeframe or market?** The concept of higher highs and lower lows is universal, but the source doesn't specify optimal settings, so test it on your own charts.

## Verdict

**HH/LL Trendlines [twr]** does one job — drawing structural trendlines from swing highs and lows — and does it in a way that saves real screen time. It's not a system, and it won't hand you an edge on a plate. But as a clean, objective way to see market structure, it earns its place on a chart. Four stars: genuinely useful, with the caveat that the intelligence still has to come from you.
