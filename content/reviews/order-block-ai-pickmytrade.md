Order blocks have become one of the most overused terms in retail trading. Every second indicator claims to find them, and most just draw a box around the last swing high and call it "smart money." So when a tool puts "AI" in its name, the fair question is whether it's adding anything real or just riding the buzzword.

**Order Block AI Pickmytrade** is a TradingView indicator in the trend category that works with order block concepts. That's the honest summary — and it's worth being upfront that the publicly available documentation for this one is thin. There's no detailed official description to pull parameter lists, thresholds or methodology from. So this review sticks to what the tool is *for* and how traders typically put order block logic to work, without pretending to know internals I can't verify.

## What it's actually doing

An order block, in the way most traders use the term, is the last opposing candle before an impulsive move — the zone where institutional-sized orders are assumed to have been placed. The idea is that price often returns to that zone before continuing, giving you a reference area for entries, stops and targets rather than a random line on a chart.

A tool like this automates the detection side of that. Instead of you manually scanning left for the last down candle before a rally, the indicator marks those zones for you and keeps them on the chart as price develops. The "AI" framing suggests some form of algorithmic filtering — sorting meaningful blocks from noise rather than tagging every candle. I can't confirm what that filtering actually consists of, and I won't guess.

## Where it fits a workflow

The practical value of any order block indicator is context, not signals. Used well, it answers a narrow question: *where did the last real imbalance occur, and is price reacting there now?*

A sensible workflow looks like this. You identify the prevailing trend first — that's the whole point of it living in the trend category. You let the indicator map its zones. Then you watch how price behaves when it returns to one: rejection, absorption, or a clean break through. The zone itself is not a trade; the reaction to it is.

As shown in the chart above, the value is in having those levels pre-marked so you're not drawing them live while price is moving against you.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Removes the manual legwork of hunting order blocks candle by candle.
- Trend-category framing keeps you thinking about direction rather than treating every zone as tradeable.
- Order block logic is a genuine, widely-used framework — not a made-up concept.
- Visual zones on the chart are easy to reason about alongside your own levels.

**Cons**

- Documentation is limited, so you're partly reverse-engineering behaviour from the chart.
- "AI" in the name sets an expectation the tool may not fully justify — treat it as a marketing label until proven otherwise.
- Order blocks are subjective by nature; two traders will disagree on which candle qualifies.
- Like any zone-based tool, it can encourage over-trading if you treat every box as a signal.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already think in terms of market structure and want their order block marking sped up. It suits people who use zones as *areas of interest* and combine them with their own confirmation — price action, volume, a momentum read.

It's not for anyone wanting a turnkey buy/sell arrow system, and it's not for pure mechanical traders who need fully defined rules with no interpretation.

## Verdict

Order Block AI Pickmytrade does a legitimate job of automating a framework plenty of traders already use manually. The trend-category placement is sensible, and the zone-based approach is honest market structure work rather than a gimmick.
