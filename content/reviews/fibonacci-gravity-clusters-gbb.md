Fibonacci and trend tools are a crowded corner of TradingView, so a name like "Fibonacci Gravity Clusters" has to earn its place. The idea the title points to is straightforward: treat Fibonacci-derived levels as a kind of gravitational field, and read clusters of those levels as areas where price behaviour matters. This review is deliberately honest about a limitation up front — the official documentation for this indicator wasn't available, so everything below describes the concept generically rather than quoting specific settings, values or behaviour I can't verify.

## What this indicator is trying to do

The premise is a familiar one dressed in newer language. Fibonacci retracements and extensions are usually drawn as fixed horizontal lines. The "clusters" and "gravity" framing suggests the author is grouping Fibonacci-derived levels and treating the zones where they coincide as more significant than any single line. That's a reasonable analytical instinct — confluence between levels is a well-worn idea — and it's the part of the tool that makes conceptual sense even without the documentation.

What I can't do is tell you what the defaults are, how many levels feed the clusters, or how the "gravity" weighting is calculated. Those details aren't in the material I have, and inventing them would be worse than useless. Treat the specifics as something you verify on the chart yourself.

## How the Fibonacci-plus-cluster idea fits together

Fibonacci tools answer "where might price react?" Cluster tools answer "where do multiple reasons point to the same area?" Combining them is a sensible marriage. A single retracement level is easy to ignore; a band where several derived levels stack up is harder to dismiss as noise.

The "gravity" wording implies price being pulled toward or repelled from these zones — the kind of language that describes reactions rather than predictions. That's the right mental model. If you approach this expecting magnet-like zones where price tends to pause or reverse, you're using it as intended; if you expect a signal generator that tells you to buy or sell, you'll probably be disappointed.

As shown in the chart above, the tool sits in the trend category, so the intent is context — where in the structure price is sitting — rather than a standalone entry trigger.

## How to actually use it

The sensible workflow, given the concept:

- Read the cluster zones first, before you look at any other indicator. Ask where they sit relative to current price and recent structure.
- Treat a cluster as a zone of interest, not a line to trade off mechanically. Price behaviour inside the zone matters more than the zone itself.
- Use it as a filter on top of your existing process. If your setup lines up with a cluster, it gets more weight; if it fights one, you've got a reason to be cautious.
- Because this is a trend-category tool, check alignment with the broader direction of the market rather than using it in isolation.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- The confluence concept is genuinely useful — stacking Fibonacci levels into zones is more robust than trading single lines.
- The "gravity" framing encourages thinking about price reaction and attraction, which is a healthier mental model than signal-chasing.
- It fits naturally into a trend-following workflow as a context layer rather than a competing system.

**Cons**
- Without documentation, the internals are opaque. You can't easily reason about why a cluster sits where it does, which matters if you want to trust a tool.
- Clustered Fibonacci zones can clutter a chart and overstate significance if you read every band as important.
- It won't replace your entry logic. Anyone looking for a mechanical signal will find it underwhelming.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already use Fibonacci retracements and want a cleaner way to spot where levels stack up. Swing and trend traders who treat indicators as context rather than commands will get the most out of it. If you're a purely systematic trader who needs to know the exact formula behind every line, the lack of documentation is a real obstacle.

The underlying idea — Fibonacci confluence expressed as zones rather than single lines — is sound, and the trend-category placement is honest about what it is. For traders who already work with Fibonacci and want a confluence overlay, it's a reasonable addition. For anyone expecting a self-contained trading system, keep looking.
