Stochastic Range Regime by MarkitTick is one of those indicators that tries to solve a specific problem rather than chase a trend. The name tells you most of what you need to know: it takes the classic stochastic oscillator — a momentum tool — and wraps it in a regime or range context. In other words, it isn't just asking "where is momentum right now?" It's trying to ask "where is momentum, and does the current market condition even make that momentum signal worth listening to?"

That distinction matters. A raw stochastic fires a lot of signals, and a good number of them are noise when the market is chopping sideways or trending hard. By layering a range/regime read on top, the indicator aims to filter momentum into something closer to a usable directional bias. It's classified under Trend, which fits: the end goal is to help you lean one way or the other, not just to spot overbought and oversold extremes.

Worth being upfront about one thing before we go further: there is no official documentation or published description available for this indicator. So this review sticks to what the tool is conceptually and how you'd think about using it. No invented settings, no made-up defaults, no performance claims. If you want the exact parameter list, you'll need to open it on a chart.

## What it actually does

At its core, the indicator combines two ideas that traders already know well. The stochastic oscillator measures where price sits relative to its recent high-low range, producing the familiar overbought/oversold readings. The "range regime" part is the layer that classifies the broader market condition — broadly, whether price is moving in a defined range or trending.

The value of the combination is in the interaction. A stochastic reading that says "overbought" means something quite different in a strong uptrend than it does in a flat, range-bound market. In a trend, overbought can stay overbought for a long time; in a range, it's more likely to mark a turning point. A tool that accounts for that context is doing more useful work than a naked oscillator, and that's the honest pitch here.

## How you'd approach using it

As shown in the chart above, the natural workflow is to read the regime first, then the momentum. If the indicator suggests the market is ranging, you're looking at stochastic extremes as potential mean-reversion points — buying weakness, selling strength, expecting reversion to the middle. If it suggests a trending regime, those same extremes become less about reversal and more about confirming that a pullback is stretched and may be worth joining.

The sensible habit is to treat it as a bias filter, not a trigger. It tells you which side of the market is more interesting right now; your entry still needs price action, structure, or another confirmation. That's how most well-built regime tools are meant to be used, and it's the fairest way to judge this one.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Combines momentum with context, which is genuinely more useful than a standalone stochastic.
- The regime layer helps you avoid applying mean-reversion logic to a market that's actually trending.
- Fits naturally into a top-down workflow as a bias filter rather than a signal machine.
- Being a TradingView indicator, it's easy to overlay on your existing setup and blend with price action.

**Cons**
- With no published documentation, you're partly reverse-engineering the logic from what you see on the chart.
- Adding a regime layer means more interpretation — two things to read instead of one, and they can disagree.
- Like any oscillator-derived tool, it lags price by nature; it won't catch turns before they happen.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already like stochastics but want a smarter filter will get the most out of this. Swing traders working across multiple timeframes will appreciate a tool that helps decide whether a given market is trend-following or range-trading. It's less suited to pure momentum scalpers who want a single, fast trigger with no interpretation required.

## FAQ

**Is it a standalone buy/sell system?**
No. Treat it as a context and bias tool that supports a decision, not one that makes it for you.

**Does it repaint?**
There's no published documentation to confirm either way, so verify that yourself on a live chart before relying on its signals.

**What timeframes does it suit?**
That isn't documented. Test it on the timeframes you actually trade.

## Verdict

Stochastic Range Regime is a thoughtful idea executed as a filter rather than a signal generator. Blending momentum with a regime read is exactly the kind of nuance that separates a useful indicator from a noisy one, and the concept earns its place in a trend-trading workflow. The lack of documentation is the main friction — you'll spend some time understanding its behaviour on your own. Worth installing if you want smarter stochastic context; skip it if you're after a plug-and-play trigger.
