The name tells you most of what you need to know. Pivot Breakout/Retest Signals[JPT] is a trend indicator built around one of the oldest ideas in technical analysis: price breaks through a pivot level, then comes back to test that level, and the test is where the trade is taken. The indicator's job is to locate those pivot levels on the chart and mark the moments where price breaks them or returns to retest them, so you aren't drawing every level by hand.

That's the concept. It's a clean one, and it's worth being clear about what it is not. This is a structural signal tool, not a black-box prediction engine. It doesn't forecast where price goes next; it flags where a specific price behaviour — a break, or a return to a broken level — is occurring.

## What the indicator is actually doing

Pivot-based breakout and retest logic rests on a simple premise: a pivot high or pivot low is a price area where the market previously turned, which makes it a natural place for supply and demand to show up again. When price breaks through one of these levels, the break itself is one signal. When price then pulls back to that same level and holds, the retest is the second, often more interesting signal.

The indicator packages that workflow. Rather than you manually scanning for swing highs and lows and then waiting for a return to them, it identifies pivot levels and highlights breakout and retest events as they form. The "Signals" part of the name tells you the output is meant to be actionable — visual markers on the chart rather than a raw line to interpret.

If you need those specifics, read the indicator's own description on TradingView before you install it. What I can speak to honestly is the method it's built on and whether that method is worth your screen space.

## Why the retest framing matters

Most breakout indicators stop at the break. Price closes above resistance, arrow prints, done. The problem traders run into is that a large share of breakouts fail or fake out, and the break itself is often the worst possible entry — you're buying into a spike with your stop tucked just below the level you just broke.

Retest logic addresses that directly. By waiting for price to come back to the broken level before treating it as a valid signal, you're asking the market to confirm the level flipped from resistance to support (or the reverse). It's a more patient, more structural approach, and for traders who already think in terms of support and resistance it will feel like a natural extension of how they read a chart.

That's the real value proposition: not a novel trading idea, but an automated, consistent way to apply a sound one. Consistency is where manual pivot drawing falls apart — you get lazy, you skip levels, you anchor to the ones you remember.

## How you'd actually use it

The general workflow is straightforward and doesn't require me to invent settings. You put the indicator on your chart, let it map the pivot levels, and then watch for two things: breakout events, where price clears a level, and retest events, where price returns to a previously broken level. Your job is to judge context — trend direction, where the level sits relative to the broader structure, and whether the retest is holding or failing.

Treat the signals as a prompt to look, not a command to click. A breakout signal into a clean trending market and a breakout signal straight into a range are not the same trade, and no indicator will tell you which is which. The tool surfaces the setup; the decision stays with you.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Built on a genuinely sound structural concept — pivots, breaks, and retests are how trend continuation actually behaves.
- The retest component is the standout. It filters out the impulse-chasing entries that plague plain breakout tools.
- Automates level-finding, which keeps your analysis consistent instead of discretionary and forgetful.
- A clear trend-category tool that fits naturally alongside how most swing and intraday traders already read price.

**Cons**
- It's a signal layer, not a complete system. You still need your own entry, stop, and target logic.
- Retest tools can lag the initial move by design — if you want to catch the very first break, the retest wait will cost you some of it.
- Without published documentation in hand, tuning it to your instrument and timeframe requires reading the source description and some trial and error.
- Pivot-based methods are inherently reactive; in choppy conditions, levels break and retest constantly and the signal quality degrades.

## Who it's for

Swing traders and intraday traders who already trade support and resistance and want a consistent, automated way to spot breakout-and-retest setups. It suits people who prefer structure over indicators that try to predict, and who are comfortable making their own calls on context. If you're looking for a one-click system that tells you exactly when to buy and sell, this isn't that — and no honest indicator is.
