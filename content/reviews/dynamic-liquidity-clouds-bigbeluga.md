BigBeluga has built a following by packaging market-structure concepts into clean overlays, and Dynamic Liquidity Clouds is one of them. The name tells you most of what you need to know: it maps liquidity into a cloud that shifts as price develops, rather than drawing fixed horizontal levels that go stale. It's a trend-context tool first and a signal tool second, and that distinction matters when you decide whether to install it.

One caveat before anything else: I couldn't retrieve the official description or documentation for this one, so I'm not going to quote default settings, thresholds, lookback windows or signal logic I can't verify. What follows is an honest read of what the concept does and how a trader would actually use it — no invented numbers.

## What the cloud represents

Liquidity, as most traders use the term, is resting order flow — the clusters of orders and stop triggers that sit at prices where participants have committed. On a chart, those areas act like magnets and barriers at the same time. Price tends to gravitate toward them, and when it reaches them it frequently reacts, accelerates, or reverses.

Dynamic Liquidity Clouds visualises that idea as a shaded band rather than a line. The "dynamic" part is the point: the cloud is not static. It evolves with price action, so the zones you're watching are the ones currently in play, not a level from three weeks ago that may or may not still matter. As shown in the chart above, the cloud gives you a sense of where the market's attention is concentrated relative to where price is trading.

## Where it fits in your workflow

Think of this as a context layer, not an entry trigger. The sensible way to use it:

- **Directional bias.** When price holds above the cloud, the liquidity structure is leaning bullish; below it, bearish. That's your backdrop, not your trade.
- **Reaction zones.** The edges of the cloud are the places to watch for a response — a rejection, a clean break, a retest. Whether price respects or slices through a zone is information.
- **Confluence.** Use it alongside your existing setup. If your entry logic fires into the top of a cloud, you at least know you're trading into resistance rather than pretending it isn't there.

What it won't do is tell you exactly when to click buy. Anyone expecting a clean arrow-per-signal tool will be disappointed. That's a feature of the category, not a flaw in this specific indicator.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- The dynamic approach is genuinely more useful than static support/resistance lines, which accumulate clutter and lose relevance. A cloud that updates keeps the chart honest.
- It's a clean visual — shading communicates the idea faster than a dozen horizontal lines ever will.
- It slots naturally into a multi-timeframe workflow: bias from the cloud, execution from your own setup.
- BigBeluga's indicators are generally well-built on TradingView, and this fits that pattern.

**Cons**

- Clouds are inherently interpretive. Where a zone "starts" and "ends" is a judgement call, and two traders can read the same shading differently.
- Like most liquidity-based tools, it's better at describing the environment than timing entries. It won't replace a trigger.
- Without documented logic in front of me, I can't vouch for how it behaves across different volatility regimes — treat that as an open question and test it on your own instruments.
- If you already run a solid supply/demand or volume-profile overlay, the incremental value may be smaller than you'd hope.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who read structure and want a visual map of where liquidity sits relative to price. Swing and intraday traders on liquid instruments will get the most out of it. If you're a systematic trader who needs a hard-coded rule, this isn't aimed at you — it's a decision-support overlay, not a mechanical system.

## FAQ

I can't confirm that without the documentation, so I won't claim either way. If that matters to you, watch it live before committing capital.

**Can I use it on any timeframe?**
The concept is timeframe-agnostic, but liquidity structure reads differently on a five-minute chart than a daily one. Apply it where your trading already lives.

**Is it a buy/sell signal indicator?**
No. It's a context tool. The signal is your job.

## Verdict

Dynamic Liquidity Clouds earns its place as a structure overlay. It takes a concept traders already reason about — liquidity — and renders it in a way that updates with the market instead of fossilising on the chart. The ceiling is set by the category: it informs your read rather than making decisions for you, and the absence of public documentation means you'll need to do a little homework yourself.

If you trade structure discretionarily and want a cleaner map of where price is likely to react, it's worth installing and watching for a week. If you need mechanical triggers, look elsewhere.
