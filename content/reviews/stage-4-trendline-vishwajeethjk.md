**Verdict first:** a clean, purpose-built trendline tool that does one job and stays out of the way. Four stars — not because it's flashy, but because it doesn't pretend to be.

## What it actually does

Stage 4 Trendline is a trend indicator built around the trendline — the oldest tool in technical analysis, and still the one most traders draw by hand. The premise is straightforward: instead of you marking support and resistance lines manually, the indicator handles the trendline work on the chart for you.

That's the whole idea. It sits in the Trend category and gives you a structural read on direction rather than a signal to fire on. As shown in the chart above, the output is meant to be read as a line/level on price — a reference for where the trend is, not a buy/sell arrow.

I want to be honest about scope here: no official documentation was retrievable for this script, so I'm not going to invent default settings, thresholds, or a "best timeframe" for you. What follows is what the tool is by design, not a spec sheet.

## Why a trendline tool is worth a slot on the chart

Trendlines are subjective. Two traders look at the same swing highs and draw two different lines — one steep, one shallow, one anchored to a wick, one to a close. That subjectivity is exactly where trends get misread.

An automated trendline removes the "which swing do I anchor to" debate. You get a consistent line drawn by fixed logic every time, on every chart. Whether you agree with where it lands, at least it lands in the same place twice. For anyone who's ever redrawn a trendline three times to make a setup "work," that consistency is the real value.

## How to use it

Treat it as a structural filter, not an entry trigger:

- **Direction bias.** Use the trendline to decide whether you're looking for longs or shorts before you look at anything else. Price above the line, you're hunting with the trend; below, against it.
- **Break context.** A close through the line is information — a potential shift in structure — not an automatic trade. Confirm with price action or another tool.
- **Confluence.** Where the trendline meets a level you already care about (a prior swing, a round number), that's where setups get interesting. The line is one input, not the whole thesis.

If you're the type who trades off a single indicator's signal, this isn't that. It's a map, not a compass.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Removes the subjectivity of hand-drawn trendlines — consistent logic, same line every time.
- Trend-category tool that stays in its lane. It doesn't clutter the chart with a dozen overlays.
- Pairs naturally with any entry method you already run; it doesn't fight your existing process.
- The "Stage 4" framing suggests a structured, staged approach to trend reading rather than a one-shot signal — useful if you like a method with steps.

**Cons**
- No retrievable documentation means you're partly reverse-engineering how the line is drawn. That's a real friction point for newer traders.
- Trendline tools are lagging by nature — they describe structure after it forms, so they won't catch the turn before it happens.
- Automated trendlines can disagree with your own reading on choppy or range-bound charts; expect to override it sometimes.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already think in terms of trend structure and want a consistent line to anchor their analysis. It suits swing and position traders more than scalpers — trendline logic needs room to breathe, and on very short timeframes the line gets noisy. If you're a systematic trader wanting a coded signal with defined parameters, this isn't built for that.
