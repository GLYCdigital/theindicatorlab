Order blocks are one of those concepts that sound simple until you try to trade them. A candle or cluster of candles where institutions supposedly left orders behind — price returns, and you either get a bounce or you don't. The hard part has never been spotting the block. It's knowing whether the block is still worth trusting by the time price comes back to it.

Order Block Holding Power [AlgoNorth] is built around that second question. It's a trend-category tool that, based on its name and framing, sets out to measure the strength — the "holding power" — of order blocks rather than just drawing boxes on your chart. That's a meaningful distinction, and it's the reason this one earns a solid four stars even though I can only speak to it in general terms here.

## What it's actually trying to solve

Most order-block indicators are drawing tools. They detect a qualifying candle pattern, paint a zone, and leave the interpretation to you. The problem is that not all blocks are equal. A block that formed on a strong displacement and hasn't been retested carries more weight than one that's been chopped through three times. A tool that tries to quantify that difference is addressing a real gap.

Holding power, as a concept, is about resilience. Does the zone absorb price and push back, or does it give way quietly? An indicator that scores that — whatever internal method AlgoNorth uses — is giving you a ranking layer on top of the raw detection. That's the value proposition, and it's a sensible one for anyone who already trades order blocks and is tired of treating every zone as identical.

## Where it fits in a workflow

The natural use is not as a standalone signal generator. Think of it as a filter. You have your directional bias from trend structure — the indicator lives in the Trend category, so it's presumably oriented around directional context rather than pure mean reversion. Then, when price approaches a block, the holding-power read tells you whether that zone deserves attention or whether you should stand aside.

That layered approach is how most discretionary traders already work, just with fewer numbers attached. The tool formalises the "is this zone still good?" gut check. As shown in the chart above, the visual is clean enough to read at a glance, which matters — an indicator that adds cognitive load to your chart defeats the purpose.

## The honest trade-offs

**Pros**

- Addresses the actual weakness in order-block trading: zone quality, not zone detection.
- Sits in the trend framework, so it complements directional strategies rather than fighting them.
- Conceptually reduces the "every block looks the same" problem that plagues manual order-block charts.

**Cons**

- Order-block logic is inherently discretionary and broker-agnostic; any automated holding-power score is an approximation, not a truth.
- Without documented methodology, you're trusting the black box. That's fine for a filter, less fine if you lean on it heavily.
- It won't teach you order blocks. If you don't already understand displacement, mitigation and retests, the output will be noise.

## Who it's for

This is for traders who already have an order-block or smart-money workflow and want a second opinion on zone strength. Swing and intraday traders working off structure will get the most from it. If you're a pure indicator-signal trader looking for entries handed to you, this isn't that — and it isn't pretending to be.

## FAQ

**Does it draw order blocks for me?**
The tool is centred on holding power, so the reasonable expectation is that it identifies blocks and scores them. Don't buy it purely as a zone-detection tool without checking the chart.

**Can I use it alone?**
You can, but holding power is context, not a trigger. Treat it as a filter alongside your own structure read.

**Does it repaint?**
I can't confirm this without the documentation, and I won't guess. Verify on a live chart before committing capital.

## Verdict

Order Block Holding Power [AlgoNorth] takes a genuinely useful angle: it asks how strong a block is, not just where it is. That's the right question, and the execution — as far as the concept goes — is coherent. It loses a star for opacity: without clear methodology, you're extending trust to a score you can't audit. Use it as a filter, not an oracle, and it earns its place on the chart.
