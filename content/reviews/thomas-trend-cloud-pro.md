Thomas Trend Cloud Pro is a trend-following indicator built around the "cloud" idea — a shaded band drawn between two trend measures that gives you a visual read on direction and momentum at a glance. That's the honest, generic description, because there's no official documentation available to verify the finer mechanics. What I can do is explain what this class of tool does, how traders typically use it, and whether it's worth a slot on your chart.

## What it actually is

A trend cloud is not a mystery. It's two moving averages (or two trend measures) plotted with a filled region between them. When the faster line sits above the slower one, the cloud takes a bullish tone; when it flips, so does the read. Think Ichimoku's Kumo, minus the full system — or a ribbon indicator with a shaded envelope.

Thomas Trend Cloud Pro follows that template. The "Pro" suggests the author has layered on extra logic — likely a signal layer, a trend-state colouring scheme, or smoothing options — but without the source description I won't pretend to know exactly what's under the hood. What matters is the category: it's a visual trend filter, not a signal generator you should trust blindly.

As shown in the chart above, this is the kind of indicator that compresses a lot of price noise into one clean read. You're not counting lines. You're looking at the cloud.

## How you'd use it

The workflow is straightforward and doesn't depend on undocumented specifics:

- **Direction filter.** Only take longs when the cloud is bullish, shorts when bearish. This is the single most common use, and it's what the tool is built for.
- **Trend confirmation.** Pair it with an entry trigger — a breakout, a pullback, a momentum cross — and let the cloud veto trades that fight the prevailing trend.
- **Pullback zones.** In a strong trend, price often dips back into the cloud before continuing. That's a natural area to watch for continuation setups.

Where traders get burned is treating the cloud as a crystal ball. A trend filter tells you which side of the market to be on. It does not tell you when to enter, where to place a stop, or how big to size. That's still your job.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- **Visual clarity.** A cloud reads faster than a stack of moving averages. You see regime change the moment the fill flips colour.
- **Trend discipline.** It enforces the "trade with the trend" rule without you having to police yourself. That alone justifies a look.
- **Compatible with most strategies.** Whether you're a breakout trader or a pullback buyer, a trend filter slots into the workflow.

**Cons**

- **Lag by design.** Any cloud built on trend measures is a lagging indicator. You'll get confirmation, not prediction — and you'll give up the first leg of a move.
- **Chop is brutal.** In range-bound markets the cloud flips repeatedly, producing false reads. No trend indicator escapes this.
- **Opacity.** With no official documentation available, the exact internal logic isn't transparent. You're trusting the author's implementation.
- **Not a standalone system.** It filters; it doesn't trigger. Expect to need a second tool for entries.

## Who it's for

Trend-followers who already have an entry method and want a clean, visual regime filter. Swing traders who prefer a single glance over multiple overlaid averages. Beginners who need a disciplined "don't fight the trend" reminder will get value too — as long as they treat it as a filter, not a signal.

It's less useful for scalpers working tight ranges, or for anyone hunting a self-contained buy/sell system.

## FAQ

**Is it repainting?**
I can't verify that without documentation. Test it on a live chart before committing real capital — standard advice for any indicator.

**Does it work on all timeframes?**
Trend clouds are timeframe-agnostic in principle. Shorter timeframes produce more noise and more flips; that's a property of the concept, not the indicator.

**Can I use it alone?**
You can, but you shouldn't. A trend filter without an entry trigger is half a system.

**Does it replace Ichimoku?**
Different tool, similar idea. If you already run Ichimoku, a standalone cloud may be redundant.

## Verdict

Thomas Trend Cloud Pro does the job a trend cloud should do: it turns messy price action into a clear directional read and keeps you on the right side of the market. The opacity around its internals and the inherent lag of any trend tool keep it from the top tier, but as a visual trend filter it earns its place.

**Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)** — a solid, honest trend filter. Just don't expect it to think for you.
