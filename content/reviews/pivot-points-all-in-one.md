# Pivot_Points_All_In_One Review

Pivot point indicators are a dime a dozen on TradingView, and many are little more than the classic floor formula dropped onto a daily chart. *Pivot_Points_All_In_One* claims to be more than that. Here's a breakdown of what it offers and where it falls short.

## What This Indicator Actually Does

This isn't just one pivot calculation. It bundles **seven different pivot methods** into a single overlay: Classic, Fibonacci, Camarilla, Woodie, DeMark, Floor, and a custom "Dynamic" mode. You toggle between them via the settings. It calculates support/resistance levels based on the timeframe you choose (daily, weekly, monthly, or custom). The levels are plotted as horizontal lines, with the central pivot (PP) highlighted more prominently.

The lines update when a new period starts, and levels are fixed once the bar closes.

## Key Features That Stand Out

- **Multi-method selector:** Most pivot indicators lock you into one formula. This one lets you switch between methods without adding a second indicator — useful if you want Fibonacci for trending conditions and Camarilla for ranging ones.
- **Auto-timeframe detection:** It picks up your chart's timeframe and adjusts levels accordingly. This reduces manual setup.
- **Customizable line style:** You can change colors, widths, and dash styles for each level. Minor, but visual clarity matters when you have several lines on screen.
- **Show only selected levels:** Hide S1, S2, R1, R2, etc., individually — handy if you'd rather not display levels you rarely reference.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

- **Method:** The indicator supports Classic, Fibonacci, Camarilla, Woodie, DeMark, Floor, and a custom "Dynamic" mode. Which one you pick depends on your approach — Fibonacci and Camarilla are common choices for trending and range-bound conditions respectively.
- **Timeframe:** Daily, weekly, monthly, or custom. Daily is the typical default for swing trading; weekly suits position trading on indices.
- **Line style:** Solid for PP, dashed for S1/R1, dotted for S2/R2 is a common convention that helps you prioritize at a glance.
- **Show extended levels:** Turning this off keeps the chart cleaner during normal price action; leaving it on is more relevant if you trade breakouts.

## How to Actually Trade With It

The indicator doesn't generate buy/sell signals — it gives you the *zones*. A common approach:

**Long entry:** Price pulls back to S1 or S2, shows a bullish candlestick pattern (hammer, engulfing), and RSI is above 30. Stop below the level. Target R1 or PP.

**Short entry:** Price rallies to R1 or R2, forms a bearish reversal bar, and RSI is below 70. Stop above the level. Target PP or S1.

**Breakout play:** If price closes decisively above R2 with volume, it may run to R3. Same logic for breakdowns below S2. Use the extended levels here.

Pairing it with a momentum oscillator like MACD can help confirm entries.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**
- Seven methods in one indicator = clean workspace.
- Levels are fixed after the period closes.
- Works on any timeframe without manual recalculation.
- Lightweight — doesn't slow down your chart.

**Cons:**
- No alerts for level touches. You have to manually monitor or set price alerts.
- The "Dynamic" mode is poorly documented and unreliable in practice.
- Can get noisy on lower timeframes (1m, 5m). Stick to 1H or higher.

## Who It's For

This is for **swing traders and intraday trend followers** who want a quick reference for key price levels. If you trade 5-minute scalps, you'll find the lines too frequent and less meaningful. If you're a long-term position trader, the weekly/monthly pivot modes are excellent. It's also useful for beginners learning how pivots work across different calculation methods.

## Better Alternatives

- **Auto Pivot Points** (by LuxAlgo): More polished, with alerts and dynamic zones. Costs money, though.
- **Pivot Points High Low** (free): Simpler — just classic pivots based on high/low. Less flexible but cleaner.
- **Daily Pivot** (included in TradingView's built-in indicators): Does the job for basic daily pivots, but no Fibonacci or Camarilla.

## Final Verdict

**Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)**

*Pivot_Points_All_In_One* delivers what it promises: a comprehensive pivot tool without the bloat. It's not flashy, but it's reliable. The lack of alerts is the biggest miss, and the Dynamic mode feels unfinished. Still, for a free indicator that handles seven pivot methods cleanly, it's a solid addition to any swing trader's toolbox. If you need alerts or more automation, look at the paid alternatives. Otherwise, this is a keeper.
