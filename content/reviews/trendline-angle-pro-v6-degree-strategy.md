Most trendline tools are visual aids. You draw a line, you eyeball it, you decide whether the slope "looks steep." Trendline Angle Pro V6 takes a different route: it quantifies the angle of a trendline in degrees and wraps that measurement into a strategy framework. That single design choice — turning slope into a number — is what makes it worth a look, and it's also where its limits start.

A quick note on scope: this review is based on the indicator's concept and stated purpose. I have not run it, so treat the specifics below as a description of what the tool is built to do, not a performance claim.

## What it actually does

The core idea is straightforward. A trendline's meaning depends heavily on its slope, but slope is slippery when you compare across timeframes or instruments — a line that looks aggressive on one chart can be mild on another. By expressing the trendline as an angle in degrees, the indicator gives you a standardized read on how fast a trend is advancing or decaying.

The "Strategy" in the name signals the second half: the angle isn't just displayed, it feeds a rule-based layer that can flag conditions based on trendline behavior. In practice that means the tool sits somewhere between a pure drawing utility and an automated signal generator.

## Where the angle approach earns its keep

The real argument for measuring trendlines in degrees is objectivity. When you're managing a position, a steep trendline and a shallow one imply very different things about momentum and about how much room you have before the structure breaks. Putting a number on that removes a chunk of the guesswork.

It also helps with consistency. If you trade multiple markets or timeframes, a degree-based read lets you compare setups on something closer to equal footing rather than judging each chart by feel. That's a genuinely useful discipline, and it's the reason the concept has fans.

The strategy layer adds a second benefit: it turns a subjective chart annotation into something testable and repeatable. Whether or not you agree with its logic, having rules is better than not having them.

## How you'd actually use it

The workflow implied by the design is: identify the prevailing trendline, let the indicator measure its angle, then use the strategy output as a filter rather than an oracle. The angle reading is best treated as context — a steepening slope suggests momentum is building, a flattening one suggests the trend is losing energy — while the strategy signals tell you when the tool thinks something actionable is happening.

Used that way, it slots naturally into a trend-following process. It won't tell you where to put a stop or how to size a position, and it shouldn't be expected to.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Quantifies trendline slope, removing a subjective judgment call.
- The degree-based framing is comparable across charts, which most drawing tools can't offer.
- Bundles measurement and a strategy layer in one indicator instead of forcing you to stitch two tools together.
- Conceptually clean — the logic is easy to explain, which matters if you're building a repeatable process.

**Cons**
- "Strategy" indicators invite over-trust. An angle reading is descriptive, not predictive, and the line between the two can blur fast.
- Trendlines themselves are still drawn or derived by rules you have to understand; the angle is only as good as the line behind it.
- Because I have no documented settings to point to here, anyone considering it should read the author's own notes carefully before wiring it into a system.
- Naming it "V6" hints at iteration — fine, but confirm what changed before assuming the current version behaves like an older one you may have seen.

## Who it's for

This suits discretionary trend traders who already draw trendlines and want a more objective read on slope, and systematic traders who like rule-based filters but still want a visual anchor on the chart. If you trade purely off price action with no interest in angle measurement, it's probably more machinery than you need.

## FAQ

**Is the angle measured in a fixed way across timeframes?**
The whole point of degrees is standardization, but how a given indicator normalizes for chart scaling is a detail you should confirm from the author's documentation rather than assume.

**Does the strategy layer generate buy/sell signals?**
The name implies rule-based output tied to trendline behavior. Treat any such output as a filter within your own process, not a standalone system.

**Can I use it on any market?**
Nothing about the concept is instrument-specific. Whether it fits your market depends on how cleanly trendlines form there.

## Verdict

Trendline Angle Pro V6 tackles a real problem — slope is subjective, and most tools leave it that way. Turning the trendline into a measured angle and pairing it with a strategy layer is a coherent, useful idea, and for trend traders who want more objectivity it's a solid addition to the toolkit. It loses a star for the usual risk with strategy-labeled indicators: the output looks more authoritative than a descriptive measurement really is. Use the angle as context, keep your own risk rules, and it earns its place.
