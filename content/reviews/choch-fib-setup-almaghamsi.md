Most indicators on TradingView are variations on the same handful of ideas repackaged under a new name. This one at least has a clear thesis: it lives at the intersection of two concepts that discretionary traders already use by hand — market structure shifts and Fibonacci retracements. The name tells you almost everything. CHoCH is change of character, the moment a trend's internal structure breaks and control arguably changes hands. "Fib Setup" is the retracement framework layered on top. Almaghamsi is the author.

What follows is an honest read of what that combination implies, based strictly on what the tool advertises. There is no official documentation to lean on here, and I have not run it, so treat this as a conceptual review rather than a performance claim.

## The idea underneath it

Change of character is a market-structure concept, not a math formula. In plain terms, an uptrend is a sequence of higher highs and higher lows. When price breaks below the most recent higher low, that sequence is broken — that's the CHoCH. It's the first credible signal that the pullback isn't a pullback anymore. The indicator's job is to flag those breaks objectively rather than leaving you to eyeball swing points.

The Fibonacci half is where the setup comes together. Once a CHoCH prints, the natural question is "where do I enter if this is real?" Fib retracement gives you a structured answer — pull back into a defined zone and look for continuation. Pairing the two means the tool is trying to answer both "has the trend changed?" and "where's a sensible entry?" in one overlay.

That's a coherent workflow. It's also one that experienced price-action traders do manually every day, which is exactly the audience this is aimed at.

## What it brings to a chart

The value of a structure indicator is objectivity. Swing highs and lows are obvious in hindsight and maddeningly slippery in real time — you second-guess whether a wick counts, whether the low held, whether the break was meaningful. A tool that marks CHoCH events for you removes that hesitation and makes the concept mechanical.

Layering Fib levels onto those events is the differentiator. Instead of a bare signal arrow, you get context: the structure break *and* a mapped retracement zone to watch. As shown in the chart above, the result is a cleaner decision framework than either concept gives you alone.

## How you'd actually use it

The intended flow is straightforward. Wait for the indicator to flag a change of character. Treat that as your structural cue — the prior trend's rhythm has broken. Then watch the Fib retracement drawn off that move to identify where a continuation entry might set up. The CHoCH tells you the *direction of interest*; the Fib zone tells you the *price area*.

The discipline it encourages is patience. You're not chasing the break — you're waiting for the retracement into a defined level. That alone tends to improve trade quality for traders who otherwise enter on impulse.

What it won't do is tell you the trend will continue. A CHoCH can fail, and a Fib level can be sliced through. The tool describes structure; it doesn't guarantee outcomes.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Combines two genuinely complementary concepts rather than stacking redundant signals.
- Automates the swing-point judgment that trips up discretionary structure traders.
- Gives both a directional cue and a price zone, which is more actionable than a lone alert.
- Fits naturally into a price-action or smart-money style workflow.

**Cons**
- Market-structure tools are notoriously sensitive to how swings are defined; if the marking doesn't match your read of the chart, the signals fight you.
- Fib retracements are only as good as the swing they're drawn from — a misidentified leg produces misleading levels.
- With no published documentation to reference, new users are largely on their own to interpret the markings.
- It's an overlay concept, not a complete system — risk management and confirmation are still yours to supply.

## Who it's for

Price-action and smart-money traders who already think in terms of structure breaks and retracements will feel at home immediately. Discretionary traders who want their CHoCH reads made mechanical will get the most out of it. It's less suited to pure momentum or indicator-crossover traders, and beginners should understand market structure before trusting any automated marking of it.

## FAQ

**Does it repaint?** Not something I can confirm without documentation. Test it on a demo chart and watch whether past signals stay put.

**Can it be used on any timeframe?** Structure concepts apply across timeframes in principle, but verify the markings behave sensibly on the timeframe you trade before relying on them.

**Does it replace my own analysis?** No. It's a structure-mapping aid, not a signal service.

## Verdict

This is a thoughtful pairing of two ideas that belong together, and it earns points for addressing both the "when" and the "where" of a trade rather than just firing arrows. The lack of documentation and the inherent subjectivity of structure detection keep it from the top tier — you'll need to calibrate it to your own read of the market. But for a structure-driven trader, it's a legitimate addition to the toolkit.
