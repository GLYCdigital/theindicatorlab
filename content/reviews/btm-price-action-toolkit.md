"Price action toolkit" is one of those names that promises a lot and often delivers a repackaged moving average. This one sits in TradingView's trend category, so the honest starting point is this: treat it as a trend-reading framework built around price action, not as a signal generator you switch on and obey.

A note on scope up front. There's no official documentation I can point to for this indicator, so I'm not going to invent default settings, thresholds, or "best timeframe" claims. What follows is a grounded look at what a price-action trend toolkit of this type is for, how you'd sensibly work with it, and where it tends to fall short.

## What it actually is

The name splits into two useful halves. "Price action" means the tool is concerned with the raw behaviour of the chart — how price structures itself, where it pauses, and how it resolves. "Trend" is the category TradingView files it under, which tells you the intended job: helping you orient around direction and structure rather than hunting isolated reversal signals.

In practice, a toolkit in this slot usually layers price-action logic over the chart to give you a cleaner read of trend — structure, momentum bias, and the points where price action confirms or contradicts the prevailing direction. It's a lens, not an oracle. If you're expecting a single arrow that tells you when to buy, you're looking at the wrong kind of tool.

## Where it fits in a workflow

The realistic use case is context. You open a chart, and before you commit to a bias you want a neutral read on trend structure and price behaviour. That's the gap this fills. It's the layer that stops you from fighting the market because your favourite oscillator said so.

As the MACD chart above suggests, it pairs naturally with momentum tools — you use price action to establish what's happening structurally, then use a momentum indicator to judge whether that structure has conviction behind it. The two answering different questions is the whole point.

The other sensible application is filtering. If you run a system with entry triggers, a trend-context toolkit can act as a gate: only take longs when structure supports it, only take shorts when it doesn't. That's a far more defensible use than treating the toolkit as the trigger itself.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- It's a trend-context tool, which is where most traders actually lose money — not on entries, but on being on the wrong side of structure.
- Price-action framing keeps you anchored to the chart rather than to a derived number, which tends to age better across different markets.
- Sitting in the trend category means it's designed to complement, not duplicate, momentum and volatility tools.

**Cons**

- Without published documentation, you're relying on the chart and the author's own notes to understand exactly what's being plotted. That's a real friction point for anyone who wants to know the logic before trusting it.
- "Toolkit" implies multiple components. More moving parts means more time spent learning the tool before it earns its place.
- Price-action interpretation is inherently discretionary. A toolkit can organise the information, but it can't remove the judgement call — and anyone selling it as a mechanical system is overselling.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who read charts for a living-ish — swing and intraday traders who already think in terms of structure and trend and want a cleaner, consolidated view of price behaviour. It's less suited to pure system traders who need every input defined and backtestable, and it's overkill for someone running a single moving-average crossover.

## FAQ

**Is this a buy/sell signal indicator?**
No — it's categorised as a trend tool built around price action. Treat it as context, not an entry trigger.

**What timeframe does it work best on?**
There's no documented answer, and I'm not going to make one up. Trend tools generally behave differently across timeframes, so test the read on the timeframes you actually trade.

**Does it replace my momentum indicator?**
No. They answer different questions — structure versus momentum. Use them together, as the chart above illustrates.

**Can I backtest it mechanically?**
Price-action tools lean discretionary by nature, so mechanical backtesting is awkward. If that's a hard requirement, look elsewhere.

## Verdict

A solid, honest trend-context tool that does the unglamorous job of helping you read price structure — not a miracle signal engine, and it doesn't pretend to be. The lack of documentation is the main thing holding it back from a higher score, because you have to earn the understanding yourself. If you're a discretionary trader who wants a cleaner structural read and you're willing to put in the chart time, it earns its place. If you need documented rules and mechanical signals, it isn't for you.
