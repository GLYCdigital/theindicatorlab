Some TradingView tools are easy to review because the author documents everything. This one isn't. The ICT Pullback Setup / Smart OTE Fibonacci Levels indicator from RadixAlgo arrived here without an official description or docs, so treat this as a conceptual review of the ICT pullback/OTE idea rather than a parameter-by-parameter walkthrough. I'm not going to invent settings, thresholds or backtest numbers to fill space. There's no honest way to do that.

## What the name tells you

The title does most of the explaining. "ICT" means Inner Circle Trader methodology, which centres on liquidity, market structure and the idea that price often retraces into a "sweet spot" before continuing with the larger move. "OTE" is Optimal Trade Entry — the Fibonacci retracement zone where ICT traders look for pullback entries in the direction of the prevailing trend. "Pullback Setup" says the tool is built to flag those retracements rather than trend reversals.

Put together, this is a Fibonacci-based pullback marker for traders running an ICT-style workflow. It plots what it considers meaningful retracement levels and, going by the "Smart" in the name, tries to filter or prioritise which levels actually matter instead of dumping every Fibonacci line on your chart. The exact filtering logic isn't documented here, so I can't tell you how it decides. That's a real limitation of this review, not a knock on the tool itself.

## Where it fits in the workflow

A fib retracement tool on its own is passive — it draws lines and leaves you to decide. An ICT pullback indicator is more directional: it's telling you "price has pulled back into a zone worth watching, in the context of a trend." That's the practical difference, and it's why these tools appeal to discretionary traders who already think in terms of structure and liquidity rather than raw indicator crossovers.

The sensible way to use something like this is as a zone-mapping layer, not a signal generator. You'd combine it with your own read on market structure — is the higher-timeframe trend intact? Where are the obvious liquidity pools? Is price retracing into a level with prior significance? The fib levels give you a reference frame; the decision stays with you. If you're expecting it to print buy/sell arrows you can follow blindly, this isn't that kind of tool, and the ICT framework isn't built for that either.

## Pros

- **Conceptually coherent.** It's grounded in a specific, well-known methodology rather than being a generic fib tool with ICT branding slapped on. If you trade ICT, the logic will feel familiar.
- **Reduces chart clutter.** The "Smart" framing suggests it aims to surface the levels that matter rather than every retracement line. For a methodology that can drown a chart in fibs, that's welcome.
- **Trend-aligned by design.** It's built around pullbacks *within* a trend, which keeps you thinking about direction rather than chasing reversals.
- **Category fit.** It's filed under Trend, and that's honest — the tool assumes a prevailing direction and works with it.

## Cons

- **Documentation gap.** Without a published description, you're reverse-engineering the logic from what appears on the chart. For a tool whose value hinges on *which* levels it chooses, that's a meaningful downside.
- **Not a standalone system.** Fibonacci retracements are context-dependent. Used without a structure read, the levels are just lines.
- **ICT-specific.** If you don't subscribe to the ICT framework, the terminology and zone logic will feel like extra steps for something a standard fib retracement tool already does.
- **Discretion required.** There's no mechanical trigger here — it's an interpretive tool, which means results depend heavily on the trader using it.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already work in the ICT/price-action space and want a cleaner way to mark optimal trade entry zones during pullbacks. It suits people who think in terms of structure, liquidity and retracement depth, and who are comfortable making the final call themselves. It's not for systematic traders looking for a rules-based entry signal, and it's not for beginners who haven't internalised what a pullback in a trend actually looks like.

Based on the name and category, no — it's a level-mapping/pullback tool, not a signal generator. You interpret the zones.

**Can I use it outside ICT methodology?**
You can, but the value is in the OTE/pullback framework. A standard Fibonacci retracement tool covers the generic use case.

I can't say without documentation, so I won't guess. Check the author's page and test on a demo before relying on it.

## Verdict

The ICT Pullback Setup / Smart OTE Fibonacci Levels indicator is a competent-looking implementation of a specific, legitimate methodology. It earns four stars on concept and category fit — the idea is sound, it targets a real workflow, and the "smart" filtering is a sensible ambition for a fib tool. It doesn't get five because the missing documentation leaves too much to interpretation, and a tool built on deciding *which* levels matter really needs to explain its logic. If you're an ICT trader who wants cleaner OTE mapping and you're willing to reverse-engineer the behaviour, it's worth a look. If you want documented, mechanical signals, keep looking.
