**Multi Asset SMT Divergence** is not a typical divergence indicator. Most divergence tools compare price against an oscillator on a single chart. This one takes a different route: it compares price action between two correlated assets (for example, BTC against ETH, or EURUSD against USDCHF) to identify structural divergences.

It is built around the Smart Money Concepts (SMC) approach, but familiarity with ICT methodology is not a prerequisite for using it.

---

### What This Indicator Actually Does

The indicator scans two assets you select and plots arrows or markers when one asset makes a higher high while the other makes a lower high, or vice versa for lows. This is an **SMT divergence** — a concept from the ICT trading methodology. The premise is that when correlated assets diverge, it can signal a reversal or acceleration in the direction of the weaker asset.

A bearish SMT divergence on BTC/USDT against ETH/USDT would show BTC pushing to a new high while ETH fails to confirm. That failure to confirm is the core signal the tool is designed to surface.

---

### Key Features

- **Cross-asset divergence detection** – Compares any two tickers you pair, rather than a single instrument against an oscillator.
- **Customizable correlation pairs** – Pre-loaded pairs such as BTC/ETH, EUR/USD, and SPX/NDX, with the option to input any two symbols.
- **Signal plotting** – Arrows print once the divergence is confirmed.
- **Alert system** – Alerts can be set for new divergences, which is uncommon among SMT-based tools.
- **Multi-timeframe support** – Operates across timeframes.

---

### Settings and How to Tune Them

- **Asset Pair**: Select two correlated instruments. Crypto traders typically pair BTC with ETH; forex traders often pair EURUSD with USDCHF or GBPUSD with USDJPY.
- **Lookback Period**: Controls how far back the indicator scans for swing structure. Shorter lookbacks tend to produce more noise; longer lookbacks can lag early signals.
- **Divergence Sensitivity**: Adjusts how strict the divergence comparison is. Lower sensitivity filters out more setups.
- **Timeframe**: The indicator runs on any timeframe. Lower timeframes tend to produce more signals and more noise; higher timeframes produce fewer signals.
- **Confirmation**: Enable "Show Labels" to display the exact price levels of the divergence.

---

### How to Use It for Entries and Exits

**Entry logic**:
1. Wait for a divergence arrow to appear on the stronger asset — the one making the higher high or lower low.
2. Confirm with price action: a break of a short-term trendline or a candlestick reversal pattern such as a pin bar or engulfing candle.
3. Enter in the direction of the divergence — short on bearish divergence, long on bullish divergence.

**Exit logic**:
- Use the opposite asset's recent swing low or high as a target. If BTC shows bearish divergence against ETH, the ETH swing low is a reference target.
- Place the stop loss above the last swing high for shorts, or below the last swing low for longs.

---

### Pros and Cons

**Pros**:
- Genuinely unique approach — fills a gap for inter-market divergence traders.
- Signals plot on confirmation rather than shifting afterward.
- Works well on correlated pairs such as BTC/ETH, EUR/USD, and SPX/NDX.
- Alerts are useful for catching moves when you are away from the screen.

**Cons**:
- High false signal rate on low-correlation pairs (for example, BTC against Gold).
- No built-in risk management or position sizing.
- Requires manual interpretation — not a "set and forget" system.
- A learning curve for new SMT users.

---

### Who It’s For

This indicator suits **intermediate to advanced traders** who already understand correlation and divergence concepts. If you trade BTC and ETH, or EURUSD and USDCHF, the tool is built for that use case. Beginners may struggle with false signals and should pair it with other confluences.

It is **not** for pure scalpers or traders who want automated entries. Decisions are made by reading the chart.

---

### Alternatives

- **Smart Money Divergence by LuxAlgo**: More comprehensive for ICT traders, but more expensive and reportedly repaints on some settings.
- **Divergence Indicator by LonesomeTheBlue**: Free and non-repainting, but single-asset only. A reasonable option for beginners.
- **Correlation Matrix by QuantNomad**: Shows correlation heatmaps, but no divergence signals.

For a simple, free alternative, standard RSI divergence on each asset separately will do — but it will not produce the SMT comparison.

### FAQ

**Q: Does this indicator repaint?**
A: Signals are designed to print on confirmation and remain fixed.

**Q: Can I use it for crypto?**
A: Yes. It is well suited to BTC/ETH, ETH/SOL, or any highly correlated pair. Low-correlation pairs are less appropriate.

**Q: What timeframe should I use?**
A: The indicator runs on any timeframe. Lower timeframes produce more signals and more noise; higher timeframes produce fewer signals.

**Q: How do I set up the pair?**
A: In the input settings, change `Symbol 1` and `Symbol 2` to any two tickers. Make sure they are on the same timeframe.

**Q: Is it worth the price?**
A: It is not free. Whether it is worth it depends on how regularly you trade correlated assets.

---

### Final Verdict

Multi Asset SMT Divergence is a solid, niche tool that does what it promises. It is not perfect — false signals on less correlated pairs are a real limitation, and it demands manual confirmation. For traders who already use SMT concepts or trade correlated markets, it is a useful addition to the toolbox. The learning curve and the absence of integrated risk management are the main drawbacks.

**Would I install it?** Yes, if you are committed to learning inter-market divergence. For casual traders, simpler tools will likely serve better.
