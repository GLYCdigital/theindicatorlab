Let me cut straight to it: Stochastic_Mtf is a multi-timeframe stochastic oscillator that plots the stochastic values from a higher timeframe directly onto your current chart. No repainting, no magic. It's a simple concept executed cleanly, and for that, it deserves a look.

I ran this on BTC/USD and EUR/USD across multiple sessions — daily chart with weekly stochastic, and 4-hour with daily stochastic. The logic is straightforward: the indicator pulls the stochastic reading from your chosen higher timeframe and displays it as a continuous line on your active chart. You get the %K and %D lines plus the usual overbought/oversold bands. The chart above shows it in action on a MACD chart setup, and honestly, the visual clarity surprised me.

**What Actually Sets It Apart**

Most stochastic indicators are single-timeframe. That means you're reacting to a signal on the same chart you're trading, which often gives you late entries. Stochastic_Mtf solves this by letting you see what the higher timeframe is doing *before* it filters down to your entry chart. Think of it as a trend filter that's built into an oscillator.

The key feature here is the time frame selector. You can set the higher TF to anything from 1-minute to monthly. This flexibility matters because it lets you align your trading style — a scalper on the 1-minute chart can reference the 15-minute stochastic without switching tabs, while a swing trader on the daily can check the weekly picture. The indicator also lets you adjust the %K and %D lengths (default 14 and 3) plus the smoothing. Nothing revolutionary, but the execution is solid.

**Settings I Actually Recommend**

After testing, here's what worked best:

- **Higher TF:** 4x–6x your current chart. On the 1-hour, use the 4-hour. On the 15-minute, use the 1-hour. This gives you a meaningful trend context without being so far removed that it lags.
- **%K Length: 14, %D Length: 3, Smoothing: 3** — the defaults are fine. I tried tighter values (8, 2, 2) and got too many whipsaws. Wider values (21, 5, 5) were too slow for intraday.
- **Overbought/Oversold: 80/20** — standard, but on trending pairs like GBP/JPY, consider 85/15 to avoid premature reversal signals.

**How I Trade It**

The setup is simple: when the higher-TF stochastic is above 50, I only take long entries on my lower timeframe. Below 50, I only short. This alone eliminated a ton of bad trades. For entries, I wait for a cross of the %K and %D lines in the direction of the higher-TF bias, ideally near the 20 or 80 levels.

On the chart above, you can see how the MTF line stays elevated during a rally while the lower timeframe stochastic dips and recovers — that's your continuation signal. Exit when the higher-TF stochastic crosses back through 50 or hits the opposite extreme.

**Pros & Cons**

Pros:
- Free and lightweight. No bloat, no confusing panels.
- Removes the need to tab between timeframes for a quick stochastic check.
- Accurate non-repainting signals — what you see is what you get.
- Clean visual design with proper band and line coloring options.

Cons:
- It's a stochastic. In strong trends, it will sit in overbought/oversold for extended periods. You need the 50-level filter or you'll get chopped up.
- No alerts on higher-TF crosses. You'll want to set your own.
- Limited customization compared to paid MTF oscillators — no divergence detection, no multi-symbol support.

**Who This Is For**

If you trade with a trend-following approach and struggle with timing entries against the macro move, this is for you. Range traders will find it less useful — it's a trend confirmation tool first. If you're purely scalping news events, the higher-TF lag will frustrate you. This is for the trader who wants context without clutter.

**Alternatives Worth Considering**

If you need more than just stochastic MTF, look at **MTF Oscillators** (paid) which bundle RSI, CCI, and stochastic in one panel. For divergence detection specifically, **Stochastic Divergence** indicators do that job better. But if you want a no-cost, no-nonsense multi-timeframe stochastic, this is hard to beat.

**FAQ**

**Does Stochastic_Mtf repaint?** No. It calculates on closed candles of the higher timeframe, so signals are fixed once that bar closes.

**Can I use it on any pair?** Yes, but it works best on liquid pairs with clear trends. Choppy pairs like EUR/CHF will give mixed signals.

**Is it good for crypto?** Yes — the 24/7 market makes multi-timeframe context especially valuable. I tested on BTC and ETH, and the higher-TF filter caught major trend shifts cleanly.

**Final Verdict**

Stochastic_Mtf does one thing and does it well. It's not flashy, but it fills a real gap: giving you higher-timeframe context without breaking your workflow. Four stars feels right — it's a solid, free tool that will improve your entries if you use it as a filter rather than a standalone signal generator. Pair it with price action or a trendline strategy and you've got a respectable edge. Just don't expect it to do your thinking for you.
