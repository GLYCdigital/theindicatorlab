Some indicators try to predict. This one tries to organize. ICT PD Arrays [LunqFX] is a chart overlay built around Inner Circle Trader (ICT) concepts — specifically the "PD arrays" that ICT-style traders use to frame where price might react. If you've spent any time in smart money circles, the vocabulary will be familiar: fair value gaps, displacement, and the zones that make up the PD array model. This tool's job is to draw those on your chart so you don't have to mark them by hand.

I want to be upfront about something before we go further: I don't have the publisher's documentation in front of me, so this review sticks to what the indicator's name and concept tell us. No invented settings, no made-up defaults, no fake performance numbers. What follows is an honest read of what the tool is for and who benefits.

## What "PD arrays" actually means

PD array stands for "Premium/Discount array." In ICT terminology it's an umbrella for the price zones that smart-money traders watch: fair value gaps (imbalances left behind when price moves aggressively), displacement candles (strong directional moves that create those imbalances), and the broader premium/discount framing of a range. The indicator bundles these into a single overlay rather than making you add three or four separate scripts.

That bundling is the main selling point. If you already trade FVGs and displacement, you know the pain of stacking multiple indicators that each draw their own boxes and clutter the chart. One overlay that handles the family of concepts is genuinely useful.

## What you're actually getting

As shown in the chart above, the output is visual — boxes and zones marking the imbalances and displacement areas the script detects. That's the whole product: detection and drawing. It doesn't tell you to buy or sell. It doesn't score setups. It hands you the structural map and leaves the decision to you.

That's a deliberate design choice, and it's the right one for this style of trading. ICT methodology is discretionary by nature. The trader reads context — where price is relative to the range, what liquidity sits above or below, whether displacement is clean — and the indicator just removes the tedium of marking. If you want a signal generator, look elsewhere. If you want a clean structural overlay, this is aimed at you.

## How traders typically use it

The workflow is straightforward. You load it, let it mark the arrays, and then read price against those marks. Two common applications:

- **Reaction zones.** FVGs and displacement areas are watched as potential support or resistance. Price returning into a marked zone is where a discretionary trader might look for a reaction.
- **Bias framing.** The premium/discount concept helps you judge whether price is in a "cheap" or "expensive" part of a range, which shapes whether you're hunting longs or shorts.

Neither of these is a mechanical rule. The indicator gives you the map; you still have to navigate.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Consolidates several ICT concepts into one overlay instead of several scripts fighting for chart space.
- Visual, non-prescriptive — fits discretionary smart-money trading rather than forcing a rigid system.
- The concept itself is well-established; if you trade this style, the zones it marks are the ones you'd otherwise mark manually.

**Cons**
- It's a drawing tool, not an edge. The value depends entirely on the trader's read of context.
- ICT concepts are interpretive. Different traders define FVGs and displacement slightly differently, and the script has to pick one interpretation — which may not match yours.
- No documentation surfaced for this review means I can't vouch for how the detection logic handles edge cases, and you should verify that yourself before relying on it.

## Who it's for

ICT and smart-money concept traders who already understand FVGs, displacement, and premium/discount framing — and who are tired of manually annotating charts. It's also reasonable for someone learning the methodology who wants a visual reference for what these arrays look like in practice. It is **not** for traders who want signals, mechanical entries, or a system that tells them what to do. Newer traders who haven't studied the concepts will likely find the output confusing rather than clarifying.
