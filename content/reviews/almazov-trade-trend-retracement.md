Most trend tools chase price. This one waits for price to come back to it. Almazov Trade: Trend Retracement is a TradingView indicator in the trend category, and its name tells you the thesis: identify the prevailing direction, then look for entries on the retracement rather than on the breakout. As shown in the chart above, that gives it a different character from a plain moving-average overlay — the emphasis is on timing within an existing trend, not on calling reversals.

Worth stating up front: I have not run this indicator, and there is no published documentation to work from beyond its classification and concept. So this review sticks to what a retracement-based trend tool is and does, and avoids the usual invented numbers.

## The core idea

Trend retracement trading is one of the oldest frameworks in technical analysis, and it is not complicated. A market moves in a direction, pauses or pulls back against that direction, and then — if the trend is intact — resumes. The retracement is the opportunity; the trend is the permission to take it.

What separates one retracement indicator from another is how it defines those two things. A trend filter has to exist to say "only look for longs here" or "only look for shorts here." A retracement signal has to exist to say "price has pulled back far enough to be interesting." Some tools lean on moving averages for the first job and oscillators for the second; others use swing structure or momentum. The name here points squarely at the trend-and-pullback concept, and that is the lens to evaluate it through.

## What it's likely doing well

The appeal of this class of indicator is behavioural as much as technical. Retracement entries get you into a move at a better price than breakout entries, and they naturally enforce a "wait for the pullback" discipline that many traders lack. If the indicator is filtering for trend before flagging anything, it also keeps you out of counter-trend setups — the single most common way discretionary traders give money back.

There's a second, quieter benefit: a retracement tool that draws your attention to pullbacks forces you to look at the chart more deliberately. You're not reacting to every candle; you're waiting for a condition. That alone changes how a session feels.

## How you'd actually use it

The workflow is straightforward. Establish direction first — the indicator's trend component should give you a bias. Then watch for price to move against that bias without breaking it. When a retracement signal appears, the entry is in the direction of the original trend, and the invalidation is usually the level that would have meant the trend was no longer intact.

Because I can't cite the indicator's specific inputs or default behaviour, treat that as a framework rather than a recipe. Read whatever settings the author exposes before you trade it live. The concept is sound; the execution details are for you to verify on your own charts.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Targets a well-established, intuitive edge: buying dips in uptrends, selling rallies in downtrends.
- Trend filter discourages counter-trend trades, which is where most retail losses originate.
- Pairs naturally with a stop placed beyond the retracement — the risk is defined by structure, not by a fixed pip count.
- Suits multi-timeframe work: a higher-timeframe bias with a lower-timeframe pullback entry is a classic combination.

**Cons**
- Retracement tools can flag pullbacks that keep going — a "retracement" that becomes a reversal looks identical until it isn't.
- In strong, fast trends there may be little or no pullback, so the indicator can sit quiet through the best part of a move.
- In choppy, range-bound conditions the trend filter is the weak link; direction is ambiguous and signals degrade.
- Without published documentation, you're relying on the author's chart examples and your own observation to understand exactly what triggers a signal.

## Who it's for

Discretionary trend traders who already think in terms of "bias first, entry second." Swing traders who are comfortable waiting for a setup rather than chasing. It is less suited to scalpers who need constant signals, and to anyone trading markets that spend most of their time ranging.

## FAQ

**Does it repaint?** I can't confirm that without documentation. Test it on a replay or bar-by-bar before relying on historical signals.

**Does it work on any timeframe?** The concept is timeframe-agnostic, but the behaviour of retracements differs between timeframes. Verify on the ones you trade.

**Is it a complete system?** No indicator is. It gives you a directional bias and a timing cue; position sizing, stops and exits are still yours to define.

**Can I use it with other indicators?** Yes — a momentum or volume tool to confirm the retracement is a common pairing. Just beware of stacking filters until you can't take a trade.

## Verdict

The premise is honest and the concept is one that has survived decades of market cycles: trade with the trend, enter on the pullback. That earns it a solid rating on design intent alone. What holds it back from the top tier is the lack of transparent documentation — the specifics that would let a trader judge signal quality before committing capital simply aren't there to inspect. If you're a trend trader who already believes in retracement entries, this is a reasonable expression of that idea. Go in with your eyes open, test it yourself, and let the chart — not the label — make the case.
