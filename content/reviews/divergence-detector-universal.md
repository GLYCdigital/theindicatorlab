# Divergence_Detector_Universal Review

Divergence indicators tend to fall into two camps: noisy tools that flag every minor wiggle, or slow ones that miss real moves. Divergence_Detector_Universal aims for the middle ground—not perfect, but a reasonable option for a free tool.

## What This Indicator Actually Does

Divergence_Detector_Universal scans price action against an underlying oscillator (RSI, MACD, Stochastic, or CCI) and draws lines directly on your chart when it finds a mismatch. It highlights both **regular divergences** (trend reversal signals) and **hidden divergences** (trend continuation signals). A classic example: price making a lower low while the RSI prints a higher low—the indicator draws a line connecting the oscillator valleys. That's a bullish regular divergence.

## Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Multi-oscillator support**: RSI, MACD, Stochastic, CCI. Pick the one that fits your style.
- **Customizable lookback period**: Controls how far back the indicator scans for pivot comparisons.
- **Pivot strength control**: You can set the minimum number of bars left/right for pivot detection. This is the single most important setting to reduce false signals.
- **Visual clarity**: Divergence lines are drawn with arrows at the oscillator level. No overlapping labels.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

- **Oscillator**: RSI is the most commonly used. Stochastic tends to be whippier on daily charts.
- **Pivot strength**: Adjust the left/right bar count for pivot detection. Higher values filter out micro-divergences that never play out; lower values make the tool more responsive on shorter timeframes.
- **Lookback**: Controls how far back the tool scans. Longer lookbacks surface older divergences; shorter lookbacks keep things current.
- **Show hidden divergences**: Turn this ON only if you're trading in the direction of the larger trend. Otherwise, keep it OFF to reduce clutter.

## How to Use It for Entries and Exits

**Bullish regular divergence** (price lower low, oscillator higher low): Wait for price to break above the most recent swing high. Do NOT buy at the second oscillator valley—it can drop further.

**Bearish regular divergence** (price higher high, oscillator lower high): Sell after price breaks below the recent swing low. Use a stop loss just above the divergence peak.

**Hidden divergence** (continuation): If you're already in a trend, a hidden divergence confirms the move has legs. Add to your position.

## Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros**:
- Free and open-source. No paywalls.
- Works on stocks, crypto, forex—any asset with enough volatility.
- Pivot strength adjustment is a lifesaver for cutting noise.
- Clean visuals compared to other free divergence tools.

**Cons**:
- No alert system. You have to watch the chart manually.
- Can repaint slightly. When a pivot is confirmed, earlier labels shift. Not ideal for scalpers.
- Hidden divergences default ON—new traders may misinterpret them as reversal signals.

## Who It's Actually For

- **Swing traders** on 1H–4H charts.
- **Position traders** using daily charts. The pivot strength setting helps isolate major turning points.
- **Not for scalpers** or 1-minute chart addicts. Repainting will work against you.

## Better Alternatives If They Exist

- **Divergence Indicator Pro** (paid): Adds alerts and no repainting. Worth it if you trade divergences heavily.
- **RSI Divergence by LuxAlgo**: More features (zone detection, multi-timeframe), but costs a monthly subscription. Overkill for most.
- **TradingView's built-in divergence tool**: Actually decent now. Just draw it manually. No automation, but zero noise.

## FAQ Addressing Real Trader Questions

**Does this indicator repaint?**
Yes, slightly. The divergence lines appear when the second pivot is confirmed. If a new bar breaks the pivot structure, the line disappears or moves. Real-time use is fine, but don't backtest with it.

**Can I use it with MACD?**
Yes, but MACD divergences are slower.

**Why am I seeing divergences everywhere?**
Your pivot strength is too low. Increase the left/right bars. Also, check if hidden divergences are ON—turn them off unless you're trending.

**Is it good for crypto?**
Generally yes. BTC and ETH tend to respect RSI divergences on 4H. Just beware of fakeouts in low-liquidity altcoins.

## Final Verdict

Divergence_Detector_Universal is a solid, no-nonsense tool for traders who understand divergence theory and want automation without the bloat. It's not a silver bullet—you still need to confirm with price action and volume—but it saves time on manual scanning. The repainting and lack of alerts are the biggest downsides.

If you're a swing trader who knows how to read divergences, it's worth a look. If you're new, use it as a learning aid but don't trust every line it draws.
