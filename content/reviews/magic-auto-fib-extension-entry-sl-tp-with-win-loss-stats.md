Some indicators try to do one thing well. This one tries to do the whole trade for you — entry, stop, target, and a running scorecard of how those levels have played out. That ambition is exactly what makes "MAGIC Auto Fib Extension Entry SL TP with Win Loss Stats" worth a look, and also what makes it worth scrutinising before you trust it with real money.

## What it actually does

Strip away the name and you have a trend tool built on Fibonacci extensions. Instead of you manually dragging retracement and extension levels across the chart, the indicator identifies the trend and then automatically projects Fibonacci extension levels that it treats as candidate entry, stop-loss and take-profit zones. On top of that, it keeps an on-chart tally of wins and losses for the setups it has generated — the "Win Loss Stats" in the title.

So the concept is three tools stacked into one script: a trend read, a Fibonacci extension map, and a trade-management overlay with a built-in record. Whether that's elegant or overstuffed depends on how you trade.

## The features that set it apart

The obvious differentiator is the automation of Fib extensions. Most Fib scripts draw levels and leave the interpretation to you. This one frames those levels as actionable — entry, SL, TP — which shortens the gap between "here's a chart" and "here's a plan."

The second differentiator is the win/loss statistics. Trend indicators rarely show you their own history on the chart. Having a visible tally of how the plotted setups have resolved is genuinely useful context, and it nudges you toward treating the tool as a system rather than a magic line.

Credit where it's due: the naming is honest about what it is. A lot of tools bury the mechanics behind flattering names. This one puts "entry," "SL," "TP" and "stats" right in the title, so you know what you're installing.

## How you'd use it

The workflow is straightforward on paper. Apply it to a trending chart, let it mark the Fibonacci extension structure, and use the projected entry, stop and target as a template for your own trade. The win/loss panel gives you a running sense of how those templates have behaved on that instrument and timeframe.

The important caveat: at the time of writing, no official description or documentation is available for this indicator. That means the specifics — how the trend is defined, how the extension levels are chosen, what counts as a win versus a loss, whether the stats are per-instrument or global — are not something I can state. I haven't run it, and I won't pretend otherwise. Treat the numbers on the chart as something you need to understand before you rely on them, not as a black box that speaks for itself.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Automates a step most traders do by hand: projecting Fib extensions and turning them into entry/SL/TP levels.
- The win/loss stats are a genuinely useful idea — most trend tools show no accountability at all.
- The name tells you exactly what it does, which is rarer than it should be.
- As a trend tool, it targets the setups where Fibonacci extensions actually make sense.

**Cons**
- No published documentation means the methodology is opaque until you dig into the code or watch it on your own charts.
- Bundling trend detection, Fib levels and statistics into one script makes it harder to isolate what's actually driving the signals.
- Win/loss stats can flatter a tool — a record of resolved setups tells you nothing about the setups you'd have skipped or the ones still open.
- It's a discretionary aid, not a signal generator you should follow blindly.

## Who it's for

Fibonacci traders who already understand extensions and want the plotting and trade-framing automated. Trend traders who like a visual entry/SL/TP template rather than raw levels. And — importantly — anyone disciplined enough to question the win/loss stats rather than take them as gospel. If you want a one-line buy/sell arrow with no thinking required, this isn't that, and the stats might mislead you.

## FAQ

**Does it tell me when to buy and sell?**
No. It projects Fibonacci extension levels and frames them as entry, stop and target zones, plus a win/loss record. You still decide whether to act.

**Are the win/loss stats a backtest?**
They're an on-chart tally of how the plotted setups have resolved. That's not the same as a rigorous backtest, and without documentation I can't tell you exactly how they're calculated. Verify before trusting.

**What markets and timeframes does it work on?**
Not stated in any available documentation. Fibonacci extensions are a general technique, but you'll need to test it on your own instruments.

**Is it beginner-friendly?**
Only if you already understand Fibonacci extensions and stop/target placement. The automation helps, but the concepts underneath are assumed.

## Verdict

This is a well-conceived idea — automating Fib extension entries with a built-in scorecard is more useful than another repainted moving average. The lack of documentation is a real friction point, and the win/loss stats deserve scepticism rather than faith. But as a trend tool that frames Fibonacci structure into an actionable plan, it earns its place. Four stars: strong concept, useful execution, with room for more transparency.
