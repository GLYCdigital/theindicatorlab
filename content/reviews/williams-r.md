# Williams %R Review

Williams %R is a momentum oscillator that's been around since the 1970s, created by Larry Williams. It's not flashy, but it's a workhorse—if you know how to use it right. It works across Forex, crypto, and equities.

## What This Indicator Actually Does

Williams %R measures the current close relative to the highest high over a lookback period. It ranges from -100 to 0. A reading below -80 suggests oversold (potential bounce), above -20 suggests overbought (potential drop). It's nearly identical to the Stochastic Oscillator but inverted—a -80 reading is like Stochastic's 20.

**Key difference from RSI:** Williams %R reacts faster to price swings. It's more sensitive, which means more signals, but also more noise.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

The default period is 14. The period controls how much history the oscillator looks back over—shorter periods react faster but produce more noise, longer periods smooth things out.

- **Shorter period** – More responsive, catches moves earlier, but whipsaws more.
- **Default period** – Standard, suited to daily charts and above, and to swing trading.
- **Very short period** – Suited only to short-term scalps, with more fakeouts.

**Levels to watch:** -80 and -20 are the default thresholds. Tighter thresholds can be used for stronger signals on trending markets, while ranging markets are generally better served by the defaults.

## How to Use It for Entries and Exits

**The classic setup (works best in ranges):**
1. Wait for Williams %R to dip below -80.
2. Wait for it to cross back above -80. That's your long entry.
3. Exit when it hits -20 or above, or when it crosses back below -20.

For shorts: flip it. Above -20, then cross below.

**Divergence:**
- Bullish divergence: Price makes a lower low, but Williams %R makes a higher low. Entry when it crosses -80.
- Bearish divergence: Price makes a higher high, but Williams %R makes a lower high. Entry when it crosses -20.

Divergence is where the indicator shines, and it tends to work best on higher timeframes.

**Filtering false signals:**
- Only trade oversold signals if the trend is up (use a higher timeframe bias or a long moving average).
- Overbought signals only if the trend is down.
- Avoid trading around major news events.

## Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Simple, visual, and easy to spot extremes.
- Divergence works well on higher timeframes.
- Free on TradingView (no paywall).

**Cons:**
- Whipsaws heavily in choppy markets. Don't use it on the 1-minute during low volatility.
- Not leading—it's a momentum indicator, so it confirms what price already did.
- Overbought/oversold alone is a losing strategy. It must be combined with trend or divergence.

## Who It's Actually For

- **Swing traders** who use daily and 4h charts.
- **Traders comfortable with divergence** and filtering signals.
- **Anyone who hates Stochastic's smoothing.** Williams %R is raw and direct.

Not for: Scalerpers who need fast, reliable entries without context.

## Better Alternatives if They Exist

- **Stochastic RSI** – Less noisy, better for ranging markets.
- **RSI Divergence** – Slower but more reliable for trend reversals.
- **CCI** – Similar momentum concept but different math.

If you're already using Stochastic, you don't need Williams %R. But if you want a less filtered view of momentum, it's worth a look.

## FAQ

**Q: Williams %R vs Stochastics—which is better?**
A: They're cousins. Williams %R is faster and less smoothed. Use it if you want quicker signals; use Stochastics if you want fewer false moves.

**Q: Does it repaint?**
A: No. It's a fixed lookback. What you see is what you get.

**Q: Can I use it alone?**
A: Better not to. Pair it with a trend filter (EMA, MACD) or volume confirmation.

**Q: Best timeframe?**
A: 4H or daily. Lower timeframes require a tighter period and more patience.

## Final Verdict

Williams %R is a solid, no-nonsense momentum oscillator. It's not a magic bullet, but it's a reliable tool when used with divergence and trend context. If you're tired of overcomplicated indicators, this one does one thing well: it tells you when price is stretched.
