Some indicators try to do everything. Auto Triangles [ITA] does one thing and puts it in the name: it finds and draws triangle patterns on your chart automatically. No manual trendline drawing, no squinting at converging price action trying to decide whether it's a real triangle or just noise. The script handles the geometry for you.

That's a narrow brief, and it's worth being upfront about that. This isn't a signal generator that tells you when to buy or sell. It's a pattern-recognition overlay. Whether that's useful depends entirely on how you trade.

## What it actually does

Triangle patterns are one of the oldest ideas in technical analysis — price compressing between two converging lines, usually read as a period of consolidation before a breakout in one direction or the other. The problem is that drawing them by hand is subjective and time-consuming, especially if you're watching multiple charts.

Auto Triangles takes that job off your hands. It scans price action and marks out triangle formations as they appear, so you can see the compression happening without tracing lines yourself. As shown in the chart above, the result is a cleaner visual read of where price is coiling.

What it deliberately does not do is predict the breakout direction, assign a probability, or tell you to enter. It shows you the shape. The interpretation is yours.

## Where it fits in a workflow

The most natural use is as a first-pass filter. Instead of scanning a watchlist and manually hunting for consolidation, you let the indicator flag triangles and then apply your own judgment: Is this at a meaningful level? Is volume behaving the way you'd expect? Does the broader trend support a breakout continuation or a reversal?

Because it's a study rather than a strategy, it won't fire alerts on its own or produce buy/sell arrows. You're meant to layer it on top of your existing process. Pair it with whatever you use for trend context — a moving average, market structure, momentum — and treat the triangles as a heads-up that something is compressing.

One thing worth flagging: triangle detection is inherently pattern-matching, and pattern-matching can produce false positives. A script will draw a triangle wherever the geometry roughly fits, whether or not a human would call it a textbook setup. Treat every marked pattern as a candidate to evaluate, not a confirmed formation.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Removes the manual, subjective work of drawing triangles — genuinely useful if you watch many charts.
- Keeps a tight focus. It doesn't clutter your chart with unrelated signals or attempt to do too much.
- The visual output is easy to read at a glance, which matters when you're scanning rather than deep-diving.
- Works as a neutral overlay, so it won't fight with the rest of your toolkit.

**Cons**
- It's pattern recognition, not prediction. If you want entries and exits handed to you, this isn't that.
- Automated detection will surface formations a discretionary trader might reject — expect to filter.
- Triangle patterns are notoriously ambiguous until they resolve, so the indicator can only tell you so much before the breakout.
- It's a single-purpose tool. If triangles aren't central to how you trade, there's little here for you.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already use chart patterns and want to speed up the scanning process. Swing traders watching several instruments benefit most, since the manual overhead of drawing triangles multiplies with every chart you follow. It's also reasonable for traders learning pattern recognition — seeing the script's output helps calibrate your own eye for what a triangle looks like.

It's a poor fit for anyone wanting a mechanical, signal-driven system. If your process relies on an indicator telling you exactly when to act, this will frustrate you.

## FAQ

**Does it tell me which way price will break?**
No. It identifies and draws the triangle. The breakout direction is for you to assess.

**Can I use it on any timeframe or market?**
The source material doesn't specify timeframes or markets, so I won't invent a recommendation. Triangles form across timeframes, but how cleanly any script detects them will vary — check it against your own charts.

**Will it produce alerts?**
It's a study, not a strategy. The documentation doesn't describe built-in alerts, so don't assume them.

**Is it a replacement for learning patterns?**
No. It automates the drawing, not the judgment. You still need to know what makes a triangle meaningful.

## Verdict

Auto Triangles [ITA] is a focused, honest tool. It does one job — finding and drawing triangles — and doesn't pretend to be more. For traders who already think in patterns, that's a real time-saver and a clean addition to the chart. The trade-off is that it's only as good as your interpretation of what it draws, and automated detection will always need a human filter.

It earns four stars for doing its narrow job well without overreaching. It loses a star because the value is entirely dependent on the trader wielding it — and because a pattern overlay, however convenient, can only ever be one input among many.
