**Description:** Schiff Pitchfork review: an honest look at what it is, how it differs from the Andrews Pitchfork, and how traders use it for trend channel entries and reversals.

You've seen the classic Andrews Pitchfork everywhere. Schiff Pitchfork is its lesser-known cousin, and it arguably deserves more attention.

## What This Indicator Actually Does

Schiff Pitchfork draws a median line between two pivot points, then projects two parallel lines outward—the same concept as Andrews, but with a different anchor point. Instead of using three distinct pivots, the Schiff version uses only two: a major high and a major low (or vice versa). The third line is derived by projecting the median line from the midpoint of those two pivots.

The practical consequence is that the Schiff version tends to handle trending markets differently than Andrews. It doesn't force a third pivot into a messy consolidation, which can result in cleaner channels in choppy price action.

## Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Two-pivot simplicity:** No wrestling with a third point. Connect a swing high and swing low, and the tool generates the channel.
- **Dynamic median line:** The middle line is equidistant from the outer boundaries. Price often treats it as support or resistance.
- **Auto-extend option:** When enabled, the pitchfork extends forward. Useful for swing trading.
- **Customizable colors and line styles:** Not revolutionary, but helpful when overlaying multiple pitchforks.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

Open the indicator's settings. The parameters worth adjusting include:

- **Line style:** A solid line for the median and dashed lines for the outer boundaries keeps the chart readable.
- **Line width:** Making the median heavier than the outer lines helps it stand out as the key level.
- **Extend:** Enabling this shows future projections rather than stopping the lines at the last pivot.
- **Color:** Distinct colors for uptrends versus downtrends help at a glance.

**Source for pivots:** The pivot source and bar count determine how sensitive the anchor points are. Shorter bar counts pick up more minor swings; longer counts anchor to more significant highs and lows. There is no single correct value—it depends on the timeframe you trade and how much noise you want to filter out.

## How to Use It for Entries and Exits

**Entry strategy (long):**
1. Identify a clear uptrend. Draw the Schiff Pitchfork from a major swing low (anchor) to a major swing high (second pivot).
2. Wait for price to pull back to the median line or the lower outer line.
3. Enter on a bullish candlestick close (e.g., engulfing or pin bar) at that level.
4. Stop loss: place it below the lower outer line.
5. Take profit: upper outer line or an extension of the channel.

**Exit strategy (short):** Same logic but inverted.

**Pro tip:** Watch for volume confirmation. If price touches the median line on decreasing volume, the bounce is weak. If volume spikes, the level is more likely to hold.

## Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Cleaner than Andrews in trending markets.
- Fewer false breakouts because the channel is less sensitive to noise.
- Works on any timeframe, from short-term scalps to weekly swings.
- Free indicator (no subscription needed).

**Cons:**
- Not great for range-bound or sideways markets. Price will chop through the lines.
- Requires manual drawing. There's no auto-detect pivot feature—you have to click two points.
- Can be subjective. Two traders might draw different pitchforks from the same chart.

## Who It's Actually For

- **Swing traders** who like trend-following with clear risk/reward.
- **Traders who hate clutter.** This is one of the cleanest channel tools.
- **Anyone who finds Andrews Pitchfork too finicky** with its third pivot.

**Not for:** Scalpers who need automated signals, or traders who rely on purely objective indicators.

## Better Alternatives If They Exist

- **Andrews Pitchfork:** Better for range-bound markets where three distinct pivots are clear.
- **Auto Pitchfork by LuxAlgo:** Automatically detects pivots and draws the pitchfork. Saves time at the cost of a subscription.
- **Keltner Channels:** Similar median-line concept, but fully automated and better suited to mean reversion.

**Verdict:** Stick with Schiff if you manually trade trends. Use an auto pitchfork if you want automation.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use Schiff Pitchfork on crypto?**
A: Yes. It can be applied to crypto pairs, though higher timeframes tend to suit the volatility better than very low ones.

**Q: Does it repaint?**
A: No. It's a fixed drawing tool. Once you place the two pivots, the lines don't change.

**Q: What timeframe is ideal?**
A: Higher timeframes tend to be more reliable. Very low timeframes produce more false touches.

**Q: Can I combine it with another indicator?**
A: Yes. It's commonly paired with momentum oscillators for divergence at the median line, and it works alongside volume profile.

## Final Verdict

Schiff Pitchfork is a solid tool. It's not a magic bullet, but it's a reliable option for trend-trading with clear structure. If you're tired of overcomplicated indicators and want something that respects price action, it's worth adding to your toolkit.

**Best for:** Manual trend-following on higher timeframes.
**Avoid if:** You want automated signals or trade range-bound markets.
