# Andrews Pitchfork Review

Andrews Pitchfork is one of the older tools in the technical analysis toolbox, and it gets overlooked by traders who prefer oscillators and signal-based indicators. But it has survived for decades for a reason: when the structure lines up, it frames price action cleanly. Here's a straightforward look at what it does and where it fits.

## What This Indicator Actually Does

Andrews Pitchfork is a **median-line tool** for identifying potential support, resistance, and trend channels. It is drawn from three pivot points and produces three parallel lines:

- **Median line** — the "handle" of the pitchfork. Price tends to oscillate around it.
- **Upper and lower parallel lines** — act as dynamic support and resistance.

It doesn't predict. It frames the market's current structure — essentially a trend-following ruler drawn from pivots you select.

## Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Fixed once drawn** — the lines stay where you place them until you move the anchors.
- **Timeframe-agnostic** — the tool can be applied to any chart interval.
- **Median line magnetism** — price often oscillates around the middle line.
- **Manual or auto-draw** — TradingView lets you connect three pivot points manually, or use the auto-draw tool.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

| Setting | Consideration | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| Pivot period | Controls how much recent structure is captured | A shorter period reacts faster; a longer one smooths |
| Line style | Visual contrast against your chart background | Purely cosmetic |
| Line width | Visibility vs. clutter | Purely cosmetic |
| Auto-draw | Manual vs. automated anchor placement | Manual placement gives more control over which pivots define the fork |

The main decision is pivot selection: which three swing points you anchor the fork to. Zoom out to a higher timeframe to identify the major swing points first, then apply the Pitchfork on your entry timeframe. That filters noise.

## How to Use It for Entries and Exits

Common ways traders frame entries around the fork:

1. **Median line bounce** — price touches the median line and forms a bullish or bearish candlestick pattern. Enter on the close of that candle.
2. **Upper/lower line rejection** — price hits the upper parallel and prints a pin bar or engulfing candle. Same logic applies in reverse at the lower line.
3. **Breakout of the fork** — if price closes outside the upper or lower line with volume, it can signal trend acceleration. Enter in the breakout direction.

Exit approaches:

- Take partial profits at the opposite parallel line.
- Move the stop to the median line after the first target is hit.
- If price respects the median line repeatedly, trail the stop below it.

## Pros and Cons

### Pros
- **Simple to learn** — three points, three lines.
- **Objective structure** — no subjective trendline drawing.
- **Adapts to price action** — usable in both ranging and trending conditions.

### Cons
- **Not a leading indicator** — it confirms what has already happened.
- **Can be ambiguous** — different traders draw different forks on the same chart.
- **Whippy in low volatility** — price can chop around the median line without direction.

## Who It's Actually For

- **Swing traders** who want a clear framework for entries and exits.
- **Price action purists** who avoid lagging indicators.
- **Traders tired of repainting tools** — the fork is fixed once drawn.

It's **not** for scalpers who need quick signals constantly, and it's not for beginners looking for a "buy/sell" button.

## Alternatives Worth Knowing

If you like the concept but want more automation or variation:

- **Auto-Fib Channels** — similar structure but with Fibonacci ratios.
- **Schiff Pitchfork** — a modified version that shifts the median line, often considered more responsive in strong trends.
- **Standard Deviation Channels** — dynamic volatility bands without manual pivot selection.

## FAQ

**Q: Does Andrews Pitchfork repaint?**
A: No. Once you place the three anchors, the lines are fixed until you move them.

**Q: Which timeframe works best?**
A: Higher timeframes suit swing trading; lower timeframes can be used for scalping but require tighter stops.

**Q: Can I use it with other indicators?**
A: Yes. It's commonly paired with momentum or oscillator tools to look for divergence at the median line.

**Q: Is it better than Fibonacci retracements?**
A: Different tools. Fibonacci gives static levels; Pitchfork gives dynamic channels. They can be used together.

## Final Verdict

Andrews Pitchfork isn't flashy, it doesn't beep at you, and it won't hand you signals on a plate. What it does offer is a clear, repeatable framework for reading price structure. If you take the time to draw it correctly and combine it with price action, it can be a useful part of a discretionary approach.

**Best for:** Swing traders who value structure over signals.
**Skip if:** You want automated entries or dislike manually drawing lines.
