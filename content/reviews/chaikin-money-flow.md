# Chaikin Money Flow (CMF) Review

Chaikin Money Flow is one of those indicators many traders try, but relatively few end up keeping. Here's an unfiltered look at what it does and where it fits.

**What it actually does**
CMF is a volume-weighted oscillator that measures how much money is flowing in or out of an asset over a set period. It combines price location (close relative to the high-low range) with volume. Readings above 0 indicate buying pressure; below 0 means selling pressure. It's not a momentum oscillator — it's a *volume-pressure* indicator.

**Key features that matter**
- The default lookback period is the standard starting point on daily charts. Shorter periods make the line more reactive and prone to whipsaw; longer periods add lag and delay signals.
- The zero line is the only real anchor. Unlike RSI or Stochastics, there are no overbought/oversold levels that consistently work.
- Divergences are worth watching. If price makes a higher high but CMF makes a lower high, that's a warning sign.

**Settings and How to Tune Them**
The lookback period is the main lever. A shorter period reduces lag but increases noise; a longer period smooths the line but delays confirmation. On intraday charts, a shorter setting can help reduce lag, while very short timeframes tend to produce too much noise to be useful. The zero line is the primary reference point regardless of the period chosen.

**How it's used for entries and exits**
CMF works best as a filter rather than a standalone signal. If price is breaking resistance and CMF is above zero and rising, that adds confidence to a long. For exits, watch for CMF turning negative while price still looks strong — that can signal distribution. A common pattern: price stalls near resistance while CMF has already rolled over earlier.

**Honest pros and cons**
Pros:
- Objective measure of volume pressure, not just price action.
- Divergences can catch tops and bottoms early.
- Works well with trendlines and support/resistance.

Cons:
- Laggy. By the time CMF confirms, you're late to the move.
- Flat during consolidation periods — gives false signals.
- Overbought/oversold levels are unreliable; ignore them.

**Who it's actually for**
Swing traders on daily charts who want a volume confirmation tool. Not for scalpers, and not for anyone expecting it to predict reversals alone. It's a supporting actor, not the lead.

**Better alternatives**
If you want volume-pressure with less lag, look at the Money Flow Index (MFI), which includes overbought/oversold thresholds. If you want pure volume analysis, On-Balance Volume (OBV) gives you a cleaner line without the smoothing.

**FAQ: Common questions**
*"Does CMF work on crypto?"*
It can be used, but with higher noise. It tends to behave better on higher timeframes for major pairs.
*"Can you trade the zero-line cross?"*
Only in strong trends. In a range, you'll get chopped up.
*"What's the best timeframe?"*
Daily is the most commonly cited.

**Final verdict**
Chaikin Money Flow is a solid confirmatory tool, but it's not a standalone system. It does one thing decently but has too many drawbacks to rely on alone. If you're already using price action and volume, CMF adds a little extra conviction — just don't expect magic.
