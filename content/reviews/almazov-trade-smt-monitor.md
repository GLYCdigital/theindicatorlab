The name says a lot here. "SMT" stands for Smart Money Technique — the idea that correlated markets don't always move in lockstep, and when they don't, that disagreement can signal something about the trend. The Almazov Trade SMT Monitor is a TradingView indicator that leans on that concept and presents it as a trend tool. If you've spent any time in the smart-money-concepts (SMC) corner of trading, the vocabulary will be familiar. If you haven't, this is a slightly more technical entry point than a moving average crossover.

Let me be upfront about something. There's no official documentation I could pull for this one, and I haven't run it on a chart myself. So this review sticks to what the tool is built around conceptually and what that means for you as a trader — not to specific settings, thresholds, or numbers I can't verify. I'd rather give you a shorter, honest read than pad it out with made-up defaults.

## What SMT divergence actually is

Before judging the indicator, it helps to understand the idea underneath it. Smart money technique looks at two markets that normally correlate — think ES and NQ futures, or a currency pair and its cross — and watches for moments when one makes a new high or low and the other refuses to follow. That gap is the "divergence." The logic among SMC traders is that the market that fails to confirm is showing weakness, and the disagreement can precede a shift in trend.

That's the conceptual core of this tool. It's a trend indicator in the sense that it's helping you read the strength and direction of a move through the lens of cross-market agreement — not by smoothing price with a lagging average. That's a meaningful distinction. A lot of trend tools tell you what already happened. Divergence-based tools are trying to flag when the trend is losing conviction.

## Where it fits in a workflow

Because the tool is built on comparing markets, it's the kind of thing that lives better on a secondary chart or a separate pane than slapped onto your main trading timeframe and forgotten. The practical use is confirmation: you've spotted a setup, and you glance at whether the correlated market is agreeing or diverging. If it's agreeing, you have a cleaner read. If it's diverging, you have a reason to be cautious or to look for a reversal.

That makes it a discretionary tool more than a signal generator. You're still the one deciding what the divergence means in context. Anyone expecting it to fire a clean buy/sell arrow and hand you a strategy is going to be disappointed — but that's true of most SMC-flavoured tools, and it's not really a knock on this one.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- It's built on a real, coherent concept. SMT divergence isn't a random mashup; it's a specific technique with a clear rationale, and having it automated beats eyeballing two charts side by side.
- As a trend tool it's genuinely different from the usual suspects. If your chart is already drowning in moving averages and MACD, this adds a perspective those don't.
- The cross-market framing forces you to think about correlation, which is a discipline worth building regardless of whether you keep the indicator.

**Cons**

- The concept is only as good as your correlated-market pairing. SMT logic depends on markets that actually track each other; pick the wrong pair and the divergences are noise.
- It's an interpretation tool, not a mechanical system. There's no "set and forget" here, and newer traders may find the divergence reading ambiguous without context.
- With no official documentation surfaced, onboarding depends on prior SMC familiarity. If you don't already know the vocabulary, expect a learning curve.

## Who it's for

This is for traders who already think in terms of smart money concepts and want a cleaner way to monitor SMT divergence without juggling multiple charts. Swing and intraday traders who trade correlated instruments — index futures, currency pairs, related commodities — will get the most out of it. If you're a pure price-action trader who ignores cross-market relationships, this isn't going to convert you, and that's fine.

## FAQ

**Does it work on any market?**
The underlying concept relies on correlated markets, so it's most relevant where a meaningful comparison exists. On an isolated instrument with no natural counterpart, the SMT logic has less to work with.

**Is it a standalone strategy?**
No. It's a trend/confirmation tool meant to be read alongside your own analysis. Treat the divergence information as context, not as an entry trigger on its own.

**Do I need to understand SMC to use it?**
It helps considerably. The tool is built around smart money technique, so the output assumes you know what divergence means and how to weigh it.

## Verdict

The Almazov Trade SMT Monitor earns its keep by doing something most trend indicators don't: it looks across markets for disagreement rather than just smoothing a single price series. That's a genuinely useful angle, and for SMC-literate traders it's a practical way to keep SMT divergence on the radar. The caveats are real — it demands the right market pairing and rewards interpretation over automation — but those are inherent to the technique, not flaws in the tool. A solid, concept-driven indicator that knows what it's for.
