Some indicators try to predict. This one tries to draw. Trendlines [FEELS] is a trend-category tool that renders trendlines on your chart, taking the manual work of connecting swing points off your hands. That's the entire premise, and it's a useful one — trendline drawing is tedious, subjective, and easy to fudge when you're emotionally invested in a position.

## What it actually does

At its core, the indicator automates a task most discretionary traders do by hand: identifying directional structure and sketching the lines that define it. Instead of you dragging a line from swing low to swing low (or high to high), the script handles the geometry and keeps the chart updated as price develops.

That's a meaningful shift in workflow. Manual trendlines are only as good as the eye drawing them, and that eye gets biased fast. A tool that draws objectively — even if its definition of a valid trendline differs from yours — removes one source of self-deception. You stop asking "does this line fit?" and start asking "is price respecting the structure the script has drawn?"

The name is honest about its intent: trendlines. This isn't a signal generator, an oscillator, or a confluence machine. It's a chart-structure overlay.

## How to use it in practice

The sensible workflow is to treat the plotted lines as a structural reference, not a trigger. As shown in the chart above, the value comes from watching how price behaves around the drawn levels — whether it respects them, breaks them cleanly, or grinds through them with weak momentum. That behavioural read is where the edge lives, not in the line itself.

Because I can't verify the exact logic from the source material, I won't pretend to describe its internal rules for selecting pivots or validating a line. What I can say is that the output is a visual map of directional structure, and the best way to use a map is to navigate with it — not to follow it blindly.

A reasonable approach: plot the indicator, let it establish structure across a few timeframes, and use the lines as context for whatever entry method you already trust. If you trade breakouts, the lines give you a clean level to watch. If you trade pullbacks, they give you a reference for where a retrace might find footing.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Removes the subjectivity of manual trendline drawing — the line is what it is.
- Saves real time on multi-timeframe analysis; you're not redrawing structure every session.
- Keeps the chart clean and readable, focused on the one thing trend traders actually care about.
- As a trend-category overlay, it complements momentum tools rather than competing with them.

**Cons**
- Trendlines are inherently interpretive. Any automated line is a judgement call baked into code, and it won't always match how you'd draw it.
- It's a visual aid, not a decision engine. If you're looking for buy/sell signals, this isn't that.
- Without documented logic (at least none I could retrieve), you're partly trusting a black box for your structural read — worth knowing before you lean on it.

## Who it's for

Discretionary trend traders who already draw lines by hand and want a faster, less biased starting point. Also useful for anyone learning trendline analysis who wants to compare their own drawings against an automated version. Swing traders working across multiple timeframes will get the most mileage, since redrawing structure at scale is where manual work hurts most.

It's a poor fit for algorithmic traders wanting hard rules, or for anyone expecting the indicator to tell them when to click buy.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
No. It plots trendlines. Interpretation and entries are on you.

**Can I use it on any timeframe?**
Trendline logic applies across timeframes in principle, but I can't verify timeframe-specific behaviour from the available material — test it on your own charts.

**Is it a replacement for drawing my own lines?**
It's a faster, more objective starting point. Whether it replaces your own drawing depends on how closely its structure matches your read of the market.

## Verdict

Trendlines [FEELS] does one job and does it with a clear purpose: automating the drawing of trend structure so you can spend your attention on price behaviour instead of line placement. It's not revolutionary, and it won't make decisions for you — but as a structural overlay for discretionary trend traders, it earns its place on the chart. Four stars: genuinely useful, honestly scoped, and refreshingly free of hype.
