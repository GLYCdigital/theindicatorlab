# Marubozu_Detection Review

Marubozu candles are among the more dependable single-candle patterns in price action trading. They signal strong momentum with no wick on one side, meaning buyers or sellers were in control from open to close.

Marubozu_Detection automates the spotting of these candles on your TradingView chart. Does it do the job? Largely, yes. Here's a breakdown.

**What this indicator actually does**
It highlights Marubozu candles in real time. A green arrow appears below a bullish Marubozu, a red arrow above a bearish one. You can adjust the sensitivity (how much wick is allowed) and whether to include doji-like bodies. The default settings are reasonable — a small wick tolerance and a minimum body size that fills most of the candle's range. No bloat, no extra gimmicks.

**Key features that set it apart**
- **Wick tolerance slider** – Many free scripts hardcode this at zero. That misses real Marubozus with tiny shadows. This one lets you allow a small amount of wick, which matters because true zero-wick candles are rarer than they look.
- **Confirmation filter** – You can toggle a trend filter that only shows signals in the direction of a moving average. This is aimed at cutting false signals during choppy sideways action.
- **Alert integration** – You get a pop-up or email when a Marubozu forms. Useful for scanning multiple symbols.

**Settings and How to Tune Them**
- Timeframe: higher timeframes are more reliable. Lower ones tend to be noisier.
- Wick tolerance: the tighter the market's wicks, the lower this should be. Forex tends to need tighter tolerance than crypto.
- Min body size: raising it makes the filter stricter; lowering it catches more candles at the cost of quality.
- Trend filter: useful for swing and position approaches, less so for scalping breakouts.

**How to use it for entries and exits**
Entry example: a bullish Marubozu forms above a trend filter on a higher timeframe. Buy at the next candle open, stop loss at the Marubozu low. Target the previous swing high or a defined risk-reward multiple.
Exit: if a bearish Marubozu appears after a long uptrend, close a partial position. It's not a reversal signal by itself, but it often precedes a pullback.

**Honest pros and cons**
Pros:
- Clean, no-fuss alerts
- Customizable wick tolerance (rare in free indicators)
- Works well with trend-following strategies

Cons:
- No multi-timeframe confirmation built-in (you'll need another script)
- On lower timeframes, false signals pile up fast
- No volume filter — a Marubozu on low volume is often a trap

**Who it's actually for**
Swing traders and position traders who already trade candlestick patterns manually. Scalpers will likely get whipsawed. Beginners may find it a useful learning tool to train their eye for Marubozus.

**Better alternatives if they exist**
- **Candlestick Patterns (by TradingView)** – Built-in, free, but less customizable for Marubozus.
- **Sniper Entry Signals** – Includes volume confirmation, but it's paid and more complex.

**FAQ addressing real trader questions**
*Q: Does it repaint?*
No. The label appears once the candle closes and stays fixed.

*Q: Can I use it for crypto day trading?*
Yes, but only on higher timeframes. On shorter ones, wick tolerance needs to be very tight to avoid misreads.

*Q: How does it handle gaps?*
Not well. A gap-up open with no upper wick might still show as a Marubozu. Check the candle manually if you trade stocks.

**Final verdict**
Marubozu_Detection does exactly what it promises: it spots Marubozu candles with adjustable parameters. It's not a holy grail, but it's a solid tool in a trend trader's toolkit. The lack of volume confirmation and multi-timeframe analysis keeps it from being exceptional. Still, for a single-pattern script, this is well above average.
