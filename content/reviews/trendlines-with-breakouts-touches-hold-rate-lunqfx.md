Most trendline indicators draw a line and call it a day. This one tries to answer the question that actually matters once a line is on your chart: did it hold, or did it break? That's the idea behind **Trendlines with Breakouts, Touches & Hold Rate [LunqFX]** — a trend-category tool that bundles automatic line detection with event tagging and a running "hold rate" metric.

One honest caveat before we go further: I don't have the publisher's full documentation in front of me, so this review stays at the conceptual level. I won't quote default settings, thresholds, or performance numbers I can't verify. What follows is what the tool is built to do and whether that's worth your chart real estate.

## What it actually does

The name is a feature list, and the tool delivers on each part:

- **Trendlines** — the indicator detects and plots trendlines on your chart rather than making you draw them by hand.
- **Breakouts** — when price moves through a detected line, the tool flags it as a breakout event.
- **Touches** — each time price tests a line without breaking it, that's tagged as a touch.
- **Hold rate** — the headline feature. The indicator tallies how often its lines have held versus broken, giving you a sense of how reliable the current structure has been.

That last piece is the differentiator. Plenty of tools draw trendlines. Very few tell you how their own lines have performed.

## Why the hold rate matters

A trendline is only useful if it means something. Anyone can draw a diagonal line across two swing points — the hard part is knowing whether the next touch is a bounce opportunity or the last gasp before a break.

By tracking touches against breaks, the hold rate gives you context for the line you're looking at. A line that's been touched repeatedly and held each time tells a different story than one that's been tested once and immediately broken. You're not just seeing structure — you're seeing how much the market has respected it. As shown in the chart above, the touches and breakouts are marked directly on price, so you can line up the history with the current setup.

Treat the hold rate as information, not a promise. Past holding behaviour is a description of what happened, not a guarantee of what comes next.

## How you'd use it

The workflow is straightforward and doesn't require much fiddling:

1. Add the indicator and let it detect trendlines on your chosen instrument and timeframe.
2. Watch the touches and breakouts as they print — each one tells you whether the line is being respected or rejected.
3. Check the hold rate to gauge how well the current structure has held up historically.
4. Combine with your own read on trend, volume, or momentum before acting. The tool describes structure; it doesn't generate entries.

That last point matters. This is a context tool, not a signal engine.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Automates a tedious job — drawing and maintaining trendlines by hand is time-consuming.
- The hold rate is a genuinely useful idea that most trendline tools skip entirely.
- Clear visual tagging of touches and breakouts keeps you from missing the moments that count.
- Conceptually simple to read, even if you're new to trendline trading.

**Cons**
- Trendline detection is inherently subjective; automated lines won't always match the ones you'd draw yourself.
- A hold rate is backward-looking — it describes history, not future probability.
- Without documented settings, tuning the tool to your taste may take trial and error.
- It complements a strategy rather than replacing one; expect it to raise questions as much as answer them.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already think in trendlines and want the drawing handled for them — swing traders mapping structure, and anyone who wants a quick read on how respected a given trend has been. If you trade purely off mechanical signals with no room for chart reading, this won't fit your process.

## FAQ

**Does the hold rate predict whether a line will hold next time?**
No. It measures how often lines have held historically. It's context, not a forecast.

**Can I use it on any timeframe or market?**
The tool is built for chart-based trendline detection, so it applies wherever trendlines make sense. I can't speak to which markets or timeframes it performs best on without the publisher's documentation.

**Does it give buy or sell signals?**
No. It flags touches, breakouts, and hold statistics — you decide what to do with them.

## Verdict

The hold rate is what elevates this above the pile of automatic-trendline indicators. It doesn't just draw structure; it tells you how well that structure has held, which is exactly the question a trendline trader should be asking. The trade-offs are real — automated lines are subjective and the hold rate is backward-looking — but as a context and structure tool, it earns its place on the chart.
