Stop hunts are one of those market behaviours that are obvious in hindsight and invisible in real time. The Liquidity Sweep Tracker sets out to change that — reading price action for the signature of a liquidity grab and marking it on your chart. This review covers what the tool attempts and where it earns its place in a workflow.

## What it actually does

The core premise is straightforward. Liquidity often pools above recent highs and below recent lows — resting stop orders and breakout entries that market makers and large participants can exploit. A sweep is the moment price pushes into that pool and then reverses, trapping the late crowd. The Liquidity Sweep Tracker watches for this pattern and plots it, turning an abstract "smart money" concept into something you can see on the chart.

That is the entire pitch, and it is a genuinely useful one. Most traders learn about liquidity sweeps from YouTube and then struggle to spot them live. A tracker that flags the behaviour mechanically removes a lot of the guesswork.

## How you'd use it

The natural workflow is as a confluence tool rather than a standalone signal. You take your existing bias — say, a downtrend you are looking to sell — and wait for the tracker to flag a sweep of highs before you commit. The sweep becomes the trigger; your trend read provides the direction.

Because the category sits under Trend, the tool is best thought of as a timing aid layered on top of a directional view. It is not a system that tells you what to buy or sell on its own, and treating it that way would be a mistake. On a chart like the one above, you would be scanning for the flagged sweeps and then asking: did this happen at a level I already cared about?

## Strengths

The biggest win is conceptual clarity. "Smart money" tools have a reputation for being black boxes dressed up in impressive language. This one is built on a single, well-defined market behaviour — the sweep — which means you can reason about why a signal appeared rather than just trusting it.

It also fills a real gap. Newer traders hear "stop hunt" constantly and have no systematic way to identify one. A tracker gives them a reference point to study from, which is arguably more educational than a pure buy/sell arrow. And because liquidity sweeps are a universal price action concept, the tool is not tied to one asset class or one style of trading — it applies wherever price pools orders.

## Weaknesses

The elephant in the room: without a documented parameter set, the exact logic for what counts as a "sweep" versus ordinary noise is opaque. How much of a wick, how much of a reversal, over what window — these determine everything about how many signals you get, and that is a black box from the outside. Expect to spend time calibrating to your market.

There is also a false-signal problem inherent to the concept. Plenty of pushes through highs and lows are genuine breakouts, not traps. Any sweep detector will mislabel some of these, and a flagged sweep on its own is not confirmation of anything. It is a prompt to look closer, not a reason to click.

Finally, the "smart money" framing invites over-trust. The label sounds authoritative; the underlying reality is a pattern heuristic. Keep that distinction in mind.

## Who it's for

Discretionary price action traders who already work with market structure and want a mechanical second pair of eyes. Also genuinely useful for anyone learning to spot liquidity grabs, because it provides a labelled reference to study against. It is less suited to pure systematic traders who want a fully specified, backtestable rule set — the undocumented internals make that difficult.

## FAQ

**Is it a buy/sell signal indicator?**
No. It flags liquidity sweeps, which are context events. The trade decision is still yours.

**Does it work on any timeframe or market?**
Liquidity sweeps are a general price action phenomenon, so the concept applies broadly, but signal frequency will differ by market and timeframe. Expect to tune.

**Can I use it alone?**
You can, but the sweeps are far more meaningful when combined with a directional bias and a key level.

## Verdict

A focused, well-conceived tool that tackles one specific behaviour instead of trying to do everything. The lack of documented internals and the inherent false-signal risk keep it from the top tier, but as a confluence and learning aid it holds up well.
