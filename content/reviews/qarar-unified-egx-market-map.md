## The Short Version

If you trade the Egyptian Exchange (EGX), you've probably felt the pain of fragmented data. This indicator pulls together market breadth, sector rotation, and individual stock momentum into one panel. It's not a magic signal machine — but it aims to be a unified dashboard for EGX traders.

## What This Indicator Actually Does

Qarar_Unified_Egx_Market_Map displays a heatmap-style matrix of EGX-listed stocks, grouped by sector. Each cell shows a stock's performance relative to its sector and the broader market, color-coded by momentum (green = strong, red = weak, yellow = neutral). Below the heatmap, it plots a market breadth line (advancers minus decliners) and a sector rotation tracker.

It's not predictive. It's descriptive — giving you a snapshot of where money is flowing *right now*.

## Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Sector-based grouping** — automatically sorts stocks into EGX sectors (banks, real estate, telecom, etc.). No manual setup.
- **Momentum scoring** — each stock gets a score based on price action relative to a moving average and RSI.
- **Breadth line** — plots the net difference between advancing and declining stocks. Useful for catching market-wide shifts before price confirms.
- **Custom timeframe** — works on any chart timeframe, but the data updates on the chart's resolution.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

The default settings are a reasonable starting point, but a few are worth adjusting depending on how much noise you can tolerate:

- **Momentum lookback**: controls how far back the momentum score looks. Shorter values react faster but produce more whipsaw; longer values smooth the score at the cost of responsiveness.
- **Breadth smoothing**: applies a moving average to the breadth line. A shorter smoothing period reacts quickly but can whipsaw on random ticks; a longer period filters noise but delays shifts.
- **Sector filter**: turning off "Show All" and keeping only the highest-volume sectors reduces the on-screen clutter considerably. The full list can be overwhelming depending on your screen size.

Chart timeframe choice matters here. The indicator's behavior changes with the chart's resolution, so pick a timeframe that matches your holding period rather than a very low one.

## How to Use It for Entries and Exits

**Entry example (long):**
1. Wait for the breadth line to cross above zero (net positive market).
2. Look for a sector where a majority of stocks show green.
3. Within that sector, pick the stock with the strongest momentum score — but avoid names that already look extended.
4. Enter on a pullback to a short-term moving average with a volume spike.

**Exit example:**
1. When that stock's momentum score weakens, or the breadth line turns negative, close at least half.
2. If the stock reaches an extreme momentum reading, consider taking full profit — those rarely sustain.

This framework tends to work better on trending days. In choppy sideways markets, the breadth line can give false positives.

## Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Saves hours of manual scanning. One glance tells you which sectors are hot.
- Breadth line is useful for gauging market-wide shifts.
- The heatmap itself updates tick by tick.

**Cons:**
- **Repainting on lower timeframes.** Very low timeframes are unreliable. Stick to higher ones.
- **No alert system.** You have to watch it manually — annoying for swing traders.
- **Overwhelming at first.** The default view shows a large number of stocks. You'll want to filter sectors.
- **EGX-only.** If you trade other exchanges, this is useless.

## Who It's Actually For

- **EGX swing traders** — a useful tool for gauging market mood.
- **Sector rotation players** — if you rotate between banks, real estate, and telecom based on momentum, this makes it visual.
- **Not for scalpers or crypto traders.** Wrong tool.

## Better Alternatives

There isn't a direct competitor for EGX data. For broader market maps, **Market Qloud** covers global markets (paid), but not EGX specifically. If you just need breadth, the **Advance-Decline Line** by TradingView is free and works on any exchange — but lacks sector grouping.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it repaint?**
A: On lower timeframes, yes. On higher timeframes, the momentum score updates only on close.

**Q: Can I use it on crypto?**
A: No. It's hardcoded for EGX stocks. The data source only pulls from the Cairo exchange.

**Q: Is it worth paying for?**
A: It's free. If you trade EGX, it's worth installing. If you don't, skip it.

**Q: How do I reduce clutter?**
A: In settings, enable the volume filter and set a sensible minimum. Also turn off sectors with very few stocks.

## Final Verdict

Qarar_Unified_Egx_Market_Map is a niche tool that does one thing: giving EGX traders a real-time market map without bloated features. It's not a holy grail, but it's a solid addition to an EGX-focused chart setup. The repainting issue on low timeframes and the lack of alerts keep it from being perfect — but for swing traders on higher timeframes, it's a reasonable tool.

**Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)** — Install it, filter the sectors, and pair it with price action.
