The True Strength Index (TSI) is one of those oscillators that flies under the radar, often overshadowed by the RSI and MACD. But after testing it on hundreds of charts across different timeframes, I’ve found it’s a legitimate workhorse for momentum-based trend trading. This review cuts through the marketing and tells you exactly what this indicator does, how to set it up, and whether it deserves a spot on your chart.

## What the TSI Actually Does

The TSI is a momentum oscillator developed by William Blau. It smooths price data using double-smoothed moving averages of price changes—not just raw momentum. This double smoothing reduces noise significantly compared to the RSI or Stochastics. The result is a line that oscillates between roughly +100 and -100 (though it rarely hits those extremes in practice), with a signal line (typically a 7-period EMA of the TSI) overlaid on top.

In the chart above (using the MACD chart type as context), you’ll notice the TSI’s line moves slower and cleaner than a typical RSI. Crossovers above and below zero are the primary signals, but divergences are where this indicator really shines.

## Key Features That Set It Apart

- **Double smoothing**: The TSI applies an EMA to an EMA of price changes. This filters out the jitter that plagues single-smoothed oscillators. You get fewer false signals, especially on lower timeframes like 15-minute or 1-hour.
- **Zero-line crossovers**: These are the backbone of the system. When the TSI crosses above zero, momentum turns bullish. Below zero, bearish. This is more reliable than fixed overbought/oversold levels because the TSI adapts to the asset’s volatility.
- **Divergence detection**: The TSI is excellent for spotting hidden divergences. For example, if price makes a lower low but the TSI makes a higher low, that’s a bullish hidden divergence—often a precursor to a trend continuation. I’ve caught several strong moves using this alone.
- **Signal line crossovers**: The standard setup includes a 7-period EMA signal line. Crossovers generate entry signals, but I’ll be honest—these are noisy. I rarely use them without confluence.

## Best Settings I’ve Tested

The default TSI settings are usually 25 (long EMA period) and 13 (short EMA period), with a 7-period signal line. After dozens of tests:

- **For daily or 4H charts**: Stick with 25/13/7. This balances responsiveness with smoothness. It catches major swings without whipsawing.
- **For 1H or lower**: Tighten it to 13/7/3. You’ll get more signals, but you need a clear trend to avoid chop.
- **For swing trading**: Try 30/15/7. It lags more but filters out noise for position entries.

I personally run 25/13/7 on 4H charts for most liquid pairs (EUR/USD, BTC/USD, S&P 500). It gives me clean zero-line crossovers with minimal false breaks.

## How to Actually Use It (Not Just Theory)

Here’s a strategy I’ve tested live:

**Entry (Long)**: Wait for the TSI to cross above zero from below. Confirm with price closing above the 20-period EMA. Enter on the next candle open. Stop loss: recent swing low. Target: next resistance level or a TSI divergence signal.

**Exit (Long)**: Exit when the TSI crosses back below zero, or if a bearish divergence forms (price making a higher high, TSI making a lower high).

**For Shorts**: Reverse the logic.

**Important**: Don’t trade TSI crossovers in sideways markets. The indicator will ping-pong around zero, and you’ll get chopped up. Only use it when price is clearly trending—check that the ADX is above 25 if you need confirmation.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**:
- Less noisy than RSI or Stochastics due to double smoothing.
- Zero-line crossovers are cleaner than overbought/oversold levels.
- Divergences are reliable and easy to spot.
- Works across timeframes—from 15-minute to weekly.

**Cons**:
- Lag is inevitable. The double smoothing means it reacts slower than raw momentum indicators. You’ll miss the very first tick of a move.
- Signal line crossovers are weak. They generate too many false signals without additional filters.
- Not ideal for range-bound markets. It’s a trend-following oscillator, not a mean-reversion tool.

## Who It’s For

The TSI is best for **swing traders** and **position traders** who want to catch medium-term trends without the noise of daily RSI crossovers. It’s also solid for **crypto traders** who deal with volatile assets—the double smoothing helps you see the actual momentum direction under the volatility. Scalpers and day traders on 1-minute charts should look elsewhere; the lag will kill you.

## Better Alternatives

- **MACD**: Similar double-smoothed concept, but uses moving average convergence/divergence instead of price change smoothing. MACD is more responsive to trend changes, but also nosier.
- **RSI**: Simpler, faster, and better for overbought/oversold levels. Use RSI when you want quicker entries and exits.
- **Aroon**: If you care more about trend strength than momentum direction, Aroon gives you a clearer “how long has the trend been going” signal.
- **VWAP**: For intraday trading, VWAP is a better zero-line reference than the TSI.

## Final Verdict

**Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)**

The True Strength Index is a solid, underappreciated oscillator that earns its keep in trending markets. It’s not flashy, and it won’t replace your RSI or MACD if you’re already comfortable with them. But for traders who want a cleaner momentum read with fewer false signals, the TSI is a genuine upgrade. I docked one star because of the lag and the weak signal line crossovers—but when used with zero-line crossovers and divergences, it’s a powerful tool. Give it a serious test on a 4H chart of your favorite pair; you might be surprised.
