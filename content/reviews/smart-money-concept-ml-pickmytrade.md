There's a crowded genre of TradingView scripts that borrow the "smart money" label and dress up a moving average in institutional language. This one is different in intent, at least on paper: Smart Money Concept ML [PickMyTrade] aims to combine two ideas that don't usually share a chart — classic smart-money concepts and a machine-learning component — to read trend direction. Note upfront: I could not retrieve the official description or documentation for this indicator, so this review sticks to the concept and the category rather than any specific settings or numbers. Treat it as a framework for evaluating the tool, not a spec sheet.

## What it's trying to do

Smart money concepts, as most traders use the term, are about structure: where liquidity sits, where price has swept stops before reversing, and whether the current push is genuine participation or a trap. That's inherently a discretionary, pattern-driven read. Machine learning, by contrast, is about fitting a model to historical data and letting it classify what comes next. Marrying the two is ambitious, and it's the reason this indicator lands in the Trend category rather than as a pure pattern scanner.

The practical output, as the category implies, is directional bias. The ML layer is effectively being asked to arbitrate the messy, subjective parts of smart-money reading — and that's the whole pitch.

## Where it earns attention

The core idea has merit. Most smart-money scripts just draw boxes around swing highs and lows and call it a day. Adding a model layer that can weigh multiple structural inputs at once is a legitimate step up in ambition, and the "ML" framing suggests the author is at least trying to move beyond fixed rules. If the model is doing real work, it could smooth out the false signals that plague hand-drawn order-block and liquidity-sweep tools.

The PickMyTrade association also matters — this is built by a group with a trading-tools background, not an anonymous one-off script. That's a mild quality signal, though not proof of anything.

## How you'd actually use it

Without documented inputs, the honest guidance is process-based. Treat the indicator as a bias filter, not an entry trigger. Let it tell you which side of the market the model currently favours, then do your own structural work — mark the levels, wait for price to react at them — and only take trades that agree with the bias. If it disagrees with your read, that's information, not an error.

As shown in the chart above, the value is in the direction call, not in the exact signal timing. Don't expect it to hand you entries.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Attempts something genuinely harder than most "smart money" scripts — combining structure logic with a model layer.
- Trend-focused output is easy to fold into an existing workflow as a bias filter.
- Backed by a named tooling group rather than an anonymous upload.

**Cons**
- With no published documentation, transparency is the big question mark. A machine-learning component whose inputs and training you can't inspect is a black box, and black boxes deserve skepticism.
- ML models are only as good as their training assumptions; behaviour on markets or regimes the model didn't see is unpredictable.
- The "smart money" label carries a lot of hype weight — this category attracts overclaiming.

## Who it's for

Discretionary trend traders who already read market structure and want a second opinion on direction. Also useful for traders who like the smart-money vocabulary but want something more systematic than eyeballing order blocks. It's a poor fit for anyone wanting a mechanical, fully documented signal generator, and a worse fit for pure scalpers who need sub-bar precision.

**Does it replace my own analysis?** No. Use it as a bias layer; keep your structural work.

## Verdict

The concept is stronger than most of its shelf-mates, and the ambition is real — but the lack of transparent documentation holds it back from the top tier. It's a solid ⭐⭐⭐⭐ for traders who want a trend bias with a smart-money flavour and are comfortable treating it as a filter rather than gospel. If the author publishes proper docs on the model, this could easily justify a higher score. Until then, install it, watch it, and let your own process carry the weight.
