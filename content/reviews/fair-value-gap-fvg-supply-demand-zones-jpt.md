Fair Value Gap FVG Supply Demand Zones Jpt is a TradingView tool built around two ideas that dominate modern price-action trading: the fair value gap (FVG) and the supply/demand zone. Both concepts come from the same root — an aggressive, one-sided move that leaves an imbalance behind. This indicator's job is to find those imbalances and draw them on your chart so you don't have to hunt for them candle by candle.

That's the pitch, and it's a crowded one. The question is whether the tool earns a spot in your layout.

## What it actually does

A fair value gap is the space left when price moves so fast that one side of the order book can't keep up. In candle terms, it's the gap between the wick of one candle and the wick of a candle a couple of bars later. Price often returns to that gap later to "fill" the imbalance. Supply and demand zones are the broader version of the same idea: a base of consolidation followed by a strong departure, marking an area where resting orders may still sit.

This indicator automates the detection of both. Instead of manually scanning for three-candle patterns or drawing boxes around every impulse leg, you get the zones plotted for you. That's the core value proposition — pattern recognition at scale.

## The concept behind it

If you've spent any time in the smart money / ICT corner of TradingView, you know the terminology. Fair value gaps, order blocks, liquidity voids, supply and demand — they all describe the same market mechanic: imbalance. The theory is that price is a two-way auction, and when one side wins too decisively, the market tends to revisit the scene of the crime to rebalance.

Whether you buy the theory or not, the practical use is real. Traders use these zones as areas of interest — places to watch for a reaction, a rejection, or a continuation. The indicator doesn't tell you what to do at the zone. It tells you where the zone is.

## How traders typically use it

The workflow is straightforward. Load the indicator, let it mark the gaps and zones, then treat those levels as context for your own decisions. A common approach is to combine it with a trend filter — a moving average, a market structure read, or a momentum oscillator — so you're only trading zones that align with the broader direction.

As shown in the chart above, the zones stack up over time, which is both the strength and the weakness of any tool like this. You get a lot of information. You also get a lot of clutter if you don't filter.

The natural use cases:

- **Trend continuation.** Wait for price to pull back into an unmitigated zone that lines up with the prevailing trend, then look for a reaction.
- **Reversal context.** A zone that price rejects hard can mark the start of a new leg the other way.
- **Target selection.** Unfilled gaps can serve as logical take-profit areas, since price often gravitates toward them.

None of this is unique to this indicator. What matters is execution — how cleanly the zones are drawn, how well they update, and whether the visual output helps or hurts your read.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Automates two of the most popular price-action concepts in one overlay.
- Saves real time versus manual zone drawing, especially on lower timeframes where gaps form constantly.
- Works as a context layer rather than a signal generator, which means it plays nicely with whatever strategy you already run.
- The supply/demand and FVG combination covers both the short-term imbalance and the broader zone, which is a sensible pairing.

**Cons**

- Zone-based tools are inherently subjective. Two traders will disagree on which gaps matter, and the indicator can't resolve that for you.
- On noisy timeframes, the chart fills with boxes fast. Without a plan for filtering, it becomes visual noise rather than an edge.
- Like any pattern-detection overlay, it identifies structure but says nothing about probability. A zone is a zone — it doesn't come with a win rate attached.
- No amount of automation replaces the judgment call of whether a level is worth trading.

## Who it's for

Discretionary price-action traders who already think in terms of imbalance and want the drawing done for them. If you're a systematic, indicator-driven trader looking for entries and exits handed to you, this isn't that. If you're a beginner, the tool will show you what an FVG looks like, but you'll still need to learn why some gaps get respected and others get steamrolled.

It suits intraday and swing traders on liquid markets best, where these patterns are most visible and most discussed.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
No. It plots zones. The decision to act on them is yours.

**Can I use it on any timeframe?**
Yes, though the density of zones changes with the timeframe — lower timeframes produce more of them.

**Is it a standalone strategy?**
Treat it as a context tool. Pair it with your own entry logic and risk management.

**Will it make me profitable?**
No indicator does that on its own. It shows you where imbalances exist; it doesn't tell you how price will behave at them.

## Final verdict

Fair Value Gap FVG Supply Demand Zones Jpt does one job and does it without drama: it finds gaps and zones and draws them. For traders who already work in this framework, that's a genuine time-saver and a clean addition to the chart. For everyone else, it's a visual aid that still requires you to bring the analysis.

It isn't revolutionary, and it won't replace your judgment. But as a context layer for price-action trading, it earns its place.
