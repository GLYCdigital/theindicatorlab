A solid tool if you trade Elliott Wave and want zigzags, flats, and triangles plotted automatically instead of drawn by hand. It isn't perfect, but it cuts down chart time.

**What This Indicator Actually Does**
The script scans price action for corrective Elliott Wave structures—zigzags, flats, and triangles—and plots them directly on the chart with labels. It's a pattern recognizer, not a forecasting tool. It surfaces the typical A-B-C formations that follow impulsive moves; it doesn't tell you what happens next.

**Key Features That Set It Apart**
- **Real-time labeling**: Marks sub-waves (A, B, C) and the structure type, so you aren't guessing at the count.
- **Customizable lookback**: Adjustable across timeframes, from intraday up to monthly.
- **Alert system**: Can notify you when a corrective pattern completes, which is useful for reversal setups.
- **No repainting in default mode**: Once a pattern is drawn, it holds. Changing the lookback mid-chart can force a redraw, so keep settings fixed if you rely on the labels.

**Settings and How to Tune Them**
- **Lookback period**: Governs how much price history the script scans. Shorter lookbacks pick up more patterns but more noise; longer lookbacks reduce noise but introduce lag.
- **Minimum wave size**: A filter on how small a wave can be before it's ignored. Raising it strips out minor structures; lowering it lets more through.
- **Pattern filter**: An option to show only major patterns, which reduces clutter on lower timeframes.
- **Color scheme**: Assign colors per pattern type so you can scan the chart quickly.

Treat these as asset-specific. The settings that work on one instrument won't necessarily transfer to another, so expect to retune per market rather than setting once and forgetting.

**How to Use It for Entries and Exits**
- **Entry**: Wait for a confirmed C-wave completion, which the indicator labels. Then take a counter-trend position in the direction of the larger trend. If a flat completes at a key support or resistance level, that's the setup—stop goes beyond the B-wave extreme.
- **Exit**: Use the next impulsive wave projection from the corrective pattern's start, or trail with a moving average.
- **Avoid**: Don't trade every pattern. Take only setups that align with higher-timeframe structure—a daily uptrend plus a corrective formation on a lower timeframe, for example.

**Honest Pros and Cons**
**Pros**:
- Saves hours of manual wave counting.
- Alerts are reliable enough to act on.
- Works across markets—stocks, crypto, forex.

**Cons**:
- False positives in choppy conditions, such as around news events.
- Heavily dependent on input settings.
- No built-in risk management; stop and target logic are on you.

**Who It's Actually For**
Intermediate-to-advanced Elliott Wave traders who already understand corrective structures. Beginners will find the labels confusing. If you don't know what a zigzag is, this isn't the place to start. If you do, it's a meaningful time-saver.

**Better Alternatives**
- **Elliott Wave Prophet**: More advanced, but paid and heavier on lower timeframes.
- **Auto Fibonacci**: For retracement levels; pairs well with this indicator.
- **Manual drawing**: Still the standard for purists—this is a helper, not a replacement.

**FAQ**
**Q: Does it repaint?**
A: In default mode, no. Changing the lookback mid-chart may cause a redraw, so keep settings fixed.

**Q: Can it detect all corrective patterns?**
A: Mainly zigzags, flats, and triangles. It misses rarer structures like double zigzags.

**Q: Best timeframe?**
A: Mid-range intraday to swing timeframes tend to be the practical window. Very low timeframes produce noise; very high timeframes produce few signals.
