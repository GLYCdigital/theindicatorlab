Divergence trading has a persistent problem: a divergence is easy to see in hindsight and much harder to trust in real time. Price makes a new low, the oscillator refuses to confirm, and you're left deciding whether that's a reversal signal or just noise before the next leg down. This indicator takes a crack at that problem by pairing RSI divergence detection with follow-through statistics — the idea being that instead of simply flagging a divergence and leaving you to guess, it frames what has tended to happen after comparable signals.

That framing is the whole appeal, and it's worth being clear about what it is and isn't before you add it to a chart.

## What the indicator actually does

At its core, the tool does two things. First, it scans for RSI divergences — the classic condition where price and momentum disagree at swing points. Second, it layers follow-through statistics on top, so the divergence isn't presented as a bare arrow but alongside a statistical view of how such signals have resolved on the instrument and timeframe you're looking at.

The name's "Dots" element points to the visual convention: divergences are plotted as dots on the chart rather than as lines or shaded zones, keeping the price panel readable. That's a deliberate design choice, and a sensible one — divergence lines can clutter a chart fast, especially on lower timeframes where they fire often.

## Where it earns its keep

The follow-through statistics are the differentiator. Most divergence indicators stop at detection, which means every divergence looks equally important. This one asks a more useful question: given the divergences this tool has flagged on this market, what has the aftermath looked like? That reframes divergence from a binary signal into something closer to a probabilistic one.

The dot-based plotting is a genuine usability win. It doesn't fight with your existing chart work, and it keeps the indicator in the background where it belongs until a signal appears. If you already run several overlays, this matters more than it sounds.

It also sits naturally in the trend-analysis category. Divergence is often read as an early warning of trend exhaustion, and pairing that with a statistical layer fits how many discretionary traders actually think — not "buy here" but "the odds of continuation look weaker than they did."

## How you'd actually use it

The workflow is straightforward. Apply it to a chart, let it mark divergences with dots, and treat the follow-through statistics as context rather than a verdict. A bullish divergence with a strong historical follow-through on that instrument carries more weight than one with a weak record.

The honest way to use it is as a filter on your existing process. If you already trade reversal setups or trend exhaustion, this gives you a second opinion on whether a given divergence is worth acting on. It is not a standalone entry engine, and nothing in how the tool is structured suggests it should be treated as one.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Combines two things most indicators keep separate: detection and context.
- The follow-through statistics add a layer of realism that plain divergence tools lack.
- Dot-based plotting keeps charts clean and doesn't obscure price action.
- Fits naturally alongside existing trend or momentum work.

**Cons**
- Follow-through statistics are only as meaningful as the sample behind them — on thin instruments or unusual timeframes, the stats can be misleading.
- Divergence detection is inherently subjective at the edges, and no indicator resolves that fully.
- It's a context tool, not a signal generator, which will disappoint anyone looking for turnkey entries.
- The naming is dense and the concept takes a moment to unpack.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already use RSI or momentum divergence and want a statistical sanity check before acting. Swing traders working on higher timeframes, where divergence signals are fewer and each one matters more, will get the most from the follow-through layer. It's less suited to scalpers who need fast, unambiguous triggers, and to anyone who wants an indicator to make the decision for them.

## FAQ

**Does it replace my own divergence analysis?**
No. It adds a statistical dimension to divergence detection. You still need to interpret the signal in context.

**Will the follow-through statistics be reliable on any market?**
Reliability depends on how much historical signal data exists for the instrument and timeframe you're viewing. Thin markets and unusual timeframes give you less to work with.

**Is it a buy/sell signal indicator?**
It's better understood as a context and filtering tool. Treating the dots as standalone entries misses the point of the statistics layer.

## Verdict

This is a thoughtful take on a well-worn concept. Divergence indicators are everywhere; ones that attach follow-through statistics to the signal are rarer, and the dot-based presentation keeps the chart usable. It won't hand you entries, and the statistics demand interpretation rather than blind trust — but for traders who already work with divergence and want a probabilistic edge on top of it, it's a worthwhile addition.
