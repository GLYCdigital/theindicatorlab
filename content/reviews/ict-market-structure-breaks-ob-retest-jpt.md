ICT methodology has a reputation problem. Half the scripts on TradingView slap "ICT" on a moving average crossover and call it smart money. So when a tool names itself after three specific concepts — market structure breaks, order blocks, and retests — the honest question is whether it actually does any of that work, or just borrows the vocabulary.

This one does the work, at least conceptually. Here's the breakdown.

## What it actually does

The name is the spec sheet. This indicator is built around three linked ideas from ICT-style analysis:

- **Market structure breaks** — the shifts in swing highs and lows that signal a change in directional bias.
- **Order blocks** — the candle zones that precede those breaks, treated as areas of institutional interest.
- **Retests** — price returning to those zones after the break, which is where ICT traders typically look for entries.

The indicator's job is to surface those events on the chart so you don't have to eyeball every swing. Instead of manually marking structure and hunting for the origin candle, the tool flags the break and the zone it came from, then tracks whether price comes back to it.

That's the entire premise, and it's a coherent one. The three concepts aren't random — they're sequential. Break, zone, retest. The indicator is essentially automating a workflow that discretionary ICT traders already run by hand.

## Why it earns its place

Most "structure" indicators stop at drawing a line where the swing broke. That's the easy part. The harder part is the follow-through: remembering which zone mattered, and staying patient when price wanders back toward it.

By tying the order block to the structure break that created it, this tool keeps that context attached to the chart. You're not re-deriving the setup every time you open a timeframe. That continuity is the real value — it reduces the mental bookkeeping that makes ICT-style trading exhausting across multiple pairs.

As shown in the chart above, the visual output is the point: structure events and their associated zones rendered directly on price, so the sequence is readable at a glance.

## How to use it

Treat it as a map, not a signal generator.

The sensible workflow is to let the indicator mark the structure break first, note the order block it highlights, then wait for a retest before considering anything. The tool tells you where the interesting zones are. It does not tell you whether price will respect them — that's still your read on context, higher-timeframe bias, and session timing.

This is the part where ICT tools get misused. People see a marked zone and treat it as a buy or sell button. It isn't. A retest is a location, not a confirmation. The indicator narrows your attention; it doesn't make the decision.

If you already trade structure, this slots into your existing process cleanly. If you don't, no indicator will teach you the framework — it'll just draw boxes you don't know how to interpret.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Automates a genuinely tedious manual process — marking structure and tracking origin zones.
- Keeps the break-to-zone relationship intact, which is the part most tools drop.
- Conceptually clean: three linked ICT concepts, not a kitchen-sink mashup.
- Useful as a visual checklist for traders who already know the methodology.

**Cons**
- Order blocks and structure breaks are interpretation-dependent. Different traders define them differently, and no script resolves that debate.
- No indicator can tell you whether a retest will hold. The tool is descriptive, not predictive.
- If you don't already understand ICT logic, the output will look like arbitrary rectangles.
- It's a trend-category tool, but structure breaks are inherently reactive — they confirm after the move, not before.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already work in an ICT or smart-money framework and want to cut down the manual charting. It's also worth a look for price-action traders who mark structure by hand and want that layer automated.

It is not for anyone looking for entry signals, alerts to trade blindly, or a shortcut past learning the methodology. If you want a system that tells you when to buy, this isn't it and doesn't pretend to be.

## FAQ

**Does it generate buy and sell signals?**
No. It maps structure breaks, order blocks, and retests. Entries remain your decision.

**Can I use it on any market?**
Structure and order blocks are universal price concepts, so the logic applies broadly. Whether it suits your specific instrument is something you'd judge on your own charts.

**Do I need to know ICT to use it?**
Practically, yes. The indicator visualizes a framework — it doesn't teach it.

**Is it a standalone strategy?**
Treat it as a confluence tool, not a system.

## Verdict

This is a focused tool that does one job and does it honestly. It doesn't oversell, doesn't bolt on a dozen unrelated features, and doesn't promise outcomes it can't deliver. The sequencing — break, zone, retest — is exactly how ICT traders think, and automating that saves real screen time.

The ceiling is set by the methodology itself, not the code. Order blocks are subjective, retests are probabilistic, and no script changes that. But within those limits, this is a well-conceived addition to an ICT workflow.
