Some indicators try to do everything. This one doesn't. Elder Ray Reach Balance [MarkitTick] is a focused study built around Alexander Elder's Ray concept — the idea of measuring who is actually in control of a move by comparing price against a reference point. If you already trade Elder's framework, this is a familiar tool wearing a new label. If you don't, it's a clean way to start.

Here's the honest version of what it is, where it helps, and where it won't.

## What this indicator actually does

The Elder Ray concept splits market pressure into two forces: bull power and bear power. Bull power reflects how far the strongest buyers have pushed price above a baseline; bear power reflects how far sellers have pushed it below. The point is not to predict, but to gauge which side currently holds the upper hand relative to that baseline.

Elder Ray Reach Balance [MarkitTick] packages this into a single TradingView study and sits in the Trend category. The "Balance" in the name points at the comparative reading — the interplay between the two forces rather than one in isolation. That's the whole idea: a bull reading alone tells you buyers are active, but it says less than seeing that reading against what sellers are doing at the same time.

Beyond that framing, the source material is thin. There's no published breakdown of default settings, calculation windows, or signal rules. So treat this review as a description of the concept and the tool's role on a chart — not a manual.

## How you'd actually use it

Because the indicator is a study rather than a strategy, it plots on your chart and gives you a read on momentum balance. The practical workflow for Elder Ray readings generally looks like this:

- **Confirm the trend first.** The Ray readings are most meaningful when you already know the direction you're trading. Use price structure or a moving average as your primary filter.
- **Read the balance.** Watch whether the bull side or bear side is dominant, and whether that dominance is building or fading.
- **Look for divergence.** The classic use of Elder Ray is spotting when price makes a new extreme but the corresponding power reading doesn't — a hint that the move is losing fuel.
- **Combine, don't isolate.** No single study should carry your entry decision. This one is a confirmation layer.

As shown in the chart above, the indicator renders as a study alongside price — the kind of secondary panel you glance at rather than stare at.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Grounded in a well-established, well-documented trading concept (Elder's Ray), so the logic isn't a black box invented for the sake of a script.
- Single-purpose. It does one job and doesn't bury you in clutter.
- Free to install on TradingView and easy to layer onto an existing setup.
- The "balance" framing adds a comparative dimension that a bare bull/bear read doesn't give you.

**Cons**
- The documentation is minimal. You won't find a clear spec of inputs, defaults, or intended signal logic in the source material — expect to do some interpretation.
- It's a study, not a strategy, so there's no built-in backtesting, alerts logic, or performance readout out of the box.
- As with any trend/momentum tool, it lags. Ray readings describe what buyers and sellers have done, not what they will do.
- Nothing here is genuinely new if you've used Elder Ray elsewhere — the value is convenience, not novelty.

## Who it's for

This suits traders who already think in terms of buying and selling pressure and want a lightweight confirmation tool on their chart. Swing traders and position traders who filter with trend first, then refine with momentum, will get the most from it. It's also a reasonable entry point for someone learning Elder's work who wants to see the concept applied visually rather than reading about it.

Discretionary scalpers chasing fast signals will probably find it too slow. And anyone expecting a plug-and-play system with defined buy/sell markers will be disappointed — that's not what a study in this category is.

## FAQ

**Is this a buy/sell signal indicator?**
No. It's a study that visualizes bull and bear power balance. You interpret it; it doesn't tell you to click.

**Do I need to know Elder's theory to use it?**
It helps a lot. The readings make more sense if you understand what bull and bear power represent. Without that context, you're looking at lines without a story.

**Can I use it on any timeframe or market?**
The source doesn't specify restrictions, so it plots wherever you apply it. Whether the readings are useful on very short timeframes is a judgment call you'll have to make yourself.

**Does it repaint?**
The source material doesn't address this. Don't assume either way — check the behaviour on your own chart.

## Final verdict

Elder Ray Reach Balance [MarkitTick] is a solid, focused implementation of a proven concept. It won't wow you with features, and the thin documentation means you'll spend some time figuring out exactly how it's calculating and what the defaults are. But the underlying idea is sound, the tool is free, and it slots neatly into a trend-following workflow as a confirmation layer.

It loses a star for the lack of documentation and the fact that it's essentially a repackaging of an established method rather than an original contribution. If you trade Elder's framework or want a clean momentum-balance read, install it. If you're looking for a self-contained system, keep looking.
