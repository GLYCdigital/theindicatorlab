Most "smart money" indicators are a marketing wrapper around the same three drawings. This one names them outright in the title: liquidity sweeps, order blocks, and fair value gaps. Whether that's a selling point or a warning depends on what you expect the tool to do for you.

## What it actually is

The name is descriptive rather than cryptic. The indicator maps three of the core concepts from the Smart Money / ICT school of price action onto your chart:

- **Liquidity sweeps** — the moments where price pushes through an obvious high or low, takes out resting orders, and reverses. The idea is that a wick through a prior swing isn't a breakout, it's a stop hunt.
- **Order blocks** — the last opposing candle before an impulsive move, treated as a zone where larger participants may have left orders.
- **Fair value gaps** — the imbalance left behind when price moves so fast it skips a stretch of the chart, which the methodology expects price to revisit.

The indicator's job is to mark these zones for you so you aren't hand-drawing them candle by candle. That's it. It is a visual annotation layer, not a signal generator, and how you treat each zone is entirely your call.

## Where the value sits

The honest appeal here is time. Drawing order blocks and FVGs by hand is slow and subjective — two traders looking at the same chart will disagree on where a zone starts and ends. A tool that applies consistent rules to the same pattern saves you that friction and keeps your charts comparable from one session to the next.

Having all three concepts in a single indicator also matters more than it sounds. Liquidity sweeps, order blocks and FVGs are meant to be read together — a sweep into an order block that leaves a gap above it is a different story than any one of those alone. Consolidating them into one overlay keeps you from stacking three separate scripts and fighting over chart real estate.

## How to actually use it

The workflow is straightforward, and that's a point in its favour:

1. **Let the indicator mark the zones.** Your first job is orientation, not action.
2. **Filter for confluence.** A zone that lines up with a liquidity sweep and sits in the direction of the broader trend deserves more attention than an isolated box.
3. **Wait for the reaction.** The zones are context. The trigger — a rejection, a reclaim, a displacement — is still yours to identify.
4. **Use zones for risk placement.** The edges of an order block or the far side of a gap give you logical invalidation levels, which is often the most practical use of the whole toolkit.

The trap is treating every drawn zone as a trade. It isn't. On any active chart you'll see plenty of boxes and gaps, and most of them are noise. The indicator gives you candidates; you supply the judgement.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Consolidates three related SMC concepts into one overlay instead of three.
- Removes the subjectivity of hand-drawn zones, keeping your charts consistent.
- Visual and immediate — you can read the market's structure at a glance.
- Naturally suited to risk placement around zone boundaries.

**Cons**
- It annotates; it doesn't decide. Anyone hoping for entries handed to them will be disappointed.
- Chart clutter is a real risk. Dense zones on busy price action can obscure as much as they reveal.
- The underlying methodology is interpretive. It reflects one school's reading of price, not an objective truth, and two users can disagree on what "counts."
- As with any zone-based approach, plenty of marked levels will simply fail — the tool doesn't rank or filter them for you.

## Who it's for

Discretionary price-action traders already working within the Smart Money / ICT framework will get the most out of this — it automates the tedious part of a process they already believe in. Swing and intraday traders who plan around structural levels rather than lagging averages are a natural fit.

It's a poor match for anyone wanting a mechanical, rules-based system with alerts doing the thinking, or for pure trend-followers who don't trade reversions into zones.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
No. It maps zones and events. You interpret them.

**Can I use it on any market or timeframe?**
The concepts are timeframe-agnostic in principle, but that's a statement about the methodology, not a tested claim about this tool. Test it on your own instrument before trusting it.

**Will it replace my own analysis?**
No. It's a drawing aid. The judgment — which zones matter, when to act — stays with you.

## Verdict

A clean, focused implementation of a popular methodology. It does the tedious work of marking sweeps, order blocks and gaps so you can spend your attention on the decision instead of the drawing. It won't think for you, and the clutter problem is real, but as a visual framework for discretionary SMC traders it earns its place on the chart.
