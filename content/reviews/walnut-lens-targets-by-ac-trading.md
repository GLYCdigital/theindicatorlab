Most indicators on TradingView try to generate signals. This one does something narrower and, for the right trader, more useful: it turns a daily set of hand-posted price levels into a live, self-updating overlay on your chart. Walnut Lens Targets by AC Trading is a companion tool for the daily targets map published by Pauli Walnuts, built specifically for ES and MES futures.

If you don't follow that map, this indicator has nothing to offer you. If you do, it removes a lot of manual drudgery.

## What it actually does

The core mechanic is simple. Each day you take the posted buyer targets, seller targets, and danger zones and paste them into the indicator's "Paste levels" box. The format is documented: **buyers | sellers | buyer danger | seller danger**, using spaces or commas, with up to five targets per side. The example given is `7445 7472 7520 | 7350 7315 | 7560 | 7310`. If you leave the paste box empty, the indicator falls back to its individual level inputs.

From there, it draws those levels on your chart, anchored from the 18:00 ET open through the cash close. Buyer targets render in teal, seller targets in coral, and the exhaustion/danger zones appear as shaded boxes. As shown in the chart above, the levels sit as clean horizontal references rather than anything generated by price action.

## The live-tracking layer

This is where the script earns its keep. Every target carries a status: either NOT HIT YET or HIT, stamped with the ET time it was touched. When a level gets hit, it doesn't vanish — it stays on the chart as a faded dashed line, so you can keep reading it as potential support or resistance after the fact.

That distinction matters. A tool that deletes a level the moment price touches it is telling you the level is finished. This one treats it as a reference that's now been validated, which is closer to how most traders actually use these maps intraday.

There's also a status table that consolidates the session picture: session high and low, the next buyer and seller target with distance from current price, which targets have been hit, and the current zone. For anyone who doesn't want to eyeball five horizontal lines while managing a position, that table is the practical center of gravity.

## Setup and housekeeping details

A few settings are worth flagging because they solve real problems rather than decorating the chart:

- **Level offset** shifts every level by a set number of points. This exists because futures contracts roll, and levels posted for one contract don't line up on the next.
- **Label vertical offset** moves label text off its line — negative values put it below — so labels don't obscure candles.
- **Price scale tags** are optional and can be finicky. The documentation notes that if they don't appear, you need to right-click the price scale, go to Labels, and enable "Indicators and financials value."

The indicator also marks the 09:30 open and 16:00 cash close, and clears the prior session's drawings each day so stale levels don't masquerade as current ones. That last behavior is easy to overlook and hard to live without once you've relied on a tool that doesn't do it.

## Alerts

Two alert paths are documented. The simplest is a single alert set to "Any alert() function call," which fires a message whenever a target is hit or price enters a danger zone. If you want finer control, separate alert conditions exist for buyer hits, seller hits, and each individual danger zone. That's a sensible split — some traders want one catch-all, others want to route buyer-side and seller-side notifications differently.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Directly automates a manual, repetitive workflow: transcribing a posted map into chart levels.
- Live hit status with timestamps removes the guesswork of "did we already trade through that?"
- Hit levels persisting as dashed lines is the right design choice for intraday reference.
- Offset setting handles contract rolls without re-entering everything.
- Session reset prevents yesterday's levels from contaminating today's read.

**Cons**
- Entirely dependent on an external daily post. No map, no indicator — it generates nothing on its own.
- The paste format is rigid. Get the pipe delimiters or side order wrong and you'll be troubleshooting instead of trading.
- Price scale tags require a manual chart setting that will confuse first-time users.
- It's a level-visualization tool, not a strategy. It won't tell you what to do at a level, only that you've reached one.

## Who it's for

Discretionary ES and MES intraday traders who already use the Walnut Lens Targets Map and are tired of hand-plotting levels every morning. It's also useful for anyone who wants a clean, timestamped record of which posted levels have already been tested during the session. It is not for swing traders, non-futures traders, or anyone hoping an indicator will hand them entries.

## FAQ

**Does it work on markets other than ES and MES?**
The documentation frames it around ES and MES. Nothing in the source material describes behavior on other instruments, so treat that as the intended use case.

**What happens if I leave the paste box empty?**
The indicator falls back to its individual level inputs.

**How many targets per side?**
Up to five for buyers and up to five for sellers.

**Do hit levels disappear?**
No. They remain as faded dashed lines.

**Why aren't my price scale tags showing?**
Right-click the price scale, open Labels, and turn on "Indicators and financials value."

## Verdict

Walnut Lens Targets by AC Trading is a focused, well-considered companion tool. It doesn't pretend to be a signal engine, and it doesn't pad itself with features outside its remit. The live hit tracking, timestamping, and session reset are exactly the details that separate a useful overlay from a decorative one.

The ceiling is structural: it's only as good as the map you feed it, and it only serves traders already inside that ecosystem. Within that scope, though, it's a clean piece of work that saves real time every morning.
