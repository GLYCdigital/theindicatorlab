# Bollinger Bands Review

Let's cut the fluff. Bollinger Bands isn't some secret weapon. It's a volatility-based tool that's been around since the 1980s, and John Bollinger himself would probably roll his eyes at half the YouTube strategies claiming to "trade the bands." Here's what the indicator actually is and how traders use it.

## What This Indicator Actually Does

Bollinger Bands plots three lines on your chart: a moving average in the middle, and two outer bands set at a standard deviation above and below that average. The bands expand when volatility increases and contract when it drops. That's it. No magic. No hidden signals.

The core idea: price tends to stay within the bands most of the time when using 2 standard deviations. When it breaks out, something statistically notable is happening.

## Settings and How to Tune Them

The default (20, 2) is the standard starting point. Traders adjust it depending on the asset and timeframe:

- **For crypto (BTC, ETH):** Widen the deviation to filter out whipsaw noise.
- **For forex (EURUSD, GBPJPY):** The default deviation is commonly used, as forex tends to respect the bands.
- **For stocks (SPY, AAPL):** Some traders tighten the deviation during earnings season, when bands contract pre-release and then expand.

Lengthening the moving average (for example to 50) smooths the bands but produces fewer signals. Shortening it makes the bands more reactive but noisier.

## How It's Used for Entries and Exits

Forget "buy the bottom band, sell the top band." That's a one-way ticket to getting stopped out. Here's the common playbook:

**Entry (Trend Continuation):**
- Wait for the bands to squeeze (a low volatility period). This is the "Bollinger Band Squeeze."
- When price breaks above the upper band with a strong candle close *and* the bands start expanding again, that's a long setup.
- A common stop placement is 1 ATR below the lower band.

**Exit (Mean Reversion):**
- If price is hugging the upper band for several candles, traders look for a bearish divergence on RSI or MACD as a signal to take profit or short.
- Same logic in reverse for the lower band.

**False Breakout Filter:**
- If price touches the upper band but the next candle closes inside the bands again, the market is rejecting that level. Only trade the break if the close stays *outside* the band.

## Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Works across timeframes and markets.
- The squeeze is a widely used volatility predictor.
- The bands are self-adjusting—no need to redraw support/resistance constantly.

**Cons:**
- Poor in ranging markets. Bands become less useful when price just meanders inside them.
- Mean reversion trades on intraday charts (1m, 5m) are noisy and prone to fakeouts.
- It's lagging by nature. The bands react to past price, not future moves.

## Who This Indicator Is Actually For

- **Swing traders:** Daily charts with the default settings are a common fit.
- **Volatility traders:** The squeeze setup is built for this style.
- **Beginners:** It's simple to understand and hard to misuse if you follow rules.

**Not for:** Scalpers on 1-minute charts. You'll get chopped to pieces.

## Better Alternatives

- **Keltner Channels:** Similar but uses ATR instead of standard deviation. Better for gap-prone markets like crypto.
- **Donchian Channels:** Plots the highest high and lowest low over a period. Better for breakout traders who want to see actual price extremes.

Bollinger Bands remains the reference point for volatility-based analysis because it's mathematically sound and universally recognized.

## FAQ

**Q: Should I use 1 or 2 standard deviations?**
A: 2 is the standard for a reason. 1 gives you more false breaks. Only use 1 if you're trading ultra-tight ranges with a very short stop.

**Q: Do Bollinger Bands work on crypto?**
A: Yes, but many traders widen the deviation. Crypto loves to spike hard. The extra buffer reduces fakeouts.

**Q: What timeframes are best?**
A: Daily for swing trades. 1-hour for intraday. Avoid anything under 15 minutes unless you're scalping with a very tight stop.

**Q: Can I use Bollinger Bands alone?**
A: No. Pair it with volume or RSI. Alone, it's just a volatility band. You need confirmation.

## Final Verdict

Bollinger Bands is a strong tool because it's simple, effective, and adapts to market conditions automatically. It won't make you money by itself—no indicator does—but it's the closest thing to a "universal" volatility gauge that exists. If you don't have it on your chart, you're trading blind to volatility.

**Rating: 5/5** – Indispensable for any serious trader.
