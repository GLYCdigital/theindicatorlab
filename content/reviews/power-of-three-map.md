The name borrows from a familiar idea in price-action circles — the "Power of Three," usually shorthand for accumulation, manipulation and distribution phases. This script, Power of Three Map [JOAT], is a chart study that maps that concept onto TradingView as a trend tool. That's the honest scope of what the source material tells us: it's a study, it lives in the Trend category, and it's built to visualize the Power of Three framework rather than to fire off buy/sell signals.

## What it actually does

As a trend study, the job here is structure, not prediction. A "map" implies overlaying the Power of Three phases onto price so you can see where the market sits in that cycle. In practice, that means you're looking at the chart for context — which phase appears to be active — and letting that frame your read of the trend rather than leaning on a single arrow to enter a trade.

That's a meaningful distinction. This isn't a signal-cranking oscillator. It's closer to a lens: it helps you organize a discretionary read of accumulation, manipulation and distribution behaviour.

## How you'd use it

The sensible workflow is to treat the map as a context layer and keep your execution separate. Read the phase the study highlights, then confirm with your own price structure, volume or momentum before acting. Because the source doesn't document which timeframes or instruments it's tuned for, start on the chart you actually trade and judge whether the mapping lines up with the moves you know happened. If it doesn't, adjust inputs yourself — don't assume defaults are optimised for your market.

As shown in the chart above, the value is in the visual overlay sitting cleanly against your candles, not in a readout that tells you what to do.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**
- Maps a well-known price-action concept directly onto the chart, which beats holding the framework in your head.
- Sits in the trend category, so it's oriented toward context rather than noise.

**Cons**
- The documentation is thin. No published inputs, defaults or guidance means you're largely on your own to interpret and configure it.

## Who it's for

Discretionary price-action traders who already think in terms of accumulation, manipulation and distribution and want a visual aid for that. It's less useful for someone hunting a mechanical signal, and probably wasted on pure-indicator systematic traders who want documented, reproducible rules.
