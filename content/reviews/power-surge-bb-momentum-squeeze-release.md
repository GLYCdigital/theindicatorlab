## The idea behind a squeeze release

Bollinger Bands widen and narrow with volatility. When price coils and the bands contract, the market is compressing energy. Eventually it expands again, and that expansion — the release — is where trend traders want to be positioned. Squeeze-release indicators exist to make that transition visible instead of something you notice three candles too late.

The "momentum" and "power surge" language in the title suggests this script isn't content to just draw the squeeze. It's trying to distinguish a genuine momentum breakout from a fakeout — the release that immediately collapses back into the range. That's the hard part of the whole approach, and it's where any squeeze tool either earns its place or doesn't.

As shown in the chart above, the kind of setup these tools highlight is a tight, low-volatility band structure followed by a decisive expansion candle or series of candles. Whether this specific script labels that moment with an alert, a plot, or a colored bar is something you'll see on the chart itself.

## How you'd actually use it

The natural workflow for anything in this category is straightforward:

- Let the squeeze build. Don't force trades while the bands are pinched and price is going nowhere.
- Wait for the release signal. This is the trigger the indicator is designed to surface.
- Confirm direction with your own read of structure, a moving average, or price action. A squeeze tells you *that* something is coming, not always *which way* cleanly.
- Manage risk off the compression range. The tight range gives you a logical invalidation point, which is one of the genuine advantages of trading squeezes.

That last point matters more than the signal itself. Squeeze setups are attractive because the risk is definable — you're entering near a contracted range, so a failed breakout is easy to identify.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Attacks a real, repeatable market phenomenon. Volatility does cycle between contraction and expansion, and building a tool around that is sensible.
- Trend-category placement fits. Squeeze releases are most useful when they resolve into directional moves.
- Potentially good for alert-driven trading. If the script fires on release, you can watch many instruments without staring at charts.

**Cons**

- Squeeze tools are notorious for false starts. Compression can release into chop as easily as into trend.
- The name promises a lot — "power surge" implies conviction, and no indicator can guarantee that.
- Without published documentation, you're dependent on reading the script or the author's notes to understand exactly what triggers the signal.

## Who it's for

This is a tool for breakout and momentum traders who already understand volatility cycles. If you trade ranges, you'll find it annoying — it's constantly pointing at the edge of the range. If you scalp reversals, it's the wrong instrument entirely. But if your edge comes from catching expansion after consolidation, a squeeze-release indicator is a reasonable addition to the toolkit, provided you treat its signal as a heads-up rather than an entry order.
