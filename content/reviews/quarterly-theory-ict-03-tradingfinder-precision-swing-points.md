Quarterly Theory is one of those ICT-adjacent frameworks that gets talked about a lot and documented a little. This indicator, built by TradingFinder, packages the swing-point side of that idea into a chart tool. The name tells you the ambition: precision swing points, derived from a quarterly-theory reading of price rather than a generic pivot formula. That's the pitch. Whether the execution earns the word "precision" is the question every trader installing it has to answer for themselves.

I'll be straight with you up front: there is no official write-up or documentation I can point to for this one. So this review sticks to what the tool is conceptually and how you'd actually work with it — not invented defaults, not made-up win rates, not a backtest I didn't run.

## What it's actually doing

At its core, this is a swing-point identifier with an ICT flavor. Standard swing tools (fractals, zig-zag, pivot highs/lows) mark turning points based on a fixed bar count on either side. Quarterly Theory approaches the same problem from a time-structure angle — the idea that price behaves differently depending on where you are within a larger cycle, and that meaningful highs and lows cluster around those structural boundaries rather than arbitrary bar windows.

The indicator's job is to surface those swing points on your chart so you can read trend and structure without squinting at every candle. That's the concept. It's a mapping tool, not a signal generator — and that distinction matters for how you should use it.

## Where it fits in a workflow

Trend traders will get the most out of this as a structural overlay, not a standalone system. The natural use is:

- **Marking structure.** Plot the swing points, then read higher highs and lower lows against them. If the indicator's points line up with the structure you already see, it's doing its job.
- **Confirming bias.** Use the swing sequence to validate or challenge a directional read you formed elsewhere — a moving average, a market-structure break, whatever your primary lens is.
- **Framing entries.** Swing points make natural reference levels for stops and targets. The indicator gives you the levels; you bring the trade logic.

What it won't do is tell you when to buy. Anyone expecting an arrow-and-alert machine will be disappointed, and that's fine — it isn't pretending to be one.

## Pros

- **Concept-driven, not cosmetic.** Quarterly Theory is a specific framework, and mapping swing points through that lens is more interesting than another repackaged fractal indicator.
- **Structure-first design.** It respects the idea that swing points are about time and context, which is closer to how discretionary ICT traders actually think than fixed-bar pivots are.
- **Complements a trend stack.** It slots in cleanly alongside your existing tools without fighting them for chart space or contradicting your bias.

## Cons

- **Documentation is thin.** This is the big one. Without a clear official description, you're reverse-engineering the logic from what appears on the chart. That's a real barrier for anyone who wants to understand *why* a point is marked, not just *that* it is.
- **Framework-dependent.** If you don't already buy into Quarterly Theory or ICT time-structure concepts, the output may feel arbitrary. The tool assumes a certain vocabulary.
- **Not a signal engine.** No entries, no targets, no alerts doing the thinking for you. That's a feature for some, a frustration for others.

## Who it's for

Discretionary trend and structure traders who already work in the ICT idiom — people who read price through time cycles and want their swing points marked consistently. It's also useful for anyone studying Quarterly Theory who wants a visual aid while learning the framework. It is *not* for set-and-forget system traders, beginners looking for arrows, or anyone who needs a fully documented, plug-and-play tool.

## FAQ

**Does it repaint?** I can't confirm that without documentation, and I won't guess. Check how the points behave on a live chart before trusting them for entries.

**Can I use it on any timeframe?** Swing-point logic is generally timeframe-agnostic — points simply scale with the chart you're on. Test it on your own instruments rather than assuming.

**Is it a buy/sell indicator?** No. It maps structure. The decision is yours.

## Verdict

Four stars, and the missing star is almost entirely about transparency. The concept is solid, the framework is legitimate, and a swing-point overlay built around Quarterly Theory fills a real gap for ICT-style traders. But an indicator that asks you to trust its "precision" without explaining its logic is asking a lot — and that gap between ambition and documentation is what keeps it from the top tier.

If you're already fluent in the framework, install it, study how it marks structure, and judge it against your own read. If you're not, this isn't the tool that will teach you.
