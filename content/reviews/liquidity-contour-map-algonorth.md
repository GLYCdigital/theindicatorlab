## What this indicator actually is

Liquidity Contour Map, published by AlgoNorth, is a TradingView indicator filed under the Trend category. Its premise is straightforward: markets have areas where resting orders cluster, and those areas tend to shape how price behaves as it trends. The indicator's job is to visualise that — to give you a map of liquidity structure rather than a single buy/sell arrow.

That framing matters. Plenty of trend indicators boil everything down to a line that flips colour. A "contour map" approach implies layered information: you're meant to read the terrain, not just a signal. On a MACD-style pane (the chart type used for this review's screenshot), the output is plotted as an oscillator-panel study, which tells you the tool is designed to be read alongside price rather than painted directly on your candles as a support/resistance band.

I'll be upfront about a limitation that shapes this entire review: AlgoNorth's official description and documentation weren't available to me when writing this. So treat what follows as an assessment of the concept and how a tool built on it fits into a workflow — not a spec sheet. I haven't run it, and I won't pretend otherwise.

## The concept, and why it's not just another trend line

Liquidity-based analysis has become genuinely popular because it addresses a real frustration: trend indicators that look great in a clean move and awful in chop. If price is being pulled toward pools of resting orders, then understanding where those pools sit gives context that a moving-average crossover simply doesn't have.

The "contour" language suggests the indicator is trying to show the shape of liquidity rather than a binary level — more like a topographical reading than a single horizontal line. Whether that's rendered as bands, gradients, or a composite oscillator curve, the intent is the same: help you see where the market's structure is dense and where it's thin.

That's a useful lens. It reframes the question from "is the trend up?" to "is the trend supported by the structure beneath it?" Those are different questions, and the second one is harder to answer with off-the-shelf tools.

## How you'd actually use it

Because the study plots in its own pane, the natural workflow is interpretive rather than mechanical. You'd watch how the contour reading evolves as price trends — does it confirm the move, or diverge from it? Does the structure show compression before an expansion, or thinning as a move exhausts?

The sensible way to deploy something like this is as a filter and context layer. You keep your price-action reading and your entry trigger, and you use the contour map to decide whether the environment is favourable. That's a more mature use of a trend tool than treating it as an alert, and it's the use this indicator seems built for.

Pair it with the MACD reading shown above and you get two views of momentum and structure that can be compared against each other. When they agree, conviction rises. When they don't, that disagreement is itself information.

## What's good about it

- **It's conceptually different.** Liquidity structure is a genuinely distinct angle from the usual trend toolkit, and that alone gives it value as a second opinion.
- **Pane-based plotting keeps your chart readable.** Not every study needs to be smeared across your candles.
- **It encourages structural thinking.** The best trend tools make you ask better questions, and this one does.
- **AlgoNorth's naming is honest.** Calling it a "map" rather than a "signal" sets the right expectation.

## Where it falls short

- **Interpretation overhead.** A contour map asks more of you than a crossover. If you want a tool that tells you what to do, this isn't it.
- **Thin public documentation.** Without a thorough write-up of how the contour is constructed, you're partly trusting a black box. That's a fair criticism of the release, not the idea.
- **Trend category, structural output.** If you're expecting a directional trigger, you'll be disappointed. This is context, not entry timing.

## Who it's for

Discretionary traders who already read price action well and want a structural second layer — particularly those who've grown tired of momentum indicators that lag at turning points. Swing traders working off liquidity concepts will get the most out of it. Pure mechanical/systematic traders should probably look elsewhere; there's too much interpretation involved.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
Based on the concept, it's a structural/context tool rather than a signal generator. Don't expect arrows.

**Can I use it on any timeframe?**
I have no documented basis for claiming a best timeframe, so I won't. Test it yourself against your own trading horizon.

**Is it a replacement for support and resistance?**
No. It's a different way of visualising where liquidity sits, not a substitute for your own level work.

## Verdict

Liquidity Contour Map is an interesting, concept-first indicator that treats trend analysis as a structural question rather than a momentum one. The idea is sound, the pane-based presentation is sensible, and it fills a niche that most trend tools ignore. It loses a star for thin public documentation and the interpretive burden it places on the user — you have to bring your own framework.

If you're a discretionary trader who values context over signals, it's worth a look. If you want something that tells you when to click, keep moving.
