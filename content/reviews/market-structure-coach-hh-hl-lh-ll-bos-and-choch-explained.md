Most price action traders learn market structure the hard way: squinting at swings until the pattern clicks. This indicator takes that vocabulary — higher highs, higher lows, lower highs, lower lows, break of structure and change of character — and puts it directly on the chart as labels. It's a teaching tool as much as a signal tool, which is a genuinely useful niche on TradingView.

## What it actually does

Market structure is the skeleton of trend analysis. An uptrend is a sequence of higher highs and higher lows; a downtrend is lower highs and lower lows. The moment that sequence breaks, you have a potential shift in control. This indicator automates the labelling of those swing points and flags the two events that matter most to structure traders:

- **Break of Structure (BOS):** price pushing through a prior swing in the direction of the prevailing trend — continuation.
- **Change of Character (CHOCH):** the first meaningful break against the prevailing sequence — a possible reversal signal.

The name says "Coach," and that framing is fair. The value isn't a magic entry arrow; it's that the chart gets annotated with the same structure concepts taught in most price action curricula, so you can see them forming instead of hunting for them manually.

## Where it fits in a workflow

As shown in the chart above, the labels sit on the price action itself, mapping swings as they develop. That makes it a top-down tool naturally: identify the structural bias on a higher timeframe, then drop down to see whether the lower timeframe is still respecting that structure or has printed a CHOCH.

The honest way to use it is confirmation, not prediction. A BOS label tells you the trend sequence held — it doesn't tell you the next candle's direction. A CHOCH tells you the character may be shifting — it doesn't guarantee a reversal. Treat both as context, and pair them with your own entry triggers, volume or momentum reads. The MACD-style divergence view in the screenshot is a reminder that structure alone is one lens, not the whole picture.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- **Educational value.** If you're still internalising what HH, HL, LH and LL actually look like in live data, having them labelled removes a lot of guesswork.
- **Clean conceptual framework.** BOS and CHOCH are the two structure events worth caring about, and the indicator keeps focus on them rather than drowning the chart in noise.
- **Discretionary-friendly.** It supports the way swing traders actually think, rather than forcing a rigid mechanical system.
- **Good for multi-timeframe work.** Structure labels make it easy to compare bias across timeframes at a glance.

**Cons**

- **Subjectivity doesn't disappear.** Swing identification always depends on how pivots are defined; different traders will disagree about where a swing "really" is. Any structure tool inherits that ambiguity.
- **Labels can lag.** By definition, a swing is confirmed after the fact, and a BOS or CHOCH is only valid once price has actually broken the level. Expect some delay between the event and the label.
- **Not a standalone system.** There are no documented entry, exit or risk rules here — it's an annotation layer, and it will punish anyone who treats labels as buy/sell signals.
- **Documentation gap.** Without a clear official write-up, newer users may need to lean on general market structure knowledge to get the most from it.

## Who it's for

Price action and swing traders who already think in terms of structure and want the labelling done for them. It's also a solid training aid for traders moving away from indicator-heavy systems toward raw price reading. If you want a fully mechanical strategy with fixed rules, this isn't it — and it isn't pretending to be.

## FAQ

**Does it give buy and sell signals?**
No. It labels structure and flags BOS and CHOCH events. Turning those into trades is your job.

**What's the difference between BOS and CHOCH?**
BOS is a break in the direction of the existing trend — continuation. CHOCH is a break against it — a potential character shift. The indicator's naming reflects that distinction.

**Can I use it on any market or timeframe?**
Structure concepts apply across markets and timeframes, but I can't confirm any recommended settings or "best" timeframe from the source material, so test it against your own approach.
